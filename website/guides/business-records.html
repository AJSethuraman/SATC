Guide

Good records for a business

A business return starts from books that match the bank statements.

01One account for the business, one for you

The IRS's own first instruction to a new business is to open a separate checking account and put every business receipt through it.

When one account does both jobs, someone has to read it line by line and decide which side each entry belongs on. That work happens before the return can start.

A credit card used only for the business does the same job.

02What matching the bank means

  • Each month's closing balance in the books equals the closing balance on the statement.
  • The same holds at year end for every account: checking, savings, credit cards, loans.
  • A number in the books with no statement behind it is a guess, and it has to be chased down before it can go on a return.

03The year-end set

  • A profit and loss, and a balance sheet, as of the last day of the year.
  • Year-end statements for every account, and for each loan its balance and the interest paid.
  • The year's payroll summary, and copies of the W-2s that went out.
  • Anything bought that lasts more than a year: what it was, the date, the cost. Vehicles, equipment, building work.
  • A count of what is on the shelves at year end, if you carry stock.
  • What the owners put in or took out, and any loan taken or repaid.

04Miles, home office, meals

  • Miles. Written and kept as you drive: date, distance, and the reason for the trip.
  • The car method is a one-time choice. Standard mileage has to be picked in the first year the car is used for the business. A leased car stays on that method for the whole lease, renewals included.
  • Home office. The square footage of the room and of the house, plus the year's utility, insurance and repair bills.
  • Meals. A receipt does not carry who was there or why. Write it on the receipt at the time.

05People you paid

  • Ask for a W-9 before the first payment, not in January.
  • $600 or more to someone who is not an employee usually means a 1099-NEC. It is due both to them and to the IRS by January 31.
  • Schedule C asks on the form itself whether you made payments that required a 1099. It is a question the return has to answer either way.

06If it is a partnership or a corporation

  • The return is due March 15 for a calendar year, and the owners' K-1s are due on that date too.
  • So the company's books decide when its owners can file, not just when the company can.
  • Past a certain size the return carries a balance sheet, which means year-end balances have to be right rather than close.
  • If you own more than 2% of an S corporation and it pays for your health insurance, that amount belongs in your W-2 wages. Fixing it after the W-2 has gone out means reissuing forms.

07Letters from a tax office

Send the whole letter, both sides, with the envelope. Some of them run on a 30-day clock from the date printed at the top.

08What a business keeps for longer

How long to keep things is on the other page. One rule sits on top of it here: payroll records, four years from when the tax was due or paid.

Behind on the books? That is a different job from the return, and it comes first.
