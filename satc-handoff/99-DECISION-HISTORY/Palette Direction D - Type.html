The cleanest, least traditional

Dropping the serif entirely removes the heritage signal — this reads as a modern practice, not an old one. Cheapest to maintain (two families) and the tightest of the six, but it gives up the oxblood-and-cream's period feel.

Archivo · IBM Plex Mono
Verdict

Take 03 (IBM Plex) if you want the safest strong answer — one superfamily means your statement, your app and your figures are provably one system, and Plex's numerals are genuinely built for tabular data. It's the least likely of the six to ever look dated or generated.

Take 02 (Caslon & Source) if the firm's positioning is heritage — it's the most convincingly chartered, and using Caslon for the wordmark only is exactly the restraint that stops it tipping into pastiche.

Rules regardless of pick

· Figures always mono or tabular. Non-aligning numerals on an accounting document undo everything else.

· Serif for the wordmark and body only. Buttons, labels and UI stay sans — mixing them is what made the original feel loose.

· Three families maximum, and never Cormorant, Playfair or Inter.
