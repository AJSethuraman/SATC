What changed

Closest to what you have — the navy barely moves. The fix is a fourth colour that shouldn't be there: oxblood, carrying the action and the labels, while gold retreats to a hairline. One unexpected pigment is the cheapest way to make a palette feel chosen rather than generated.

keep navy · add oxblood #6E2A2A · gold to hairline only · Spectral
Portable rules

1 · Never fill with gold. Metallics read as cheap simulation the moment they cover area. Hairlines, rules, 1px borders, small-caps labels — nothing larger.

2 · Name your blue after a pigment. Prussian, ultramarine, indigo, payne's grey. "Navy" is a category; a pigment is a decision, and decisions look human.

3 · Break 60/30/10. Go 90/9/1. Let one colour overwhelm and make the accent genuinely scarce — scarcity is what makes it feel expensive.

4 · Add one colour that doesn't belong. Oxblood, sage, dusty rose at 5%. The off-note is the signature.

5 · Retire Cormorant. It's half the tell on its own. Newsreader, Libre Caslon, Spectral, Source Serif — or drop the serif to the wordmark only.

6 · Warm or cool, not both-and-safe. Mid-cream is the fence. Commit to manila-warm or crisp-cool paper.

My pick for a chartered firm

A (Prussian & Ochre) — the pigment has a real reason to exist on an accounting document, and it's the furthest from the default while still unmistakably blue and gold. D if you want to keep the existing navy and change the least.
