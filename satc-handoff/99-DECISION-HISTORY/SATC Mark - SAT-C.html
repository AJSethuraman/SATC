SAT-C LLP · mark, round two

Making the notch into a C.

If people should say "sat-see," the lockup has to lead with SAT‑C rather than SETHURAMAN. The question is whether the mark itself carries the letter.

Three abstract-first options, three letterform options. Judge them small — the 16px tile is the honest test, not the 96px version.

The good news

The notch already wants to be a C. Move the bite from the corner to the middle of the right-hand edge and the remaining outline is a blocky C — same single subtraction, same concept, no new idea bolted on. That's what family A is built from, and it means you don't have to choose between the razor story and the letter.

FAMILY A

Abstract first

Reads as a clean geometric mark. The C is there, but it whispers — most people will see a square with a slot until you point it out. Survives 16px and embossing.

A1 · The Slot

The bite moves to the edge

Exactly the Notch you approved, with the 8×6 bite taken from the middle of the right edge instead of the corner. The residual outline is a blocky C; the removed piece slides out sideways.

Cleanest evolution of what you already have. The C is genuinely legible once seen, and the razor story is unchanged.

A2 · The Deep Slot

Same idea, deeper bite

The slot cuts further in, so the two arms of the C get shorter and the letter asserts itself more. The removed piece is correspondingly wider.

More obviously a C than A1 while staying geometric. Watch: the deeper the bite, the more the mark reads as a bracket rather than a letter.

A3 · Control

Change the words, not the mark

The Notch exactly as specced, with SAT‑C simply promoted to the wordmark. No letter in the symbol at all — the name does that work.

Worth keeping honest about: this may be enough. The mark doesn't have to spell anything for people to learn to say "sat-see."
FAMILY B

Unmistakably a C

The letter comes first. More immediately legible as SAT‑C, at the cost of some of the geometric calm — and, in one case, of working at favicon size at all.

B1 · The Open Square

A squared C, piece set aside

Three strokes of a square, left open on the right. Unambiguously a C, still entirely orthogonal, and the removed piece keeps its place at the corner.

Reads as a letter instantly and holds at 16px. Watch: an open square is also a common bracket / crop-mark motif, so it's less ownable than A1.

B2 · Piece in the Mouth

The removed square, held

The C with its removed piece floating in the opening rather than beside it — the subtraction and the letter occupy the same space.

Real risk: a C with something in its mouth reads as a G to a lot of people, and at 16px the piece merges with the arms. I'd rule this out.

B3 · Logotype

The hyphen becomes the notch

No symbol at all — SAT‑C set tight, with the hyphen replaced by the removed square. The name and the mark are the same object, which is the most direct answer to "I want people to associate SAT‑C."

The cost: no favicon, no app icon, nothing to emboss. You'd need a separate symbol anyway — so this is really a wordmark to pair with A1, not a replacement for it.
Verdict

A1, paired with B3's wordmark.

A1 is the answer I'd argue for. It's the same single subtraction you already approved — nothing added, nothing decorative — and simply moving the bite to the edge turns the residue into a C. The razor story survives intact: we take out what isn't necessary, and what's left is the C. That's a better sentence than either idea alone.

Then take B3's device for the wordmark — the hyphen in SAT‑C set as the small oxblood square. It ties the letters to the symbol without needing the symbol present, which is what you want on a letterhead where both appear.

A3 deserves a real hearing. The mark doesn't have to spell anything. Promote SAT‑C to the lockup, keep the Notch as-is, and people will learn to say "sat-see" from the wordmark alone — that's how nearly every initialism works. It's the cheapest option and it isn't wrong.

I'd rule out B2 outright: it reads as a G.

If you pick A1 or A2, note what changes:

· The mark spec, the CSS .s-o path, the favicon data-URI, and make-images.py all take a new path string — about ten minutes of work, already isolated to those four places.

· Every lockup flips from SETHURAMAN to SAT‑C, with the full legal name moving to the small print. That's a one-line change per template.

· The 8×6 bite is no longer square, so "the piece fits the hole" still holds — but the misuse rule becomes "don't change the bite's proportions," not "keep it 8×8."

Say the word and I'll re-cut the whole system to whichever you pick.
