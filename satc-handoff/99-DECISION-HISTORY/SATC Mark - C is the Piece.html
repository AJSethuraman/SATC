SAT-C LLP · mark, round four

Don't put a C on the piece. Make the C be the piece.

You were right that a letter stamped on a floating square looks like a sticker. But the piece and the letter were always the same thing — so the C stops being decoration and becomes the shape cut to fit the gap.

01

Two states

Same notched square. The oxblood block becomes a C set in Plex Sans Bold — outside the gap, then inside it.

State 1 · Open

The gap, and the C outside it

The C sits where the block used to — apart, at the corner, unmistakably the thing that belongs in the hole. This is the working mark.

Use for Nav, letterhead, card, report cover — everywhere at 64px and above.
State 2 · Filled

The C seated in the notch

The letter drops into the void it was cut from and sits clear of the seam. The square isn't solid — a C can't fill a square hole — but the C is now inside the business instead of beside it, which is the part that means something.

Use for Engagement confirmed, statement paid, filing complete, the closing slide.
One correction worth stating plainly: the seated C is set to font-size 7.8, not the notch's full 8 units, so its ink clears the 2.4-wide stroke band on the notch edges. Sized to the void it gets sliced by the outline. If you ever want the filled state to read as a genuinely solid square — for a paid stamp, say — use the plain block from the earlier spec rather than the C.
02

The square becomes the hyphen

Here's the honest constraint: inside the artwork the square is 16 units and the C is 8, so a C scaled to match cap-height letters forces the square to twice their height. One SVG can't do both. So the wordmark is real text — SAT and C at the same size — with the notched square standing in for the hyphen, its gap facing the C. The C is oxblood, so it still reads as the piece.

What this buys you. It reads as SAT‑C at a glance, because the square sits exactly where a hyphen would and the C is a real letter at full size. On a second look, the square has a corner missing and the only oxblood thing on the page is the C — so the gap and the piece find each other without anyone explaining it. The mark and the wordmark are now the same object, which is what you were after.
03

Where the C survives

One boundary, and here's the basis for it: the C is set at font-size 11 in a 29.4-unit viewBox, so its cap ink measures roughly 0.7 × 11 × (px ÷ 29.4) — 16.8px at 64px overall, and shrinking fast below that. 16px of cap ink is about where a bold letterform stops being a letterform on screen. So: C at 64px and above, plain block below 64px.

One mark, two resolutions. At 64px and above the piece is a C; below 64px it's the plain block. Nobody sees both at once and the silhouette is identical, so it reads as one mark rather than two logos. Favicon, app icon, and the email signature all use the block.

What it means, in a meeting

"The square is your business, and the corner that's missing is the part nobody's handling. The C is us — cut to the exact shape of the gap."

And if they ask why a C: because it's the C in SAT‑C. The mark is the last letter of our name. Short enough to say once and never again.
