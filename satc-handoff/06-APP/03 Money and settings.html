Money, re-pricing, and the two settings screens

Four things can be true of a bill and three of them looked the same. Then the re-quote, which is a good screen with the wrong thing at the top. Then the two settings screens, which are the only screens in the software that change what a future client reads — and should say so.

Job 05

Four things that can be true of a bill

The interesting one is part paid — money arrived and did not cover it. That is not a status, it is a shortfall, and a shortfall has a number. So the amount column carries a fill: how much of the bill has arrived. No link is a fact about the bill, not a failure, so it is grey and quiet. Paid is done and gets the smallest treatment on the page, because a paid bill is not work.

The long paragraph explaining that the page shows what was last written down becomes a timestamp on the summary line. Same promise, one line, and it is the reassurance the paragraph was reaching for.

Why part paid is “your call” and not a refusal: nothing is broken — money came in and the arithmetic does not close. Only the firm decides whether that is a short payment to chase, a fee that was agreed down, or somebody paying in two halves. So it takes the third colour, and it is the reason the day page has a row for Marcus Ellwood.

Job 03, continued

Re-pricing: the reason box is the screen

Seventeen answers with what is on file beside them, and only the changed ones matter. So the changed ones are the only thing at full strength and everything else is available underneath — and the summary at the top counts them, because “you have changed two of seventeen answers” is the sentence that stops a mistake.

Then the difference. What was at the top of that screen was the total; what belongs at the top is the reason, because in February somebody asks why this client was charged more than the letter says and this sentence is the whole answer. The total stays — it just is not the thing you read first.

The page I cut: “The new quote is recorded”. Recording lands you back on the client’s file with the new figure on it and the line about whether the letter still reads correctly — which is the only sentence that page carried.

Job 06

And both are two columns, because twenty-four prices down a single column is a scroll for no reason.

The wording of a letter, and one section open

The section editor is right as it is; what it needs is for the merge fields to stop looking like ordinary text. They are the one thing on the screen you can break by deleting, so they are set in the third colour — the software is waiting on the client’s own details there. Everything else on the page is the firm’s own sentences and looks like sentences.

Adding a section is this same screen with an empty one open, not a page of its own.

The parts in double angle brackets are filled in per client

You can move the words around them; leave them in place. Deleting one takes the date or the fee off the letter. Two are in this section: <<FirstDeliverableTarget>> and <<MaterialsDeadline>>.

**like this** makes a phrase bold. It is the only mark this box understands.

Takes effect on the next letter written, not on any already sent.

One section, open — the merge fields are the only thing on the screen a person can break, so the only thing in colour

What I would have the software do here, and it is one line of Python: count the merge fields in the section as saved and refuse the save if a field that was there has gone, the same way the gate refuses a pack. That turns “leave it in place” from an instruction into a rule — and it is exactly the kind of thing a test can prove.

Last

What ships, in order

  • The stylesheet, and the chrome. One file, one line on the body, and the standing today line. Every screen improves before you have redrawn any of them, because the marks and the tallies are already in it.
  • The gate. The most important control in the software, and the one where the current design costs you the most: it buries the two things you have to act on under eleven rows of things you do not.
  • The day. Needs a real answer from the data — the three groups, and the rule that a row may only sit in Waiting on you if the software can name what it is waiting for.
  • Money and the shelf. Both are mostly the marks applied honestly, plus the shortfall bar.
  • The four pages I would delete, whenever it is convenient. Nothing depends on them.

Where a sentence on any of these screens is the firm’s to write and not mine, I have left [CONFIRM: …] in it rather than invent one — there are two, both on the engagement letter, and they are the reason the third colour exists.
