Six things had no visual language. Here they are.

Everything on the four pages of this set is real markup against one stylesheet, satc-app.css. Paste that file into the block at the bottom of the web app and put class="app" on the body; every screen after that is markup you can lift line for line. No webfonts — the system stack is the design, so the app looks the same on a laptop with no internet as it does here. No script except the two small pieces marked as such.

One person operates this under time pressure. So the rule I designed to is: the screen answers the question you opened it with, in the first inch. Everything that is reference rather than answer moves down or folds away.

Pattern 01

Stage: a count, a name, and how far along

An engagement has nine stages and no screen showed any of them. It reads as a count with the stage named beside it — the count says how far, the name says where. Nine ticks, filled to the stage. It appears on every row of every list and at the top of the client’s own page, and it is the same object in both places, one size smaller in a row.

4 of 9sent

3 of 9pack built, blocked

2 of 9priced, waiting on you

9 of 9closed

The nine names, as a client’s file moves: interviewed · priced · pack built · sent · signed · filed · billed · paid · closed. Those are the words on screen. The bar has nine ticks whether or not you can name them all from memory, which is the point of the count.

Pattern 02

Every number says what it counted

The codebase already refuses to let a check that examined nothing look like a check that passed. On screen that rule was bare text. It is now one object, and a bare number cannot be written with it: the count and what was counted are the same element.

1,249 words in 4 letters read

5 of 13 answers checked

nothing to look at — no clause was cited

Tally — the third one is the case worth designing: examined nothing, so it says so in words and never shows a zero

<p class="tally"><b>1,249</b> words in 4 letters read</p>
<p class="tally empty">nothing to look at &mdash; no clause was cited</p>

Wording, not styling: “11 check(s)” has to go. A machine wrote that bracket and a person reads it. Say “11 checks”, and “1 check” when there is one — the plural is one line in Python and it is the difference between software that was written for you and software that was generated.

Pattern 03

Five things a line can be, and they do not look alike

Blocked, not-yet, waiting-on-you, fine, and nothing-to-look-at were three shades of the same grey. Five marks, one vocabulary, used on every screen and nowhere reinvented:

finestops ityour callnothing to look atnot builtdone

Marks — filled means it needs a person: oxblood for a refusal, the third colour for a decision. Outlines are facts.

<span class="mk pass">fine</span>                a check that looked and was satisfied
<span class="mk stop">stops it</span>             blocked: a fact is wrong or missing
<span class="mk wait">your call</span>            the software is waiting on the firm
<span class="mk none">nothing to look at</span>   examined nothing; not a pass
<span class="mk notyet">not built</span>          nobody has made this yet
<span class="mk done">done</span>                 finished and filed away

Blocked against not-yet is carried by the border, not the chip, so it survives being seen from three feet away: a blocked thing has a solid oxblood edge, a thing nobody has built has a dashed grey edge and no fill. Both appear on the document shelf on page three of this set.

Pattern 04

The third colour, and it means one thing

[CONFIRM: …] is not an error and must stop looking like one. It gets a colour of its own — burnt orange #A8571C — used for nothing else in the software, ever. Navy is the firm acting. Oxblood is a refusal. Orange is the software declining to invent something and waiting on you.

  • Navy#132437 — the firm acting
  • Oxblood#6A2833 — a refusal
  • Waiting on you#A8571C — new, one meaning
  • Ink#242C36 — body
  • Ink 2#4A5360 — secondary
  • Paper#FCFCFA

It also carries the focus ring, which is the one honest overlap: a focus ring means the software is waiting on you here. On a keyboard-first tool in February that is worth the brightest colour in the set.

Pattern 05

The app knows what month it is

A standing line in the navy bar on every screen: today, the next date on the calendar, and how many of your clients are due before it. It is the smallest type in the software and the only thing that never changes position, which is what makes it readable rather than loud. Inside thirty days the deadline gets a struck underline in the third colour — the only time the chrome raises its voice.

SATC

Wednesday 2 Septembernext: 15 September — partnerships and S corporations on extension4 clients due before it

The chrome — the mark still goes home; the line beside it is new

<header class="bar">
  <a class="mark" href="/">SAT<span class="hy"></span>C</a>
  <p class="today"><b>Wednesday 2 September</b><span class="sep"></span>
    <span class="soon">next: <b>15 September</b> &mdash; partnerships and S corporations on extension</span>
    <span class="sep"></span><a href="/due">4 clients due before it</a></p>
</header>

Drop class="soon" when the next date is more than thirty days out and the line goes quiet on its own. The count links to the list of those four, which is the same list the day page opens with.

Two things the linter would fail if it looked

Found while reading the screens

  • The client’s record prints ClientFullName and TaxYear under every label. You asked to keep them, quieter — they are now 10.5px in the palest grey the page allows and sit under the label rather than beside it, so the eye reads twenty-six labels and no codes unless it goes looking. They are on page three of this set.
  • The gate names a failing document as SAT-C Engagement Letter - Reyes - 2026.html. That is a filename on a screen, and the file it names is the one thing on that page you cannot do anything about. Cuts

What I would cut, and what I drew anyway

Twenty-five screens, and about seventeen of them are screens a person needs. You asked me to list the cuts and draw them anyway, so all of them are in this set — but here is what I would delete, and every one of them is a page that exists because a step needed somewhere to land.

“The engagement exists”cut the page

A page whose whole content is three buttons, one of which you always press. Create the engagement and land on the client’s file with a line at the top saying it was just made. Same information, one fewer press, and you arrive somewhere you can work.

“The pack, and every check that passed”cut the page

Marked “nothing to press. Read it and go on.” in your own walkthrough — which is the definition of a page that should be a line on the page you came from. The document shelf can say “built this morning, eleven checks, all fine” and that is the whole of it.

“The new quote, recorded”cut the page

Same shape. Record the quote and return to the file, with the new figure on it and the note that the letter still reads correctly.

“Adding a section that is not there”fold in

Not a screen. It is the section editor with an empty section open. Two pages of markup that differ by whether one field has a value.

Lead detail, and the phone leadfold in

Three walkthrough steps are one screen with a panel open, which the software already does with a disclosure. One screen, three states.

The review before creatingkeep, redraw

Thirty-seven Change buttons is thirty-seven of the same word down a page, and the eye cannot find the one answer that is wrong. The screen stays — it is the last look before anything exists and it earns its place — but the buttons go: the row itself is the control, and the word appears on the row you are pointing at.

“A hard no”keep

Keep it, unchanged in substance. It is a refusal with a way back and it is already the right shape. It gets the stage bar in oxblood and stops there.

The interviewkeep, barely touched

You said it was closest to right and it is. What it gains is a denominator on the progress bar and the running summary as a fixed rail rather than a list that grows down the page. Nothing else.
