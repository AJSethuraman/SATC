SAT-C LLP · palette stress test

Does it survive a photocopier?

Clients photocopy, scan, and fax statements. If meaning is carried by colour alone, it disappears on the second-generation copy. This tests the navy / oxblood / gold system at greyscale, at photocopy contrast, and at fax threshold.

1.47:1 Navy and oxblood are nearly identical in greyscale. That's the finding, and it's a real one — but the system survives, because everywhere it uses both, shape already carries the meaning. Rules below.

Why gold is fine and oxblood isn't. Gold sits far up the luminance range, so it separates cleanly from navy even in mono — which is exactly why it works as a hairline. Oxblood is a dark colour like navy; it reads as different only because of hue, and hue is the first thing a photocopier throws away.

What actually breaks: the gold hairline under the total. It's the lightest ink in the system, so at fax threshold it either vanishes or blows out to black. On print documents, make that rule navy at 0.5pt instead of gold — the specimen below the letterhead does exactly that. Everything else holds: the button survives because it's a filled block, and the mark survives because outline-versus-solid is a shape difference, not a colour one.
03

Rules this produces

The palette stands. These are the constraints that keep it standing on paper.

Safe — shape carries it

Keep doing these

  • Oxblood as a filled block. A solid button reads as a button in any generation of copy — the fill is the signal, not the hue.
  • The mark's outline vs. solid piece. Survives greyscale, threshold, and embossing, because the difference is geometric.
  • Gold on screen only, as hairlines. 6.5:1 against navy in mono — clean separation where it survives at all.
  • Navy for every rule and heading on print. One dark ink, doing the structural work.

Banned — colour alone

Never do these

  • Oxblood text on navy, or navy on oxblood. 1.47:1 — fails in colour before it even reaches the copier.
  • Status coded by navy vs. oxblood. Paid vs. overdue must differ by word, weight, or position — never hue alone.
  • Gold hairlines on anything printed. Use navy at 0.5pt. Gold is a screen material here.
  • Oxblood on a figure to mean "negative." Use parentheses — the accounting convention, and it photocopies.
Verify it yourself in ten seconds: print any document to PDF, then open it and print again with "greyscale" or "black and white" ticked. If you can still tell what to pay and by when, it passed. The filters above are approximations — a real toner path is always harsher.
