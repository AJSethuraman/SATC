Thank you for choosing <<FirmName>> to prepare the returns for <<ClientFullName>>, <<EntityType>>. This letter outlines what each of us is responsible for, and what the work costs. Please read it thoroughly.

01What we will prepare

  • Federal: <<FederalReturns>>
  • State: <<StateReturns>>
  • Local: <<LocalReturns>>
  • Also included: <<AdditionalForms>>

Returns and forms not outlined above are outside of this engagement. If the return requires additional work, a new estimate will be provided in writing. Additional work will not begin without your written consent to the new estimate.

This engagement covers the corporation, not its shareholders. A C corporation is taxed on its own income, so nothing on this return flows through to a shareholder’s personal return.

02What this engagement is not

We prepare returns from the information you give us. We will not audit or otherwise verify it. We may ask you to clarify an item, but our work is not designed to detect errors, fraud, theft, or other illegal acts. If we find something material, we will tell you.

We will perform bookkeeping only as far as preparing the returns requires. We do not perform audits, reviews, or any assurance engagement, and we issue no opinion on your financial statements.

Payroll processing, sales tax filings, and annual report or registered-agent filings are not included unless they are listed in section 01. Representing the entity in a notice response or an appeal is a separately quoted engagement.

[[IF SCorpElection]]

Setting or reviewing officer compensation is not within this engagement unless section 01 lists it. We will report the compensation the entity actually paid, as its records show it. If you would like us to advise on a reasonable figure, we can take that on as a separate, named piece of work — tell us and we will add it to the engagement in writing before we start, and it is billed at our hourly rate as it is worked.

[[END IF]]

03What you are responsible for

  • Providing complete, accurate, and timely books for the period, closed and reconciled, including bank and card accounts, loans, and any activity outside the accounting system.
  • Providing a current and correct list of shareholders — names, addresses, ownership percentages, and every change during the period, with its date.
  • Telling us about dividends, contributions, loans between the corporation and its shareholders, and any change in ownership, however informal it seemed at the time.
  • Telling us about any foreign account, asset, or ownership interest, and any digital asset activity. These carry separate filings with potential penalties.
  • Keeping the records that substantiate what is on the return, such as but not limited to invoices, receipts, mileage logs, and business-purpose notes for travel and meals.
  • Reviewing the return before it is signed. The entity is responsible for its contents.

04Your deadline, and extensions

To file by the original due date, we need the entity's complete information by <<MaterialsDeadline>>. Material arriving after that date, or arriving incomplete, may require an extension.

An extension gives you more time to file, not more time to pay. Interest and penalties run from the original due date on anything unpaid.

We will file an extension on the entity's behalf where one is needed, unless you instruct us otherwise in writing. An extension does not move the payment date.

The deadline is the entity's, not ours. We will tell you what we need and when, and we will chase it.

05Unclear law, and penalties

The law imposes penalties when a taxpayer understates a liability. Where the law is unclear, or where positions conflict, we will set out the reasonable courses of action along with the risk and consequence of each, and we will adopt the one you choose. We will not prepare or file a return taking a position we could not defend in writing.

06Fees and billing

Our fee for this engagement is the amount shown on the estimate accompanying this letter. Work outside the scope described in section 01 is quoted in writing, and does not begin without your written consent to the new estimate.

Invoices are due on presentation. Balances unpaid after thirty (30) days carry interest at the maximum rate state law permits. We may suspend or withdraw from the engagement if an invoice goes unpaid, or if requested information is not provided. We are not responsible for a late filing that results.

We will not e-file a return before the invoice for it is settled, unless agreed upon in writing.

07Your records, our files, and delivery

We return your original records at the end of the engagement; store them, and everything supporting them, somewhere secure. We keep copies of your records and our work papers for seven years, after which they are destroyed. Our work papers are ours, not part of your records.

We send documents for signature through Encyro, and final returns are delivered password-protected. You send us documents through the secure upload link we email you. We will not send you anything unprotected, and anything you send another way is sent at your own risk.

08Confidentiality

We will not disclose the entity's return information to anyone without written consent from the entity, except where the law requires it. A shareholder is not automatically entitled to the corporation's return information, and we will not treat a call from one shareholder as authority to release the whole return. Tell us in writing who may ask, and what they may be told.

09Ending this engagement

Either of us may end this engagement in writing at any time. The entity is responsible for fees for work done up to that point. Our engagement otherwise concludes when we deliver the completed returns or, for e-filed returns, when the authorization is signed and we transmit them — not when the return is accepted.

If you have chosen not to e-file, filing the returns with the taxing authorities is the entity's responsibility, on time, with proof of mailing.

What was taken out, and why each one is untrue of a C corporation

  1. The whole of section 02 — “Schedules K-1, and your personal return”. A C corporation issues none. This is the section that made the business letter refuse to render for an 1120.
  2. The K-1 scope line in section 01, and the owner count that fed it.
  3. “that penalty is commonly charged per owner, per month” in the deadlines section. That is how a partnership and an S corporation are charged for late filing. It is not how a C corporation is, so leaving it in would have stated something false about this client's exposure.
  4. “An extension of the entity return does not extend any owner's personal return” — there is no flow-through to extend.
  5. The K-1 carve-out in the confidentiality clause. The rule is unchanged; only the exception that has no C-corporation equivalent is gone.
  6. “final returns and K-1s are delivered password-protected” → returns only.

Nothing was added. The firm said there may be things it wants specific to a C corporation; those are its to write, and this letter is the floor to write them onto, not a guess at them. Officer compensation still sits behind [[IF SCorpElection]] and therefore never fires here — whether a C corporation needs its own version of that clause is an open question, not an omission.

Section numbering

All three conditionals sit inside numbered sections, so nothing needs renumbering. That is deliberate: this letter is signed, and a signed document with a gap in its clause numbers invites exactly the question you do not want asked about it later.

Open item

Settled 26 August 2026 and the [CONFIRM] is gone. The firm's instruction was to combine the two options on the table: "exclude unless specified as part of the engagement". So section 03 excludes officer compensation by default, and offers it as a separate named piece of work that has to be added to the engagement in writing before it starts — which is the sentence that keeps a client from later claiming it was implied. It is billed hourly rather than at a fixed price, because a reasonable-compensation analysis is advisory work whose size is not knowable when the letter is signed, and hourly is what this firm does with work like that. No wording here makes a claim about what a reasonable figure IS; that stays a matter for the engagement it belongs to.

Deliberately not here

  1. No owner signature block. One signature binds the entity. Collecting owner signatures on an entity letter implies each owner is a client of this engagement, which is the opposite of what this engagement covers.
  2. No statutory dates in the prose. Every date is a merge field. A hardcoded March 15 is wrong for a fiscal-year filer and wrong again whenever Congress moves something.
  3. No reasonable-compensation figure, and no method for reaching one. A number in an engagement letter reads as advice, and this letter is not where that advice belongs.
  4. No fee. The estimate owns it; this letter points at it.
  5. No entity-formation or election advice. Whether the entity should be taxed as it is, is a different engagement with a different risk profile.

For whoever wires the software

  1. OwnerReturnsPrepared and OwnerReturnsElsewhere must be derived from one stored value. Two independent booleans can both be false, leaving section 02 silent on the single question this letter exists to answer.
  2. MaterialsDeadline is keyed by return type, and the entity value is earlier than the individual one. The registry already calls a MaterialsDeadline mismatch the organizer's most likely bug; an entity letter carrying the 1040 date is the same bug, one season earlier and with more owners downstream.
  3. ScheduleK1Target is a promise other people schedule around. If the software can only produce it as a guess, it should refuse rather than guess.
  4. The entity's EngagementRef is its own. Do not reuse an owner's — one engagement, one ref, and an entity with three owners must not inherit whichever owner was set up first.
  5. Strip the .f class and the .cond markers on the client-facing render.
