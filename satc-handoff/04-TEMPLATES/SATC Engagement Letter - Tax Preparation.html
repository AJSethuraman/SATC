02What this engagement is not

We prepare returns from the information you give us. We will not audit or otherwise verify it. We may ask you to clarify an item, but our work is not designed to detect errors, fraud, theft, or other illegal acts — and should not be relied on for that purpose. If we find something material, we will tell you.

We will perform bookkeeping only as far as preparing the returns requires. We do not perform audits, reviews, or any assurance engagement, and we issue no opinion on your financial statements. If a lender or investor asks for audited statements, you will need a registered audit firm.

Representing you in an examination, notice response, or appeal is a separate engagement, quoted separately.

03What you are responsible for

  • Providing complete, accurate, and timely information for each item of income, deduction, and credit upon request.
  • Keeping the records that substantiate what is on the return, such as but not limited to mileage logs, receipts, charitable acknowledgements, and business-purpose notes for travel and meals.
  • Telling us about any digital asset activity, any foreign account, asset, trust, or gift, and any business interest you hold. These carry separate filings with potential penalties.
  • Reviewing the return before you sign it. You are responsible for its contents.

04Your deadline, and extensions

To file by the original due date, we need your complete information by <<MaterialsDeadline>>. Material arriving after that date, or arriving incomplete, will likely require an extension.

An extension gives you more time to file, not more time to pay. Interest and penalties run from the original due date on anything unpaid. If we extend, we will estimate what you should pay with the extension based on the information available at that time.

We will file an extension on your behalf where one is needed, unless you instruct us otherwise in writing.

05Unclear law, and penalties

The law imposes penalties when a taxpayer understates a liability. Where the law is unclear, or where positions conflict, we will set out the reasonable courses of action along with the risk and consequence of each, and we will adopt the one you choose. We will not sign a return taking a position we could not defend in writing.

06Fees and billing

Our fee for this engagement is the amount shown on the estimate accompanying this letter. Work outside the scope described in section 01 will be quoted before we begin it.

Invoices are due on presentation. Balances unpaid after thirty (30) days carry interest at the maximum rate state law permits. We may suspend or withdraw from the engagement if an invoice goes unpaid, or if requested information is not provided. We are not responsible for a late filing that results.

We will not e-file a return before the invoice for it is settled, unless agreed upon in writing.

07Your records, our files, and delivery

We return your original records at the end of the engagement; store them, and everything supporting them, somewhere secure. We keep copies of your records and our work papers for seven years, after which they are destroyed. Our work papers are ours, not part of your records.

We send documents for signature through Encyro, and final returns are delivered password-protected. We will use another method at your direction, though it may not be secure and we would not advise it.

08Confidentiality

We will not disclose your return information to anyone without your written consent, except where the law requires it. This includes lenders, family members, and anyone calling on your behalf. We will speak with your attorney, banker, or advisor only if you instruct us in writing.

If you have chosen not to e-file, filing the returns with the taxing authorities is your responsibility, on time, with proof of mailing.

Merge fields

Two syntaxes, deliberately different so a regex can tell them apart: <<Field>> substitutes a value, [[IF Name]] … [[END IF]] keeps or drops a block. Every field below must resolve to something — a merge that leaves << in a letter sent to a client is the one bug that actually costs you a client, so have the software fail loudly on an unresolved field rather than shipping it.

FieldRequiredExample / notes
<<LetterDate>>RequiredFebruary 3, 2027 — spelled out, never 2/3/27
<<EngagementRef>>Required2027-0114 — same ref as the invoice and the statement
<<TaxYear>>Required2026 — appears three times; drive them all from one value
<<ClientFullName>>RequiredMr. and Mrs. Daniel Reyes — the addressee block
<<ClientLetterName>>RequiredDan — the salutation only. Keep it separate from the legal name; getting this wrong reads badly.
<<ClientAddress1>> <<ClientCity>> <<ClientState>> <<ClientZip>>RequiredAdd an optional Address2 line only if your data has one — an empty line in an address block looks like a bug
<<FederalReturns>>RequiredForm 1040 with Schedules A, C, and SE
<<StateReturns>>RequiredOhio IT 1040 — name every state. This is your scope boundary.
<<LocalReturns>>RequiredSolon municipal — name every locality. Emit "None" when there are none.
<<AdditionalForms>>RequiredTwo K-1s as reported · FBAR (FinCEN 114) — or "None" if empty. Never blank: with foreign reporting in scope here, blank and "None" are not the same statement.
<<MaterialsDeadline>>RequiredMarch 15, 2027 — a real date, not "early March"
<<ReturnInstruction>>RequiredSign through Encyro and it comes straight back to us. — replaces the old "return it in the envelope provided", which is wrong for an emailed letter.

The fee is not a field

Section 06 points at the estimate attached to the letter rather than restating a number. One place for the figure means the letter and the estimate can never disagree — so the merge has nothing to get wrong, and a re-quote means reissuing the estimate, not editing the letter.

For whoever wires the software

  1. Fields are text substitution into HTML — escape the values. A client named "Ross & Sons" will otherwise break the page.
  2. Fail on any unresolved << or [[ before rendering the PDF. No silent pass-through.
  3. PDF at Letter, 100%, no browser headers or footers. The footer block repeats on every page automatically; nothing needs to be positioned by hand.
  4. The merge-field styling (.f) exists so an unfilled proof is obvious. Strip the .f class, and the .cond markers, on the client-facing render — a real letter should show no field chrome at all.
  5. Name the output for a human: SAT-C Engagement Letter — Reyes — 2026.pdf. It sits in a client's downloads folder for years.
