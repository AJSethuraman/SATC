Merge fields

Serves either engagement letter — tax preparation or bookkeeping. Clauses are referenced by name, never by number, so renumbering either letter cannot break the pointer. Same conventions as the letters: <<Field>> substitutes a value, [[EACH List]] … [[END EACH]] repeats the rows between them once per item. Inside an EACH block, fields are dotted — <<Item.Service>>. Fail the render on any unresolved << or [[.

FieldRequiredExample / notes
<<EstimateDate>>RequiredFebruary 3, 2027 — this estimate's own date, at the head of the sheet. Set to the letter's date when the engagement is created, and moved when the engagement is quoted again. Two estimates in a drawer showing different totals under the same date is a question nobody can answer next February.
<<LetterDate>>RequiredFebruary 3, 2027 — the engagement letter's date, in the one sentence that names it. Never moves: the client has signed that letter. Byte-identical to the letter's own.
<<EngagementRef>>Required2027-0114 — the join key. Must be byte-identical to the letter's, or the pair comes apart in a file drawer.
<<ClientFullName>>RequiredMr. and Mrs. Daniel Reyes
<<ClientAddress1>> <<ClientCity>> <<ClientState>> <<ClientZip>>RequiredSame four fields as the letter, from the same record
[[EACH Assumptions]]ListOne per assumed item in the fee schedule, always — not only when something looks unusual. An assumption a client hears about after it fails is not an assumption, it is a surprise.
<<Item.Text>>RequiredA whole sentence, built in pricing.assumptions() from the schedule's assumed: block. Never typed here. It names what the fee assumes, whether it is inside the fee, what puts a client outside it, and the hourly rate that then applies.
[[EACH LineItems]]ListOne or more. Three sub-fields per item, below.
<<Item.Service>>RequiredFederal Form 1040 — the line as a client reads it
<<Item.Detail>>RequiredWith Schedules A, C, and SE — emit an empty string when there is nothing to add, never the word "None"
<<Item.Amount>>Required$450 — pre-formatted by the software, including the $
<<PeriodLabel>>RequiredSelf-describing, because the label above it is only "Period". "2026 tax year" for a tax engagement; "Monthly, from July 2027" for bookkeeping. Appears twice. Not shared with either letter — derive it from whichever engagement this accompanies.
<<EstimateTotal>>Required$785 — computed, not typed. Sum the line items in code so the arithmetic can't be wrong on a client-facing document.
<<PreparerName>> <<PreparerTitle>>RequiredArjun Sethuraman, CPA · Managing Partner

12 fields + 1 repeating list of 3. Nine come from the same records that drive either engagement letter, so generate the pair together from one call and they can never disagree. The other three — <<EstimateDate>>, <<PeriodLabel>> and <<EstimateTotal>> — are the estimate's own, and the first of them is why: an engagement can be quoted again, and the sheet has to say when.

Deliberately not here

  1. No expiry date. An estimate that goes stale invites a client to ask for it again at the old number; without a date on it, the conversation is simply a new estimate.
  2. No signature line. The engagement letter is the signed instrument. Two signature blocks in one envelope means one gets left blank.
  3. No restated scope. The estimate points at the letter's scope section rather than repeating it, so there is exactly one description of the work and no chance of two that differ.
  4. No hourly rates. The line items are the answer; a rate card invites arithmetic the client shouldn't have to do.

Figures

Amounts use tabular numerals, right-aligned, with the total ruled above and double-ruled below — the house convention from SATC Figures and Tables.html. Format money in one place in the software and pass strings through; never let two templates format currency differently.
