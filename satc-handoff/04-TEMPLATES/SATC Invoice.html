Arithmetic is the software's job

Never let a human type Subtotal or AmountDue. Sum the line items, apply the credit, format once. A client who finds an arithmetic error on an accountant's invoice has learned something about the firm that no amount of good work undoes.

Deliberately not here

  1. No hours, no rates. Consistent with the estimate: the line items are the answer. A rate card on an invoice invites the client to audit your time.
  2. No "thank you for your business". The letter and the delivery email carry the warmth; an invoice should be easy to read and easy to pay.
  3. No aging table. A first invoice showing a 30/60/90 grid reads as an accusation. Overdue balances are a separate statement document, not this one.
  4. No restated scope or terms. Both live in the engagement letter, which this one points at by name.

For whoever wires the software

  1. The due box duplicates <<AmountDue>> from the ledger deliberately — a client who skims sees the figure without reading the table. Two renders of one value, never two values.
  2. [[IF EstimateReference]] wraps text inside a list item, not a whole block. The marker stripper must handle inline conditionals as well as block ones.
  3. Strip the .f class, the .cond markers, and the tr.mark marker rows on the client-facing render.
  4. Name the output: SAT-C Invoice INV-2027-0361 — Reyes.pdf. The number goes in the filename; a client with three invoices needs to tell them apart.
