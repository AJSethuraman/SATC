The Notch

A square with a square removed — and the removed piece set beside it. Subtraction stated in pure geometry, so the razor is the operation, never a depicted blade.

01

Construction

Built on a 32-unit grid. The notch and the removed piece are both exactly 8×8 — the piece fits the hole it came from. That equality is the whole concept; if they differ, the mark becomes decoration.

outerSquare from 3 → 19 (16×16), stroke 2.4, no fill. Drawn as one path so the notch is part of the outline, not an overlay.
notch8×8 removed from the bottom-right corner, at 11 → 19. Exactly half the outer square's side.
piece8×8 solid, at 22 → 30. Identical to the notch — shown here dashed so you can read the correspondence.
gap1.8 units between the outline's outer edge and the piece. Tight enough to read as related, wide enough to read as separate.
pathM3 3 H19 V11 H11 V19 H3 Z
02

Colour

Navy monochrome is the primary. It prints, embosses, faxes, and photocopies without thinking about it — the test the old gold seal failed. The oxblood variant is for digital only.

04

Lockups & clear space

Clear space is one notch (8 grid units = 25% of the mark's width) on every side. Nothing enters it — not text, not a rule, not a page edge.

05

Don't

The concept is one square, one subtraction. Each of these breaks it.

Don't

Fill the outline. The notch stops reading as removal and becomes a shape.

Don't

Outline the piece. Solid vs. void is the contrast the mark depends on.

Don't

Resize the piece. It must stay 8×8 — it has to fit the hole it left.

Don't

Rotate it. The mark is orthogonal; tilting it reads as a lifestyle brand.

Pillow · replaces mark() in make-images.py
def mark(d, x, y, size, outer, inner):
    k = size / 27          # 27-unit footprint
    w = max(2, round(2.4 * k))
    # notched outline, drawn as 4 edges
    p = [(3,3),(19,3),(19,11),(11,11),(11,19),(3,19)]
    pts = [(x+(a-3)*k, y+(b-3)*k) for a,b in p]
    d.line(pts + [pts[0]], fill=outer, width=w, joint="curve")
    # the removed piece, 8x8
    d.rectangle([x+19*k, y+19*k, x+27*k, y+27*k], fill=inner)
What it means, in a meeting

"Occam's razor says don't keep what you don't need. The square is the work; the piece beside it is what we took out. We show it to you rather than hide it — that's the whole practice in one shape."

Say it once and never again. A mark that needs explaining twice isn't working — but having a true answer ready is worth more than a mark nobody asks about.
