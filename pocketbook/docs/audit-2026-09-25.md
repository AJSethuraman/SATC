# Audit, 25 Sep 2026: what the cube does now, against the next goal

The goal (`docs/NEXT-GOAL.md`): *"RANR is profit after losses — interest income + fees
− cost of funds − losses — and the cube's outputs, tests and synthetic book should
treat it that way; and the cube should be ready to test a derived column (income ÷
sales) against a dated outcome, on a holdout, from a committed pre-spec."*

The reference is `docs/statistics.md`, cited below as A1, B2 and so on.

**How this was done.** Three agents each read part of the code and ran scripts, and
changed nothing in the repository. Their scripts are in the session scratchpad. The
main claims were then checked again by hand against the source; the ones that
decide a fix are marked **checked**. Paths are under `src/origination_cube/` unless
another folder is named.

**Verdicts:** **fits** means the code already does what the goal needs. **Does not
fit** means a fix is needed. **Missing** means the thing doesn't exist yet.

---

## The short version

1. **RANR is still a multiple almost everywhere.** A multiple is the pocket's rate
   divided by the rest's rate.
   - The 25 Sep patch fixed the direction when the comparison is negative.
   - A comparison near zero still blows up. A pocket 0.19 points ahead of a rest
     earning 0.01% reads **20×**.
   - The Split tab's pooled RANR figure can come out **negative, with its range
     upside down** (−2.15, range 0.00 to −1.85).
   - The table in item a lists every output that is still a multiple.
2. **The synthetic RANR contradicts ruling OC-29.** Every loan gets a random −$200 to
   +$400, whatever it lost. A loan that charged off $20,000 still "earns" up to $400.
   Two tests lean on this (item c).
3. **"Losses" inside RANR is never defined.** The design notes imply GCO (gross
   charge-off), but no code or document says so (item d).
4. **Every rate runs the same test.** It is a z-test on a ratio of two sums. Three
   things follow:
   - **Share of loans:** this is *not* the pooled two-proportion test that A1
     describes. On one example it gives p = 0.090 where A1 gives 0.035.
   - **Dollar rates:** they use the same z-test. There is no permutation test (B2)
     anywhere.
   - **CMH:** the test subtracts ½ (a continuity correction). The worked example in
     `statistics.md` does not, so on the same counts the cube gives 0.085 where the
     reference gives 0.060 (items b, j).
5. **Below the fewest-loans floor, nothing is tested.** A 53-loan pocket with 30 bad
   (57%, against 8% in the rest of its band) reads "too few loans to test". Fisher's
   exact test gives it p = 3.9 × 10⁻¹² (item h).
6. **Edges are chosen blind.** Before cutting a column the analyst sees:
   - its blank share
   - three sample values
   - one line on why its meaning was suggested

   There is no histogram, spread or list of repeated values (item f).
7. **No dates, no window, no holdout, no pre-spec, no derived column.** Loan age is a
   filter on months since origination. The outcome is a flag "as of the extract",
   with no date (item g).
8. **The tree code is `docs/scout-vs-measure.py`.** It works on a synthetic book and
   trains on development loans only. It scores the frozen model on the holdout once,
   for AUC (item k).
9. **`statistics.md` itself:** almost every worked example reproduces exactly. Three
   small slips are listed at the end.

## a. Where RANR is a multiple

A **multiple** is the pocket's rate ÷ the comparison's rate. For a rate that can be
zero or negative (profit), a multiple has no stable meaning:
- near zero it explodes;
- two negatives divide into a positive.

The goal replaces it with a **difference in percentage points of booked dollars**
(pocket − rest).

**The two-negatives flaw, confirmed by running it:**

| Pocket | Rest | Gap in points | `stats.multiple` today | Before the 25 Sep patch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| −3.8% | −1.8% | −2.00 | −0.111× (reads worse, correct direction) | 2.11×, "better" |
| +1.0% | −1.8% | +2.80 | 2.56× | −0.56× |
| 0.20% | 0.01% | +0.19 | **20.0×** | same |
| 2.19% | 2.00% | +0.19 | 1.095× | same |

Two pockets each 0.10 points short read differently under the 10% setting:
- 1.90% against 2.00% reads "in line";
- 0.02% against 0.12% reads **"worse"**.

## b. What each rate's per-pocket test is

**One test for all four rates (checked).** It is a **Wald z-test on a ratio
estimator**:
- The rate is Σy ÷ Σx over the pocket's loans. Its **standard error** (how far the
  rate computed from these loans typically lands from the true rate) is the
  linearised one, built from six sums: n, Σy, Σx, Σy², Σx², Σxy (stats.py:236-243).
- z = (pocket rate − rest rate) ÷ √(SE² + SE²), using each side's own variance
  (unpooled). The p-value is two-sided (stats.py:258-272).
- "Rest" is the parent cell minus the pocket (engine.py:753).

| Rate | y / x | What that makes the test | Against `statistics.md` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bad, share of loans | flag / 1 | unpooled two-proportion z with n − 1 | **Does not fit A1**, which pools the rate. Pocket 12/200 against 30/1,000: cube p = **0.0897**; pooled A1 p = **0.0351**; Fisher exact 0.0541 |
| Bad, share of booked dollars | booked × flag / booked | the same z, one loan = one unit | **Does not fit**: the table under "Which test, where" says B2 (permutation) |
| GCO per booked dollar | GCO / booked | the same | **Does not fit**: B2 |
| RANR per booked dollar | RANR / booked | the same | **Does not fit**: B2, on the difference |

No t-test is used anywhere.

**The OC-31 per-pocket RANR test** (the suggested option, "each pocket's own luck
range") uses the same z-test's p-value after the allowance: RANR moves only if
p < 1 − confidence, and in its own direction (engine.py:207-218, 237-241). The
fewest-losses floor is skipped for RANR (engine.py:234).

**The allowance for many tests** (Benjamini–Hochberg or Bonferroni, engine.py:250-270)
matches statsmodels exactly. The family is one grid × one rate × one comparison, as A2
says, with two differences:
- For the rest-of-book comparison, the family also counts the band and segment
  **margin** cells, which are never shown. In a 2×2 grid that makes the family 8
  where A2 says 4. **Does not fit.**
- Pockets under the fewest-loans floor are counted in the family and never read.

The split's pooled p-values get no allowance, which is correct: there is one per grid
and rate.

**Smallest gap (A3).** The cube solves n = ((z₁₋α/₂ + z_power·√g)·S_d ÷
(x̄·(g−1)·R))² using the whole book's spread, and ignores the rest's noise. A3's
formula is different, but the answers are close:

| Pocket | Cube, 80% power | A3, 80% power | Cube, 50% | A3, 50% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 71 loans | 2.40× | 2.37× | 1.84× | 1.85× |
| 140 loans | 1.96× | 1.95× | | |
| 280 loans | 1.66× | 1.67× | | |

**Partly fits:** the same idea, a different formula.

**`luck_gap`** (book.py:1200-1219) is the suggested "how much worse" line. It is the
median over testable pockets of the smallest gap at power 0.5, and it matches its
closed form.

## c. How the synthetic book makes RANR

**`synth.py` (does not fit; contradicts OC-29):**
- Balance: `bal = uniform(5000, 60000)` (:72).
- Bad: driven by FICO, channel, revolving debt and asset class (:76-84).
- GCO: `bal × U(0.3, 0.8)` if bad, else 0 (:85).
- RANR: **`uniform(−200, 400)` per loan, whatever else is true of it** (:86).
- No dates, so there are no months on book (:29, :52).

On 20,000 rows (seed 7):
- **Correlations with RANR:** GCO +0.006, balance +0.001, bad +0.004. The average
  RANR is $102.50 on bad loans and $99.84 on good ones.
- **RANR per booked dollar is flat across score bands while losses climb:**
  - under 620: RANR 0.30%, GCO 13.2%
  - 740 and up: RANR 0.31%, GCO 2.3%
- **RANR per dollar falls 5.3× by loan size** (0.95% smallest quintile, 0.18%
  largest). That comes only from a flat dollar amount per loan divided by balance.

Tests that rely on RANR being independent of everything:
- `test_engine.py:96-100` ("RANR has no plant")
- `test_book_results.py:511-521` and the comment at :139-140 (the planted pocket
  "earns about the same")

**`test_generic._auto_book` (partly fits).** Its revenue is `amount × (4% + score
term) − loss × 0.5`: it takes out only **half** the loss, and has no months on book,
fees or cost of funds. It also labels a column `NetLossDollars` as meaning `gco`,
which the catalog calls "gross charge-off dollars".

## d. What "losses" inside RANR is

**Not defined anywhere.**
- The catalog says RANR is "RANR dollars" and GCO is "gross charge-off dollars"
  (settings.yaml:375-380).
- OC-29 (design.md:421-425) says RANR already includes credit losses and that "RANR
  less GCO would count the losses twice". That equates the loss with GCO without
  saying so.
- GCO can be negative in practice: a recovery (test_engine.py:47; test_findings.py has
  a RECOVERY measure). But the `gco` meaning's value check requires "never negative"
  (meanings.py:144-147).

So contribution before losses = RANR + GCO is the implied reading. If RANR nets
recoveries (net charge-offs), it should be RANR + net charge-offs. **This is the
firm's call** (question 2 below).

## g. Loan age and the outcome

**Loan age.** The setting is at settings.yaml:29-48 and is applied in
`engine.age_filter` (engine.py:406-444).
- **Months on book** = calendar months from the origination date to one as-of date.
  The as-of date is either a column holding one date or an `as_of:` date in the cube
  file; the workbook route only reaches it through a column.
- Loans younger than the setting, or with no readable origination date, are
  **dropped and counted**.
- Without the dates, 0 means no filter and anything else is refused with a reason.

**The outcome is an undated flag as of the extract**: "charged off, ever delinquent".
There is:
- **no outcome-date role**;
- **no window** ("bad within N months");
- **no months to bad**.

The 12-month option's own explanation admits the gap: "late ones may not yet" show.
**Does not fit** a dated outcome.

## h. Pockets below the floors

`reading_of` returns **"too few loans to test"** when loans < fewest loans, before
anything else is looked at (engine.py:221-247). Three things follow:
- The p-value is still computed and still counted in the allowance's family
  (engine.py:796, 805-810), then blanked in the workbook (book.py:1114-1119).
- A thin pocket is listed on Where it bleeds if its excess is above 0, and shaded blue
  if material.
- **Fewest losses never applies to RANR** (engine.py:234): RANR with 3 losses still
  reads "worse".

Reproduced on the synthetic book (every-20 edges, suggested floor 71 = 5 ÷ 7.13%):
**560–579 / Broker, 53 loans, 30 bad (56.6%)**, 7.42× the rest of its band.
- The z-test gives p = 1.5 × 10⁻¹¹, or 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ after the allowance for many
  tests. *(Corrected 26 Sep by the final check: this line first gave the
  after-allowance figure as the test's own.)*
- The workbook says "too few loans to test".
- Fisher's exact test (written by hand in the scratchpad; the cube has none) gives
  p = 3.9 × 10⁻¹².
- Walk 6's 29 of 50 gives p = 3.1 × 10⁻¹².

**Does not fit** A4 or B1.

## i. The Split tab's actual against expected (A7)

**Expected uses the low half's rate** (checked; engine.py:893-915):
- O = Σ high-half numerator.
- E = Σ (low-half rate × high-half exposure). This is A7's `E'`, the version the
  reference recommends. **Fits.**
- The range is O/E ± z·√V/E: symmetric on the ratio scale, floored at 0, two-sided,
  with no continuity correction. It runs for all four rates.

For comparison, if the pooled rate were used instead, O/E would fall from 1.620 to
1.237 on the audit's synthetic run.

**Gaps:**
- For RANR it is still a ratio. When E ≤ 0 there is no ratio, p or range at all
  (engine.py:911). **Does not fit.**
- For share of loans, the Mantel–Haenszel odds ratio (A5) and its Robins–Breslow–
  Greenland interval match statsmodels to 10 digits. But the CMH p-value beside it is
  **computed and never shown**: the "Luck alone" column shows the O/E test's p
  (book.py:1714).

## j. The CMH test and sidedness

- **The CMH test subtracts ½** (checked, stats.py:347:
  `(max(abs(num) − 0.5, 0))² / var`). On `statistics.md`'s own example (A6) the cube
  gives χ² 2.962, p = **0.0852**; the reference computes 3.525, p = **0.0604** without
  the ½. Both match statsmodels with the matching setting to 10⁻¹⁶.
- **Two-sided everywhere:**
  - the per-pocket z-test: swapping pocket and rest gives the same p;
  - the CMH test: a 1-degree-of-freedom χ²;
  - the Split dollar test (engine.py:913).
- **The steadiness test** is Cochran's Q on log odds ratios with Woolf weights and ½
  added to a pocket with a zero cell, as A8 describes. One difference: it is centred
  on ln OR_MH, not on the weighted mean. On A8's example that gives p 0.80510 against
  0.80511. **Fits**, with an immaterial difference.

## k. The tree code: `docs/scout-vs-measure.py`

Supplied by the firm on 25 Sep 2026; `statistics-examples.py` section 8 repeats part of
it.

- **Trains on:** a synthetic book it makes itself. There are five columns (FICO, log
  loan size, income ÷ sales, two noise columns) and a known planted effect.
  - "Development" is 40,000 loans from one seed; "holdout" is 15,000 from another.
  - They are two separate books, not an origination-date split of one extract.
- **Outputs:**
  - a plain logistic regression;
  - a random forest (400 trees, leaves of at least 40, seed 7) fitted on the first
    28,000 development loans;
  - permutation importance (drop in AUC, 10 repeats) on the other 12,000 development
    loans;
  - partial dependence on the ratio over a fixed grid;
  - a binned logistic regression, with edges typed in by hand "after looking at" the
    partial dependence, reported on development and then on holdout.
- **Can it touch the holdout?** The forest never trains on it. It *scores* the frozen
  forest on the holdout once, for AUC, and `statistics-examples.py` also checks its
  calibration there. **Separation is by convention**: they are separate arrays, and
  nothing stops a caller from passing holdout loans in.
- **What it doesn't do:**
  - read an extract
  - use dates
  - take columns by meaning
  - stratify by pocket
- **The binned regression adjusts for FICO and loan size as straight lines, not as
  pocket constants.** So B5's formula (`α_pocket`) is not the model that produced
  B5's table.
- **Needs numpy, scipy and scikit-learn.** The cube uses none of them.

**Three slips, none of which changes a conclusion:**
1. **B1** says "expected bad in the pocket 0.84". That is at the *rest's* rate (12 ×
   14/200); the script says so, the doc drops it. Under the no-difference model B1
   describes (the hypergeometric), the expected count is 12 × 18/212 = **1.02**.
   Either way it is far below 5.
2. **B3/B4's worked example is not printed by `statistics-examples.py`.**
   - Its numbers do reproduce exactly if the five groups hold 100, 400, 500, 400 and
     100 loans, which the doc doesn't say.
   - 66.47 is printed as 66.4; it should be 66.5.
   - The script's own two-stratum example, labelled "U-shape planted", has a trend
     p of **0.0069**, not the "trend says no" that B3/B4 describe.
3. **B7** says partial dependence is "flat at 7.1–7.4% from 0.1 to 1.0"; the script
   prints 7.64% at 0.10, so the range is 7.1–7.6%.

A5's "1.828" for the logistic cross-check: scikit-learn prints 1.8276 and an
unpenalised fit gives 1.8286. That is a rounding difference.
