# Design brief: the threshold-engine workbook

Paste this whole file into Claude Design, with the pictures in `screens/` and
the sample workbook `Thresholds-sample.xlsx` attached.

## The ask

Redesign how this Excel workbook looks and reads, above all the **Assess** tab.
Keep everything it does. Make it something a credit-risk analyst at a bank
can sit in front of and set thresholds in, without a manual, without flipping
between tabs, and without reading anything twice.

Deliver:

1. **Mockups of the Assess tab**, laid out as it would appear in Excel at 100%
   zoom on a 1920-wide screen: what sits above the fold, what is below, and in
   what order.
2. **Mockups of Thresholds and Chart**, and a light treatment for Evidence,
   Backtest, Glossary, Data and Run (reference tabs: read rarely, must stay
   clear).
3. **A token sheet** a developer can implement directly: colours as hex,
   fonts (name, size, weight), fills, borders, row heights and column widths in
   Excel units, number formats, and chart styling (series colours, line widths,
   marker sizes, legend position, axis label formats).
4. **For each change, the reason**: what a reader could not do before and can
   now.

## Who uses it, and for what

A bank sets the lines where a risk measure (a charge-off rate, a delinquency
rate, a credit score) moves from score 1 Low to 5 High. The workbook proposes
those lines from history; the analyst decides by:

- **picking a product** and looking at its history against the five score bands;
- **leaving out periods** that do not represent the risk (a financial crisis, a
  pandemic), each with a reason;
- **setting three distances** (how wide "normal" is, where Moderate-Low begins,
  how rare High is) and one rule (which score a value exactly on a line takes);
- **comparing scenarios**: their lines against keeping every quarter, and what
  each setting does to the count of quarters in each score.

Everything is live: typing a value moves every line, count, band and chart at
once. The analyst's own words, 13 Oct 2026: *"i want it way easier to fill
out"*, *"easier to understand this stuff"*, *"i dont want to flip back and
forth"*.

## What each part of Assess is for (top to bottom, today)

| Part | Job | Today's problem |
|---|---|---|
| Banner | Says what the tab is and how to use it | Heavy black band |
| Product picker, Latest, Check | The product, its latest quarter and score, and whether the lines stand (or why not, in words) | Check is easy to miss; it is the most important sentence on the tab when it is not blank |
| Score table | Each score's From/To line and how many quarters fall in it | Five scores read as plain text; nothing ties a row to its band colour on the chart |
| Main chart | History over the five shaded bands; left-out quarters as grey dots with a dark strip underneath | Good. Legend is crowded along the top |
| Periods left out | 12 rows: Product, From, To (dropdowns of real quarters), Reason, Status | Sits below the chart, so a change and its effect are not on screen together |
| Your settings | One row per product; picked product highlighted | Headers wrap awkwardly; the input cells don't look like the most important cells |
| What each setting does | Plain-English notes | A wall of italic grey |
| What the evidence says | The data's suggestion for the picked product | Easy to skip |
| Scenarios | Table and chart: your lines vs keeping every quarter; three bar charts of quarters per score as each setting changes | Good content; far down the page |
| What it rests on, dials | The numbers the lines are built from; for each setting, the lines and counts at other values | Long black-banded tables |

## Hard constraints (the design has to live inside these)

- **It is generated by code**: Python with `openpyxl` writes the file
  (`workbook.py`, styled through `keybank_style.py`). Anything in the design
  must be something openpyxl can write: cell fills, fonts, borders, number
  formats, row heights, column widths, merged cells (never on input rows: Excel
  then refuses to clear a row), conditional formatting (including colour
  scales and formula rules), data validation dropdowns, frozen panes, hidden
  columns, and native Excel charts (line, area, bar/column; stacked or not;
  combined on one axis). No images are needed and none should be required.
- **Plain Excel, no macros.** Banks block them. So: no buttons, no toggles
  that run code, no cell that writes to another. A control can only be a cell
  whose value formulas read.
- **One input per fact.** Without macros, one cell cannot follow the product
  picker and still hold a separate value per product. That is why Your
  settings is a table with one row per product. A design may show the picked
  product's values prominently, but it cannot make one set of input cells that
  switches with the picker.
- **Excel 16 on Windows** is the target. Fonts must be installed there:
  Arial, Calibri, Aptos, Segoe UI, Consolas. Charts render with Excel's own
  engine: no gradients worth relying on, no custom label positions beyond what
  Excel offers.
- **Live formulas stay.** Every number on Assess is a formula; the design may
  move cells, but the code keeps the formulas and their meaning. Cell
  positions are set in one place (`assess_layout()` in `workbook.py`), so a new
  layout is a change there, not a rewrite.
- **Words are facts.** Labels may be reworded for clarity, but each heading on
  Thresholds and on Assess's inputs needs a Glossary entry (a test enforces
  it), and a refusal's text says exactly why it refuses.
- **Brand.** It follows the KeyBank Excel design system already in the code.
  Current tokens: INK `0A0908`, KEY_RED `CC0000` (accent, sparingly), CRIMSON
  `960019` (alert text), PAPER `FFFFFF`, CANVAS `F4F1EC`, MIST `E4DFD5`, STONE
  `B9B4AC`, SLATE `57534B`, INK_TEXT `16130F`; score bands `F4F1EC`, `E4DFD5`,
  `EFD7CF`, `E0A6A6`, `CC8080` (Low to High); input cells `FFF2CC`; picked row
  `F6D9A8`. Fonts: Arial for display, Calibri for data, Consolas for code. A
  redesign may extend these tokens; it should not abandon them.
- **Lower is worse works too.** For a credit score the lines fall and the band
  order flips. The design must read correctly both ways.

## What good looks like

- On opening, without scrolling: the product, its latest score, the Check, the
  chart with its bands, and the inputs that move it. A change and its effect on
  one screen.
- Inputs look like inputs and nothing else does.
- A refusal is impossible to miss and says what to do.
- The scenarios answer "what happens if" at a glance, beside the main chart or
  directly under it.
- The reference tabs are quiet, scannable and consistent with Assess.

## In this pack

- `Thresholds-sample.xlsx`: the workbook on public data (Federal Reserve and
  FDIC charge-off rates), with credit card's agreed settings typed in.
- `screens/`: every tab as Excel draws it today, numbered in reading order.
- `code/`: the generator. `src/threshold_engine/workbook.py` builds the
  workbook; `keybank_style.py` holds the style system; `glossary.py` the terms;
  `tests/test_workbook.py` what must stay true.
