What changed, and what the reader can do now
1
Slicers become rows of buttons (Product 3 × 2, Measure 4 × 2, Source 3 × 1). They took 300 px before; now 78. Before, the pickers alone filled most of the first screen. Now the pickers, the yellow row, the headline, the Check and the chart are all on screen without scrolling.
2
The table's filter buttons are off (Table Design › Filter Button). The slicers still filter. Before, the arrows covered the header words ("Width of Moderat"). Now each header reads in full.
3
The Check cell says only OK or Refused. The full reason and its fix sit in a band right under the headline, with the To fix part in black. Before, a four-line refusal was squeezed into one cell and cut off. Now it is the widest line on the screen and says what to type.
4
Refused is crimson text on canvas, never a red fill. A red fill means 5 High. Refused means the settings don't fit, not that the series is bad, so it reads as "needs your attention", not as an alarm.
5
The chart sits beside its numbers: where each rating begins, how many kept quarters fall in each rating, and which rating holds the latest quarter. Change a setting and the lines, counts and shading move together, in one glance.
6
The sources table is also the chart's key. Each name is in its line colour, with its On chart switch beside it. Before, the overlays needed a paragraph to decode. Labels at the chart's right edge name each rating's line.
7
The explanations go below the fold, one line each. The full wording lives in Glossary (every heading still has an entry there).
Constraint kept. The yellow row is still a table row filtered by the slicers. Rows 10 to 113 hold the other 104 series and are hidden by that filter, so the row numbers jump from 9 to 114 for the first series. Everything that must always show sits above row 8 or below row 113.

Quarter: 2024Q1, 2024 Q1 or a quarter-end date. Product, Measure: your own names. Value: the rate. Units: percent, decimal or bps (blank means the default on Internal). CRR grade (optional): the grade for that quarter. System, Note (optional): kept as is.
What this adds, and why
1
A slot that takes the data as it comes. Internal data is an Excel table in the bank's own names and units. Nothing has to be renamed before it is pasted, and no macro reads it: every view is formulas over that table.
2
Map each name once. Every product and measure name found in the file gets a yellow dropdown (a UNIQUE list over the table). Rows that are not mapped are counted in crimson, so nothing drops out unseen.
3
Coverage at a glance. A 7 × 5 grid shows how many quarters each series has. It turns green once there are enough to judge.
4
The test that matters: do the ratings agree? The internal value and each outside source are rated on that source's own lines from Decide. If they get the same rating in most quarters, and move together, the outside series is a fair stand-in for setting lines. Correlation and gap back that up.
5
A verdict the analyst sets. The three yellow cells in step 3 define "reliable", so the call stays theirs and can be read off the sheet.
6
CRR grades, if they are there. Map the grades onto the 1 to 5 ratings, and the workbook says how often the bank's own grades agree with the proposed lines.
Assumption to confirm. "Internal CRR data" is read here as the bank's own quarterly rates per consumer product, with an optional CRR grade per quarter. If it is loan-level instead, the slot stays the same but needs a roll-up step (a pivot or SUMIFS by quarter) before this tab.
Constraint kept. The pickers are plain dropdown cells, not slicers, so filtering never hides rows on this tab. The mapping dropdowns are data validation over UNIQUE spill ranges.

What I would cut, and why
Six peer tabs (Peers delinquency, balances, pack as a visible tab, lagged, normalisation, mix). Their views live on the one Peers dashboard and follow its filters. Nothing to flip to.
Peers glossary and Peers run as tabs. They become sections of Glossary and Run, so there is one place to look up a term.
The paragraphs under Decide's chart and settings. Each becomes a one-line note below the fold; the full text is already in Glossary.
The header filter arrows on Decide's table. The slicers do the filtering, and the arrows covered the header words.
Gridlines on Decide's chart. The rating shading and the labels at the right edge already give the scale; gridlines under opaque shading never show anyway.
Repeated product names on Summary and Thresholds. They are hidden by formatting, not deleted, so filters and formulas are untouched.
Kept on purpose: the "Every quarter" chart option, the "On a line" setting, and the Decision column in the sources table. Each answers a question the analyst asks.
Open question: the lagged peer view. It is kept below the fold. If the analyst does not use it, it is the next thing to cut.
