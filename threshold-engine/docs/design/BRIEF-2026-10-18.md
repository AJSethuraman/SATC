## Why now

Round 2 (15 Oct) designed a workbook of 105 series. Since
then it has grown: two combined products (All consumer; Credit card + Other
consumer), new measures (30+ to charge-off and KeyBank's gap to peers and to
the industry on it, the large banks' share paying only the minimum), the
industry's charge-offs from 1990 as a separate source, and a whole new tab,
**Signals**. It is now 164 series, 56 rows to decide, and 19 visible tabs.
Everything was built to work and proven in Excel (2,396 of 2,396 reads in the
walkthrough); none of the new parts has had a design pass. The analyst, 18 Oct:

- *"i also think we can clean up the tabs in view or make it so that things
  are contained in the same page"*
- *"i want the SIGNALS tab to basically have everything we have been talking
  about and ways to compare them"*
- *"peers should carry throughout the workbook and be recorded somehow next
  to their graphs or whatever, similar to how the peers charts already update
  themselves, so it's clear who was included and who was not"*
- *"if i change the peers judgmentally it should show which turned off
  manually"*
- *"the normalization is good, but context is needed on its inputs"*; *"show
  the size as well"*
- *"shading helps understand on the gap that negative is better"*
- *"i want to see the like raw graphs too … i don't want to mask it and make
  it look bigger/smaller than it is"*

## The ask

**Go through every tab, visible and hidden, and design the whole workbook
again with what it is now for.** Not a restyle of what is there: for each tab,
work out what the analyst comes to it to do, whether the tab lets them do it
without hunting or scrolling, and design it so it does. Where the right answer
is a different set of tabs, a different order, or a section moved from one tab
to another, propose it. Fix every design problem you find, not only the ones
listed below; the list is what was seen in one look and is not complete.

Be thorough in how you work:

1. **Read before designing.** Open the workbook in Excel (Enable Editing),
   click through every tab, change the yellow cells, the slicers and Banks in
   the pack, and watch what follows. Read the walkthrough PDF for what each
   tab is used for, and `LOG.md` for why it is the way it is.
2. **For every tab, write down first**: its job in one sentence, who opens it
   and when (deciding, presenting in a meeting, checking a number, rarely),
   what must be visible without scrolling, and what is wrong today. Then
   design.
3. **Design the workbook as one thing**: one grid, one type scale, one way to
   show an input, a rating, a refusal, a source, the peer group, a chart, a
   note. A reader who has learned one tab should be able to read the next.
4. **Check your design against the constraints below before handing it
   over**, and say where an idea did not fit and what you did instead.

**The analyst cares about fine detail, and judges the work by it.** Correct
number formats, consistent spacing, the same fonts and sizes for the same job,
columns that line up, rules and borders used the same way everywhere, labels
that match their contents, nothing cut off or wrapping badly. That is the bar.
What it does not mean is more words: no added explanatory text, helper
sentences or decorative headings. If a tab needs a sentence to be understood,
fix the layout first. Fewer, exact words beat more.

Priorities, in order:

1. **Signals**, which has had no design pass at all and most needs one.
2. **The tabs in view**: 19 visible is too many. Which to merge, hide, move
   right, and in what order, so Summary is the front page and the rest is
   found without hunting.
3. **The peer group, carried through**: one way, on every tab that draws on
   the pack, to show which banks are in, which the book's rules took out this
   quarter, and which the analyst switched off.
4. **Decide, Summary, Thresholds and Lines by source** with the new content:
   11 measures and 7 products on the slicers, six source columns, 56 rows,
   measures that run negative.
5. **Every reference and data tab**: quiet, consistent, readable when someone
   does open it.

Deliver: mockups of every visible tab at 100% zoom on a 1920-wide screen (what
is above the fold and below, in order); for each tab, its job and what changed
and why; a token sheet a developer can implement directly (extend round 2's,
don't replace it); the tab list in its new order with what is hidden; anything
you would cut, and why.

## Known rough edges (from looking at the build, 18 Oct)

- The peer panel lists all 15 banks beside almost every Signals section: the
  same list, eight times.
- Every Signals chart is the same size in a three-across grid whether it is a
  headline or a supporting view.
- The fit tables carry eight fixed rows the analyst rarely needs all of; the
  highlighted pick row is easy to lose.
- The rescaled chart shows "yearly moves from each line's own normal". The
  inputs (each line's normal and one yearly move in its own units) are written
  under it in a sentence, because the axis cannot say both. See constraints.
- "Where the dollars are" is blank (with a line saying why) when the picked
  source has no Call Report dollars (Large banks, Industry from 1990).
- Summary's two new source columns are mostly *Not in this workbook*.

## Constraints added since round 2 (round 2's all still hold)

- **A chart's axis bounds cannot follow a cell**, so a chart cannot redraw its
  scale when the pick changes; a secondary axis is possible, but its bounds
  cannot be tied to the primary's either. That is why the rescaled view puts
  both lines on one standardized axis and states the units in words. If you
  have a better way inside this, say so.
- **Everything on Signals that depends on the pick, the window, the shift or
  the pack is a formula**, on the hidden Signals workings tab. Layout can move
  freely (positions are set in `signals_tab.py`), but nothing may become a
  pasted value.
- **Signals rates nothing.** It shows; the analyst decides on Decide. A
  design that looks like a scorecard would mislead.
- **Words are facts, in American spelling.** No verdicts on the tab (*"kept"*,
  *"set aside"*); the decision record is the evidence document, not the
  workbook.
