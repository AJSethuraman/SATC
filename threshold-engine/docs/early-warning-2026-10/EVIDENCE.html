CONSUMER INDUSTRY ANALYSIS · EVIDENCE RECORD · 18 OCT 2026

Early warning signals for consumer credit: what was tested, what was kept, and why the rest was set aside

Over 16 and 17 October 2026 the analyst asked whether FICO should be a rated dimension of the consumer book, and the question widened to every public signal that might come before card and mortgage losses. This is the record: each signal drawn against what later happened, in its own units and rescaled for timing, with the numbers behind it. Public data only. The outcome is at the top; the evidence follows in the order the decision was made.

A. What was kept, and the evidence for it

Each measure here is in the book, or shown beside its ratings. The charts are the test.

A1 · 30+ to charge-off, and the gaps to peers and industry

30+ to charge-off as a rating: the level, and the gap to peers

Two ratings from the same measure, both by level, as the book rates everything else. The level: KeyBank’s share of 30+ (including nonaccrual) dollars charged off within two quarters, on rating zones drawn from the peer group’s history at the agreed settings. The gap: KeyBank minus the peer group in the same quarter, on zones drawn from the gap’s own history; this is “performing worse than peers”.

Card: the level

Zones from the peer group: 2 begins 84.3%, 3 86.3%, 4 90.4%, High 101.4%. KeyBank 2025Q4: rates 1. KeyBank’s 30+ includes its nonaccrual card loans, which most banks do not use, so its share reads lower than it would on the same definition.

Card: KeyBank minus peers

Now -8.4 points: rates 3. At z 2.5 the method refuses the gap’s own history; zones shown at z 1.79, the highest that fits.

Other consumer: the level

Zones from the peer group: 2 begins 26.7%, 3 31.5%, 4 41.0%, High 69.2%. KeyBank 2025Q4: rates 4. Every source stepped up from about 35% to 60–85% from 2023, which looks like reporting and product mix rather than a collapse in cures, so lines drawn across the whole history flag everything since.

Other consumer: KeyBank minus peers

Now -22.7 points: rates 1. Zones at the agreed settings. The gap is measured in the same quarter, so the industry-wide step in 2023 largely cancels out.

Estimated roll and cure, card and other consumer

Built from every bank’s Call Report: 30–89, 90+ and nonaccrual dollars at each quarter-end, and gross charge-offs each quarter. Not tracked cohorts, so these are estimates of the waterfall, not true roll rates. Industry solid black, peer group dashed, KeyBank red.

All charge-offs here are gross (before recoveries): recoveries arrive later and mostly come from loans charged off in earlier quarters. The waterfall in three steps: 30–89 to 90+, 90+ to charge-off, and the two together.

Step 1, card: the roll ratio, 90+ this quarter over 30–89 last quarter

Above 1 because the 90+ bucket holds about three months of loans and 30–89 two. Its rise came before rising card charge-offs by 2–3 quarters, loosely (0.40 to 0.43 industry, 0.25 peers).

Step 2, card: gross charge-offs this quarter over 90+ last quarter

Industry about 80–90%: most of the 90+ bucket charges off in the next quarter, at 180 days. KeyBank has fallen to about 64% because its 90+ includes nonaccrual card loans, which most banks do not use and which are not all on the way to charge-off. Peers ran low in 2008–2010 as delinquency piled up faster than it was charged off.

30+ to charge-off, card: share of delinquent dollars that end up charged off (steps 1 and 2 together)

Charge-offs over the next two quarters ÷ (30–89 + 90+) at the quarter-end. Cards charge off at 180 days, so every delinquent dollar either charges off within two quarters or cures; one minus this is the estimated cure. About 80% charge off; it rose to 85% in 2009 and 88% in 2023. It needs the next two quarters, so it is known six months late. KeyBank includes nonaccrual, which it uses for cards where most banks do not.

30+ to charge-off, other consumer

Lower and less steady: 24–50% before 2023, 64–81% since. Closed-end loans charge off at 120 days and open-end at 180, and the line mixes student, auto (until 2011), unsecured and secured loans; it breaks at 2011 and 2024Q4.

For other consumer there is no usable link from the roll ratio to charge-offs. A tracked cohort, internally, would separate rolling from curing directly.

90+ to charge-off: one quarter and two quarters later

For each quarter-end: gross charge-offs over the next quarter, and over the next two quarters, as a share of the 90+ (including nonaccrual) dollars at that quarter-end. If everything in 90+ either charges off or cures within a quarter, the two lines sit close together; where the second runs well above the first, charge-offs are also coming from loans that were not yet 90+ at the quarter-end.

Card, next quarter

Card, next two quarters

Card charges off at 180 days, so the 90+ bucket (90–179 days) clears within one quarter: the one-quarter figure is the 90+ roll to charge-off (about 80% for the industry). The two-quarter figure adds the next wave, loans that were 30–89 at the quarter-end, so it runs about twice as high and is not a rate of the 90+ bucket.

Other consumer, next quarter

Other consumer, next two quarters

Closed-end loans charge off at 120 days, often inside a quarter without appearing in a quarter-end 90+ figure, so even the one-quarter figure can exceed 100%. It measures how much is charged off relative to the visible 90+ stock, not a roll rate.

B. What was set aside, and the evidence for that

Each of these was tested the same way. The charts show why it adds nothing the kept measures do not, or measures something else.

B1 · Refreshed FICO

Change in FICO: the early-warning measure

The level of a score trends with lending policy, but its change over a year moved ahead of card losses. The series is the score of the weakest tenth of card accounts at the large banks, on outstandings, so it falls for either of two reasons the data cannot separate: existing cardholders’ scores fall, or more low-score borrowers carry balances and so make up more of the accounts. Both lead to losses. Over 12 separate years, the large-bank card 10th percentile’s change and the next year’s change in industry card net charge-offs correlate at −0.91; measured in the same year, −0.36. Shown as a fall, so higher is worse, like the loss series.

What was inferred, and how

  • Every bank. Under 660 is exact where the bank publishes a 660 edge. Elsewhere it is a range between the bank's nearest published edges below and above 660, which the filing proves. Balances with no score are left out of every share, with their dollars stated.
  • Band edges. Each band's top is read as the lower of its own stated top plus one and the next band's bottom: KeyCorp's tag says 'greater than 750' where its table prints '750 and above'.
  • Bank of America. Home equity is its total home equity class (lines, loans converted to term and reverse mortgages are parts of it); before 2022Q4 that total is tagged with no class member, and equals its parts ($30.5bn at 2021Q2). Other consumer is 'direct and indirect', which includes auto loans; most of it, securities-based lending, has no score.
  • Wells Fargo. Mortgage excludes government-insured loans. Until 2022 it split first and second mortgages and cut every 40 points (600 to 800); home equity is its second mortgages until then, after which they sit inside residential mortgage and home equity has no Wells figure. Other consumer is mostly securities-based lending with no score; the share is of the scored part.
  • Citi. U.S. classes only. Card is its 'credit card receivables' class; it also tags a slightly smaller 'credit card' class, not used. Until 2024 it cut at 680 and 760 only, so its share under 660 there is known only to be at most its share under 680.
  • JPMorgan. Residential real estate, which also holds home equity, summed across its loan-to-value splits; it publishes only the 660 edge.
  • Regions. Home equity is its lines and its loans added together. Edges 620, 680 and 720.
  • Citizens. Other consumer is its other retail loans plus student loans, as the Call Report counts them. Edges 620, 680, 740, 800.
  • Huntington. Other consumer is RV and marine plus other consumer. Edges 650 and 750, so under 660 is a wide range. Whether its scores are refreshed is not stated in the filing read.
  • PNC. Edges 660, 720, 780. Card, auto and other consumer are its 'using FICO' rows.
  • SoFi. Card only, held at fair value; fine bands every 20 points.
  • Capital One. Auto only, and scored at origination, not refreshed: shown apart, never in a peer range.

Mortgage: why high DTI is not turning into foreclosures

The same database values every outstanding mortgage against today’s home price (current, or mark-to-market, loan-to-value), from 2013. A borrower with equity who cannot pay can sell; one who owes more than the house is worth cannot.

owe more than 90% of current valueowe more than 100% (underwater)in foreclosure

2013Q1: 25.2% owed more than 90% of their home’s value and 11.3% were underwater, with foreclosure still high. 2026Q2: 4.7% and 0.3%; the average outstanding mortgage owes 45% of current value. That cushion, from the 2020–2022 price rise, is why the high share of high-DTI loans has not produced foreclosures. The point to worry is when this line climbs: home prices falling, or new loans at high combined LTV piling up. The series starts in 2013, after the crisis, so it cannot show the run-up to 2008.

Rerun

From threshold-engine, after the book has been built (its series files are read for the Call Report measures):

python docs/early-warning-2026-10/record/evidence.py
# the derived data, when the caches are to hand:
python docs/early-warning-2026-10/record/rolls.py CACHE config/peers.json docs/early-warning-2026-10/record/data/rolls/rolls.json
python docs/early-warning-2026-10/record/fdic_numbers.py CACHE config/peers.json docs/early-warning-2026-10/record/data/rolls/numbers.json
python docs/early-warning-2026-10/record/peerfico_pull.py docs/early-warning-2026-10/record/data/peerfico "NAME EMAIL"
python docs/early-warning-2026-10/record/peers.py docs/early-warning-2026-10/record/data/peerfico/facts.json docs/early-warning-2026-10/record/data/peerfico/peers.json

The SEC facts file (14 MB) is not in the repository; peers.json, read from it, is. The FHFA zips (21 MB) are not; nmdb.json, the national series read from them, is.

What was not checked

  • Competitors’ FICO figures come from their filings’ tagged data; only KeyCorp’s 2026Q2 amounts were tied to a printed table.
  • Whether every KeyBank nonaccrual card loan is past due: the internal check that decides whether KeyBank’s 30+ share is flattered.
  • Which FICO version each bank uses, and whether the Philadelphia Fed counts every open account or only active ones for the score percentiles.
  • The lines in sections A and B are set from the same history they rate; the book’s Backtest, which sets each quarter’s lines from earlier quarters only, is the strict test and runs in the workbook.
  • With 2020–2023 left out the card matches rest on 20 to 37 quarters; the mortgage matches rest mostly on 2005–2010.
