CONSUMER INDUSTRY ANALYSIS · FICO EVIDENCE · 17 OCT 2026

What public refreshed FICO shows

KeyCorp’s own refreshed FICO by product from its 10-Qs and 10-Ks, ten competitors from theirs, and the large-bank card and mortgage scores. Every value is read from the filing or series named under Sources. Nothing here is internal.

  • No bank here publishes an average FICO. KeyCorp reports balances in four refreshed bands (under 660, 660–749, 750 and above, no score). The share of scored balances under 660 is the closest public stand-in for a weighted-average refreshed FICO; an average would need a midpoint for each band, which the filing does not give.
  • Run-off moves it. Home equity under 660 rose from 6.4% to 7.9% as its balance fell from $7.9bn to $5.4bn (2022–2026). Other consumer fell to 3.4% when indirect auto was sold in 2021Q3, then rose to 6.0% as the book ran off from $6.5bn to $4.0bn.
  • At the agreed settings, the book’s method fits KeyCorp’s own history for mortgage and home equity, and refuses card and other consumer. Card’s worst quarter, 12.0% in 2025Q1, is not far enough from its 10.9% median to rate High at z 2.5 (it fits at z ≤ 0.92). Other consumer has only 19 quarters since the 2021 sale, none far from the rest (z ≤ 0.76). Mortgage and home equity fit because 2020Q1, before the 2020–21 score rise, sits far above the rest.
  • FRED carries twelve score series, all from the Philadelphia Fed’s large banks, 2012Q3 on: card and first-lien mortgage, current (refreshed) and at origination, at the 10th, 25th and 50th percentile. FDIC Call Reports carry no score. The free bureau sources are averages of consumers, not of a loan book: VantageScore’s monthly CreditGauge (VantageScore 4.0, from 2020) and FICO’s national average (published twice a year, not as a data series).
  • The large-bank card score has no stress in its history. Its 10th percentile ran from 624 (2019Q4) to 644 (2021Q2). At z 2.5 it is refused; it fits at z ≤ {{P10FIT}}.

Why KeyCorp’s share under 660 rose: the waterfall

Since 2020 each bank’s FICO table is also split by the year the loans were made. That separates three things: loans made since the start (new loans), older loans paying off and changing the mix (run-off and mix), and the same loans’ scores moving (score drift). KeyCorp, end-2023 to mid-2026. Red raises the share under 660; green lowers it.

Score drift also includes better borrowers paying off within the same year’s loans; the filing cannot separate the two. Card is all revolving balances, so it has no years to split.

Large-bank mortgages: current score of accounts

Read it as a ruler. Line up every mortgage account at the large banks from lowest score to highest. The 10th percentile is the score one in ten accounts fall at or below; the 25th, one in four; the median, half. Where a dot sits in the red zone, at least that share of accounts scores under 660.

How it was read, and what was not checked

  • KeyCorp: each filing’s own quarter-end, bands summed per product; before 2020, purchased credit-impaired and other loans added together, as one book. The 2026Q2 band amounts tie to the printed table (mortgage 15,781 / 1,991 / 391 / 15; home equity 3,808 / 1,168 / 425 / 7; other consumer 2,831 / 952 / 240 / 326, $ millions). Earlier quarters are from the XBRL only.
  • Not explained: KeyCorp mortgage 660–749 went from 9.1% to 10.9% between 2025Q4 and 2026Q1 with no matching change in balance.
  • Not checked: whether any bank publishes a weighted-average refreshed FICO in its earnings supplement; the FICO version each bank uses; peers before 2026Q2.
  • Lines: the book’s method at the agreed settings (width 0.5, Moderate-Low 1.0, z 2.5), every quarter kept, higher share is worse; the score percentile treated as lower is worse.

Sources: KeyCorp 10-Q and 10-K filings, 2018Q2–2026Q2 (SEC EDGAR); Bank of America, Wells Fargo and PNC 2026Q2 10-Qs; Philadelphia Fed large bank credit card data via FRED (RCCCBSCOREPCT10, 25, 50); industry card charge-offs from FDIC Call Reports, as built in the workbook.
