# Labor market and household income indicators for a lending macro-risk narrative (US, as of Oct 2026)

_Research run 14 Oct 2026. Scope: labor market and household income only (household costs, balance sheets/housing, business conditions and regulators' use of macro data are covered by other researchers). Method: every FRED fact below (start date, frequency, SA, latest value, update and next-release dates, licence tag) was read directly from that series' FRED page or `fredgraph.csv` on 14 Oct 2026; bls.gov, bea.gov, oui.doleta.gov, philadelphiafed.org, atlantafed.org, federalreserve.gov and sec.gov pages were fetched directly and quoted. bls.gov refuses plain scripted clients (HTTP 403) but served pages to a browser-like request. Where only a secondary source was found, it is labelled._

## Labor-market indicators: what each measures, publisher, series id, frequency, history, geography, SA, access, lag, breaks

### Takeaway
Every core labor series is free and public-domain (BLS, DOL) and reachable keyless through FRED CSV or the publishers' own downloads; the national unemployment rate (BLS `LNS14000000` / FRED `UNRATE`, from 1948) and weekly initial/continuing claims (DOL ETA 539 / `ICSA`, `CCSA`, from 1967) are the backbone, with LAUS giving state (SA, from 1976) and county (NSA, from 1990) depth. The tool must handle three live data hazards: the permanent October 2025 hole in every CPS-based series (national, state, metro, county unemployment, U-6, participation, Sahm, Atlanta Fed WGT), the large CES benchmark revision (−898k for March 2025, about three times the 10-year average), and discontinued/changed products (Philadelphia Fed state leading indexes ended; state JOLTS now annual; CES Highlights ended).

#### Unemployment rate, U-3 (national)
- FRED `UNRATE` = BLS CPS series `LNS14000000`; monthly, percent, seasonally adjusted; first observation Jan 1948; latest Sep 2026 = 4.2%, updated 2 Oct 2026, next release 6 Nov 2026. Definition: unemployed as % of labor force, civilians 16+, 50 states + DC, non-institutional. — [FRED UNRATE](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/UNRATE)

#### U-6 (broad underutilization)
- FRED `U6RATE` = BLS `LNS13327709`; monthly, SA; first observation Jan 1994 (the redesigned CPS); latest Sep 2026 = 7.6%. — [FRED U6RATE](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/U6RATE)

#### Labor force participation
- FRED `CIVPART` = BLS `LNS11300000`; monthly, SA; from Jan 1948; latest Sep 2026 = 61.8%. FRED notes that long-run participation changes reflect secular/demographic trends (aging) unrelated to the cycle — relevant to any "normal level" estimate. — [FRED CIVPART](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CIVPART)

#### Sahm rule (current-vintage and real-time)
- FRED `SAHMCURRENT` (source: Claudia Sahm), monthly, SA, percentage points, from Mar 1949; latest Sep 2026 = 0.00; definition: "signals the start of a recession when the three-month moving average of the national unemployment rate (U3) rises by 0.50 percentage points or more relative to the minimum of the three-month averages from the previous 12 months." Tag: "Public Domain: Citation Requested". — [FRED SAHMCURRENT](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SAHMCURRENT)
- FRED `SAHMREALTIME`, from Dec 1959; latest Sep 2026 = 0.00 (Aug 2026 −0.07, Jul −0.03, Jun 0.07, May 0.10); "based on 'real-time' data, that is, the unemployment rate (and the recent history of unemployment rates) that were available in a given month. The BLS revises the unemployment rate each year at the beginning of January ... Revisions to the seasonal factors can affect estimates in recent years. Otherwise the unemployment rate does not revise." — [FRED SAHMREALTIME](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SAHMREALTIME)

#### Initial jobless claims (DOL ETA 539)
- FRED `ICSA` (source: U.S. Employment and Training Administration, release "Unemployment Insurance Weekly Claims Report"), weekly, SA, from week ending 7 Jan 1967; latest week ending 3 Oct 2026 = 197,000, updated 8 Oct 2026, next 15 Oct 2026. "An initial claim is a claim filed by an unemployed individual after a separation from an employer." — [FRED ICSA](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/ICSA)
- State initial claims, e.g. `CAICLAIMS`: weekly, **not seasonally adjusted**, from Feb 1986, release "State Unemployment Insurance Weekly Claims Report", published one day later than the national (updated 9 Oct 2026 for week ending 3 Oct). Pattern `{ST}ICLAIMS`. — [FRED CAICLAIMS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CAICLAIMS)

#### Continuing claims / insured unemployment
- FRED `CCSA`, weekly, SA, from Jan 1967; latest week ending 26 Sep 2026 = 1,716,000 (one week behind initial claims). "Continued claims data are based on the week of unemployment, not the week when the initial claim was filed." — [FRED CCSA](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CCSA)
- FRED `IURSA` insured unemployment rate (continued claims / covered employment), weekly, SA, from Jan 1971; latest 1.1% (week ending 26 Sep 2026). — [FRED IURSA](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/IURSA)

#### Payroll employment (CES)
- FRED `PAYEMS` = BLS `CES0000000001`; monthly, SA, thousands, from Jan 1939; latest Sep 2026 = 159,044k. NSA twin `PAYNSA`. — [FRED PAYEMS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PAYEMS)

#### Temporary help services employment
- FRED `TEMPHELPS` = BLS `CES6056132001`; monthly, SA, from Jan 1990; latest Sep 2026 = 2,486.8k. — [FRED TEMPHELPS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/TEMPHELPS)

#### Average weekly hours
- FRED `AWHAETP` = BLS `CES0500000002` (all employees, total private); monthly, SA, **from Mar 2006 only**; latest Sep 2026 = 34.4. — [FRED AWHAETP](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/AWHAETP)
- Longer history: manufacturing production-worker hours `AWHMAN`, from Jan 1939 (verified below).

#### JOLTS (openings, layoffs, quits)
- FRED `JTSJOL` (openings), `JTSLDL` (layoffs and discharges), `JTSQUL` (quits): BLS JOLTS; monthly, SA, thousands, **from Dec 2000**; latest Aug 2026: openings 7,079k, layoffs 1,641k, quits 3,066k; updated 29 Sep 2026, next 3 Nov 2026 (i.e., ~1-month-plus lag relative to the Employment Situation). — [FRED JTSJOL](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/JTSJOL); [FRED JTSLDL](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/JTSLDL); [FRED JTSQUL](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/JTSQUL)

#### State unemployment (LAUS)
- FRED `CAUR` (pattern `{ST}UR`): BLS release "State Employment and Unemployment"; monthly, SA, from Jan 1976; latest Aug 2026 = 5.1%, updated 18 Sep 2026, next 20 Oct 2026 (state data run about two weeks behind the national report). — [FRED CAUR](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CAUR)

#### County and metro unemployment (LAUS) — method, ids, SA status
- LAUS is "a federal-state cooperative effort" producing monthly employment and unemployment for "over 7,500 areas": states, MSAs, metropolitan divisions, micropolitan areas, combined areas, small labor market areas, counties and county equivalents (plus cities). "State monthly model-based estimates are controlled in 'real time' to sum to national monthly employment and unemployment estimates from the CPS"; models combine CPS, CES and state UI data. Counties use the "Handbook method", a building-block approach using CPS, CES, state UI and the ACS, "adjusted to the statewide measures". BLS also publishes "Seasonally Adjusted Metropolitan Area Estimates" and "Alternative Measures of Labor Underutilization for States" (state U-1–U-6). — [BLS LAUS overview](https://www.bls.gov/lau/lauov.htm)
- BLS LAUS series id format: e.g. `LAUCN281070000000003` = prefix LA, seasonal code U, area code CN2810700000000 (county FIPS 28107), measure code 03 (unemployment rate). — [BLS Series ID formats](https://www.bls.gov/help/hlpforma.htm)
- FRED county example `OHCUYA5URN` (Cuyahoga County, OH): monthly, **not seasonally adjusted**, from Jan 1990; release "Unemployment in States and Local Areas (all other areas)", updated 30 Sep 2026 for August 2026, next 28 Oct 2026 (≈2-month lag); "Public Domain: Citation Requested". FRED county ids embed a non-derivable digit, so they must be looked up, not constructed (repo note `macro-early-warning-dashboard/COVERAGE_RESEARCH_MACRO.md`). — [FRED OHCUYA5URN](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/OHCUYA5URN)
- The October 2025 state and metro releases were canceled because the CPS — LAUS's primary input — was not collected; the FRED state series (e.g. `CAUR`) shows October 2025 blank. — [BLS revised release dates](https://www.bls.gov/bls/2025-lapse-revised-release-dates.htm); [FRED CAUR CSV](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=CAUR)

#### Additional series verified on FRED (14 Oct 2026)
- `IC4WSA` 4-week moving average of initial claims, weekly, SA, from 28 Jan 1967; 198,000 (week ending 3 Oct 2026). — [FRED IC4WSA](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/IC4WSA)
- `AWHMAN` average weekly hours, production and nonsupervisory employees, manufacturing; monthly, SA, **from Jan 1939**; Sep 2026 = 42.0 (an LEI component; longer history than `AWHAETP`). — [FRED AWHMAN](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/AWHMAN)
- `JTSLDR` layoffs and discharges rate (1.0% Aug 2026) and `JTSQUR` quits rate (1.9% Aug 2026), monthly, SA, from Dec 2000. — [FRED JTSLDR](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/JTSLDR); [FRED JTSQUR](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/JTSQUR)
- BLS JOLTS series id format: e.g. `JTU110099000000000HIL` = JT prefix, seasonal code, industry (6), state (2), area (5), size class (2), data element (2, e.g. HI hires, JO openings, LD layoffs, QU quits), rate/level (L/R). — [BLS Series ID formats](https://www.bls.gov/help/hlpforma.htm)

#### Temporary help services — leading-indicator status (official but descriptive)
- BLS Monthly Labor Review (Conlon, April 2011): temporary help services is an industry "which regularly leads fluctuations in aggregate employment" and "is considered by many to be a leading indicator of change in aggregate employment"; it lost 93,000 jobs in the 16 months before the 2007–09 recession and fell 912,000 from its August 2006 peak to its August 2009 trough; "Since 1990, peaks and troughs in temporary help services employment generally have led those of total nonfarm employment." Fell 4% and 13% in the 1990–91 and 2001 recessions. Series starts 1990. — [BLS MLR, "The 2007–09 Recession: Professional & Business Services" (PDF)](https://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/2011/04/art6full.pdf)
- Lead length is only blog/industry-sourced (e.g. ~4 months over 1990–2013, "noisy"); no peer-reviewed lead-time estimate found. — [Calculated Risk, 2013](https://www.calculatedriskblog.com/2013/05/temporary-help-services-and-employment.html)

#### Challenger job-cut announcements (private; free to read, not a government series)
- Challenger, Gray & Christmas (outplacement firm) publishes a monthly PDF of **announced** job cuts by industry and reason, with annual history in the report's tables back to 1993; each table carries "Source: Challenger, Gray & Christmas". August 2026 report: 52,881 announced cuts. No licence or reuse terms were found in the PDF; it is not a government statistic, measures announcements rather than separations, and there is no API — treat as proprietary (reuse terms **not verified**). — [Challenger Report, August 2026 (PDF)](https://www.challengergray.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Challenger-Report-August-2026.pdf)
- September 2026 (secondary source only): 43,281 announced cuts, down 18% from August; 573,195 year to date, down 39% from 946,426 in Jan–Sep 2025. — [Dow Jones via Morningstar, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202610012110/us-employer-layoff-plans-fall-in-september-challenger-says)

#### Philadelphia Fed state coincident indexes
- Publisher: Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia; monthly coincident index for each of the 50 states, released "a few days after" BLS state employment data; January release follows ~2 weeks after BLS's mid-March annual benchmark because all 50 models are re-estimated. Inputs: nonfarm payroll employment, average hours in manufacturing (production workers), unemployment rate, and wages and salaries plus proprietors' income deflated by US CPI; trend set to state GDP trend; Stock–Watson dynamic single-factor model adapted by Crone and Clayton-Matthews. Page last updated 23 Sep 2026 (current report: August 2026). "Changes to Methodology" document last updated 9 Jan 2026. — [Philadelphia Fed, State Coincident Indexes](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/regional-economic-analysis/state-coincident-indexes)
- On FRED: `{ST}PHCI` (e.g., `CAPHCI`), index 2007=100, SA, from Jan 1979; licence tag **"Copyrighted: Citation Required"** (unlike BLS/DOL series, which carry "Public Domain: Citation Requested"). — [FRED CAPHCI](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CAPHCI)
- **State leading indexes are frozen:** FRED `USSLIND` last observation Feb 2020, last updated 14 Apr 2020, next release "Not Available". — [FRED USSLIND](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/USSLIND)
- **State leading indexes formally discontinued:** "The Philadelphia Fed Will No Longer Release the State Leading Indexes ... In 2020, due to the sudden, extreme impact of the COVID-19 outbreak on initial unemployment claims, our researchers' standard approach ... was not well suited. ... the Philadelphia Fed has reevaluated the potential to resume accurate estimation in 2025 and ultimately made the decision to no longer release the state leading indexes." Inputs had been: the coincident index, state housing permits (1–4 units), state initial UI claims, ISM delivery times, 10y–3m Treasury spread; target = 6-month growth of the coincident index. — [Philadelphia Fed, State Leading Indexes](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/regional-economic-analysis/state-leading-indexes)
- **Coincident-index methodology changes relevant to a time-series tool:** (a) effective with the November 2025 release, all 50 state and US indexes treat the October 2025 unemployment rate as missing, following BLS's 11 Dec 2025 announcement that "household survey data were not collected for the October 2025 reference period due to a lapse in appropriations and will not be collected retroactively"; (b) since the January 2024 release, the annual estimation of smoothing weights uses Jan 1978–Jan 2024 excluding 2020 (2020 treated as missing to exclude the pandemic shock); (c) January 2020: proprietors' income added and the practice of excluding large changes in inputs dropped ("some turning points have been smoothed out or lessened" before); (d) April 2018: re-indexed to 2007 annual average (previously July 1992); (e) January 2017: model coefficients estimated once a year (January reference month); (f) January 2016: switched to BLS seasonal factors for state employment. — [Philadelphia Fed, Changes to Methodology: State Coincident Indexes (PDF, updated 9 Jan 2026)](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/FRBP/Assets/Surveys-And-Data/coincident/Methodology.pdf)

#### Data disruptions and breaks that apply across BLS labor series (verified on bls.gov, 14 Oct 2026)
- **2025 lapse in appropriations (1 Oct – 12 Nov 2025):** "All CPS operations, including data collection, were suspended during that time period." "There are no CPS estimates for October 2025. CPS data were not collected for October 2025 ... and were not collected retroactively." BLS issued no October 2025 Employment Situation release. — [BLS, 2025 federal government shutdown impact on the CPS (Q&A, last modified 11 Mar 2026)](https://www.bls.gov/cps/methods/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cps.htm)
- How the hole appears: a dash in CPS tables; in the LN database HTML "-(9)" with footnote "Data unavailable due to the 2025 lapse in appropriations"; blank cells in XLSX; dashes with no footnote in CSV/text; in the BLS Public Data API "a dash to represent the missing data value" and net/percent changes not visible. No public-use microdata file for October 2025. — [BLS CPS shutdown Q&A](https://www.bls.gov/cps/methods/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cps.htm)
- CPS seasonal adjustment methodology unchanged ("can accommodate missing values"); November 2025 response rate 64.0% ("a series low"; prior 12-month average 68.4%; previous low 64.9% in June 2020); November's unemployment rate needed a 0.26 pp change to be significant vs 0.21 pp in September (factor 1.23); 2025 annual averages use 11 months and "are not strictly comparable"; **no CPS Q4 2025 quarterly estimates**. — [BLS CPS shutdown Q&A](https://www.bls.gov/cps/methods/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cps.htm)
- **Population controls (annual CPS break):** normally introduced with January data in February; in 2026 they were introduced with February data on 6 Mar 2026, and all January 2026 CPS estimates in the database were revised on 6 Mar 2026 (the January release was not reissued). BLS also published an "Experimental series accounting for January 2026 population control effects" (9 Apr 2026). — [BLS CPS shutdown Q&A, item 15](https://www.bls.gov/cps/methods/2025-federal-government-shutdown-impact-cps.htm); [BLS CPS notices](https://www.bls.gov/cps/notices/)
- CPS sample redesign (2020-Census-based) phased in April 2025 – July 2026; "expected to have a negligible effect on published estimates." — [BLS notice, 13 May 2025](https://www.bls.gov/cps/notices/2025/2025-sample-redesign.htm)
- **CES / JOLTS / LAUS / ECI release disruptions from the 2025 lapse:** September 2025 Employment Situation moved from 3 Oct to 20 Nov 2025; October 2025 CES establishment data were published with November data (16 Dec 2025); October 2025 State and Metro Employment and Unemployment releases canceled (LAUS's primary input, the CPS, was not collected); September 2025 JOLTS release canceled and data published with October (9 Dec 2025); Q3 2025 ECI moved from 31 Oct to 10 Dec 2025; October 2025 CPI all-items not published; October 2025 Real Earnings not published. — [BLS, Revised release dates following the 2025 and 2026 lapses](https://www.bls.gov/bls/2025-lapse-revised-release-dates.htm)
- **A second, short 2026 lapse** shifted early-February 2026 releases by 2–5 days: December 2025 JOLTS (3→5 Feb), December 2025 metro employment (4→6 Feb), January 2026 Employment Situation (6→11 Feb), January 2026 CPI and Real Earnings (11→13 Feb). The page does not state the lapse dates. — [BLS revised release dates page](https://www.bls.gov/bls/2025-lapse-revised-release-dates.htm)
- **CES annual benchmark (2026):** March 2025 benchmark revised SA total nonfarm by **−898,000 (−0.6%)** (NSA −862,000, −0.5%); "Over the prior 10 years, the annual benchmark revision at the total nonfarm level has averaged 0.2 percent (in absolute terms)". Revisions wedge back to April 2024 and forward through the post-benchmark period. Prior year (March 2024 benchmark): −589,000 (−0.4%). — [BLS CES benchmark article](https://www.bls.gov/web/empsit/cesbmart.htm); [BLS CES benchmark announcement, Feb 2025](https://www.bls.gov/ces/notices/2025/ces-benchmark-announcement-revisions-to-total-nonfarm-employment.htm)
- JOLTS annual revision with January 2026 data: NSA and SA data from January 2021 forward subject to revision. **State JOLTS moved from a monthly to an annual news release** (first annual release 22 Jul 2026; state maps now show annual averages). — [BLS JOLTS revisions notice](https://www.bls.gov/jlt/notices/2026/jolts-2026-annual-revisions.htm); [BLS JOLTS state publication changes 2026](https://www.bls.gov/jlt/notices/2026/jltst-publication-changes-2026.htm)
- CES "Highlights" publication discontinued (notice dated 19 Dec 2025). — [BLS CES notices](https://www.bls.gov/ces/notices/)
- LAUS posted "Important Information on LAUS Data Revisions" on 28 Sep 2026, but the notice body did not render in a scripted fetch; contents not verified. — [BLS LAUS notices](https://www.bls.gov/lau/notices/)

#### Free access and licensing (BLS, DOL, FRED)
- BLS: "everything that we publish ... is in the public domain, except for previously copyrighted photographs and illustrations"; citation requested. — [BLS Copyright Information](https://www.bls.gov/opub/copyright-information.htm)
- BLS Public Data API: v2.0 requires (free) registration (email + organization), returns up to 20 years for up to 50 series per request, daily limit 500 queries; v1.0 (no registration) returns up to 10 years for up to 25 series; one-day lag between publication and API availability. — [BLS Data API features](https://www.bls.gov/bls/api_features.htm)
- DOL ETA 539 ("Weekly Claims and Extended Benefits Trigger Data"): "Initial claims measure emerging unemployment and continued weeks claimed measure the number of the insured unemployed"; published in ETA's weekly release; downloadable national and state history (HTML, spreadsheet, XML) without a key at oui.doleta.gov. On 14 Oct 2026 the claims page carried a notice: "We are currently encountering technical issues with our database, which may affect the timely availability of data". — [DOL ETA data downloads](https://oui.doleta.gov/unemploy/DataDownloads.asp); [DOL weekly claims data](https://oui.doleta.gov/unemploy/claims.asp)
- FRED tags DOL claims series and most BLS series "Public Domain: Citation Requested"; Philadelphia Fed coincident indexes and the KC Fed LMCI "Copyrighted: Citation Required". The `SAHMREALTIME` page carries both the tag "Public Domain: Citation Requested" and the banner "Data in this graph are copyrighted. - **Claims during the 2025 shutdown (secondary source):** Bloomberg reported that DOL issued no weekly claims report after 25 Sep 2025 during the lapse but kept downloadable data for most states online; Bloomberg seasonally adjusted those with BLS's pre-published factors (e.g. ~218,000 for the week ended 25 Oct 2025). The national weekly series has no hole in FRED's current vintage, but real-time users saw a gap. — [Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/daily-labor-report/us-initial-jobless-claims-rose-last-week-state-data-suggest-1)
- **KC Fed Labor Market Conditions Indicators:** `FRBKCLMCILA` (level of activity) and `FRBKCLMCIM` (momentum), monthly, SA, from Jan 1992; Sep 2026 = 0.216 and 0.195; "A positive value indicates that labor market conditions are above their long-run average"; updated 6 Oct 2026, next 10 Nov 2026. — [FRED FRBKCLMCILA](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/FRBKCLMCILA); [FRED FRBKCLMCIM](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/FRBKCLMCIM)
- **County-data caution (observation, cause not checked):** FRED's Cuyahoga County, OH NSA unemployment rate reads 3.2% (May 2026), 3.7% (Jun), 3.3% (Jul) and 1.9% (Aug 2026). A 1.4-point one-month fall is unusual; it coincides with BLS's 28 Sep 2026 LAUS notice on data revisions, whose text could not be read. A tool should flag such county moves rather than score them. — [FRED OHCUYA5URN CSV](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=OHCUYA5URN); [BLS LAUS notices](https://www.bls.gov/lau/notices/)

#### WARN notices (advance layoff notices) — public aggregate exists, but research-grade
- Cleveland Fed WP 20-03R (Krolikowski and Lunsford, 1 Feb 2022): "We collect data from Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification (WARN) Act notices ... The number of workers affected by WARN notices ('WARN layoffs') leads state-level initial unemployment insurance claims, and changes in the unemployment rate and private employment. WARN layoffs move closely with aggregate layoffs from Mass Layoff Statistics and the Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey, but are timelier and cover a longer sample. ... they improve pseudo real-time forecasts of the unemployment rate." Data posted at openICPSR (doi 10.3886/E155161). — [Cleveland Fed, Advance Layoff Notices and Aggregate Job Loss](https://www.clevelandfed.org/publications/working-paper/2022/wp-2003r-advance-layoff-notices-and-aggregate-job-loss)
- The openICPSR project page refused a scripted fetch (HTTP 403), so whether the panel is still updated in 2026 was **not verified**; search results show versions up to at least V28. — [openICPSR project 155161](https://www.openicpsr.org/openicpsr/project/155161/version/V28/view)
- There is no federal WARN aggregate: raw notices sit on individual state workforce agency websites (per the Cleveland Fed paper's description of data collection). — [Cleveland Fed WP 20-03R PDF](https://www.clevelandfed.org/-/media/project/clevelandfedtenant/clevelandfedsite/publications/working-papers/2022/wp-2003r-advance-layoff-notices-and-aggregate-job-loss-pdf.pdf?sc_lang=en)

#### Extended Benefits trigger data (DOL) — a statutory, published threshold on insured unemployment
- DOL publishes a weekly "Trigger Notice Report" under the Federal-State Extended Unemployment Compensation Act of 1970 giving, for each state, the 13-week insured unemployment rate (IUR), IUR as a percent of prior years, the 3-month SA total unemployment rate (TUR), TUR as a percent of prior year, and on/off status. Trigger Notice No. 2026-39, effective 11 Oct 2026 (IUR for 13 weeks ending 26 Sep 2026; TUR for 3 months ending Aug 2026): "Total Number 'ON': 0". ### Inferences
- For a "normal level and outliers" tool, the October 2025 hole must be stored as missing, never interpolated, in every CPS-derived series (U-3, U-6, participation, LAUS state/metro/county, Sahm, Atlanta Fed WGT); FRED's CSV leaves it blank and the BLS API returns a dash, so a parser that coerces "-" or "" to zero would manufacture a false 0% unemployment outlier. 3-month averages spanning Oct 2025 (Sahm, WGT) are computed from two months by their publishers — the tool should do the same or flag it.
- Claims are the timeliest public labor signal (5-day lag, weekly, from 1967) and are unaffected by the CPS hole in the current vintage, which makes them the natural early-warning lane; unemployment is the anchor for loss translation because that is the variable the Fed and banks' CECL models actually use.
- State SA unemployment (LAUS, 1976+) is long enough to include five recessions (1980, 1981–82, 1990–91, 2001, 2007–09) plus 2020; county data (1990+, NSA only) supports a year-over-year or same-month comparison, not month-to-month outlier rules.
- The −898k CES benchmark (≈0.6%, three times the 10-year mean absolute revision) means payroll-growth "outliers" computed on first prints can disappear after benchmarking; a payroll lane should score on the latest vintage and keep vintages (ALFRED) if real-time behaviour matters.
- Philly Fed coincident indexes add a single state-level activity measure, but their copyright tag and whole-history annual re-estimation make them better as context than as a scored input.

- No reachable source confirmed whether the Cleveland Fed WARN panel on openICPSR is still updated in 2026 (HTTP 403).
- Challenger licence/reuse terms: none stated in the report PDF; status unverified.
- OCC Semiannual Risk Perspective (Spring 2026) page did not render its text in a scripted fetch, so OCC's use of labor indicators was not verified here (other researcher covers regulators).

## Income indicators: real disposable personal income, wages (Atlanta Fed WGT, ECI, AHE), personal saving rate

### Takeaway
Income series are free and long (BEA monthly from 1959; ECI first published in 1976, covering Sep 1975–Mar 1976, and on FRED's current basis from 2001; Atlanta Fed WGT from 1997), but they are the most revision-prone inputs in this strand: BEA's 30 Sep 2026 annual update revised personal income back to January 2021 and moved the saving rate up by more than a point (October 2025: 3.7% first print → 4.9% now). October 2025 real income exists only because BEA interpolated the missing CPI. Their documented link to loan losses is weak (Gross and Souleles found state income not significant; the 2025 FEDS Note found debt-to-income models fit worse), although KeyCorp names "U.S. household income" as a CECL driver for cards, other consumer and mortgage.

### Inferences
- Because BEA revises personal income back several years every September, a saving-rate or real-income "normal level" estimated in August can shift by more than a point a month later; the tool should re-estimate after each annual update and store the vintage it scored against.
- Real disposable income includes transfers, which rose sharply in 2020–21; `W875RX1` (excluding transfers) is the cleaner cyclical income signal, and is the one NBER uses.
- Wage measures answer different questions: ECI is mix-free (fixed basket), AHE is a pool average that can rise in recessions as low-wage workers are laid off, and the Atlanta Fed WGT follows the same people 12 months apart. For household stress, real wage growth (wage growth minus inflation) matters more than nominal, which depends on the CPI — itself missing for October 2025.
- The saving rate is low in absolute terms (4.1%) but its level was just revised up by over a point, which shows why level-based outlier bands on it are fragile.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that tests real disposable income, the saving rate or wage growth as a leading indicator of consumer or commercial loan losses (the repo's earlier search reached the same result).
- BEA state (quarterly) and county (annual) personal income exist under BEA Regional accounts but their history, lag and 2026 revisions were not checked this run.
- The ECI history start for the private wages-and-salaries index at BLS (pre-2001 on the older basis) and its BLS series id were not verified; only FRED `ECIWAG` (2001+) and the 1975 first release were confirmed.
- How the Atlanta Fed will handle the October 2026 tracker (no October 2025 base for 12-month matches) was not stated on its page as of 8 Oct 2026.
- BEA's revised July 2026 saving rate before the update (3.0%) was taken from a search summary, not the opened release.

## Which indicators have a documented recession or loan-loss-leading record, and how strong

### Takeaway
Only unemployment has a documented, official link to loan performance: the Fed's 2026 stress-test models use state unemployment (previous quarter, plus its year-over-year change) for cards and quarterly unemployment changes as "the key macroeconomic specification" for other retail, a 2025 FEDS Note puts it at about +45 bp of card delinquency per point of unemployment, and every large-bank 10-K read (KeyCorp, JPMorgan, Citi, PNC, BofA, U.S. Bancorp) names it as a top CECL driver. It is coincident-to-lagging for recessions, though: the Sahm rule fires on average about three months after a recession starts. Claims, temp help, hours, WARN notices and the vacancy-unemployment combination have documented recession or labor-market leading records from the Fed, BLS or peer-reviewed work, but no published test links them directly to loan losses; and in 2020 both the unemployment and the claims links to card losses broke (losses fell as unemployment hit 14.8%).

### Cited Findings

#### Recession-leading or coincident records of the labor series (what is published)
- Sahm rule: triggered "within three months of the start of the past six recessions" on average (1970–2019, real-time data); "gives little advance warning". — [Sahm (2019), Hamilton Project](https://www.hamiltonproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Sahm_web_20190506.pdf)
- Michaillat–Saez (vacancies + unemployment): average detection delay 1.2 months (max 3) vs 2.7 (max 7) for Sahm; all 15 recessions since 1929 without false positives. — [Oxford Bulletin of Economics and Statistics (2025)](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~saez/michaillat-saezOBES25.pdf)
- Initial claims: one of ten LEI components; St. Louis Fed finds a claims threshold signalled the 1974 downturn months before the 400k rule, but post-1984 the threshold is "more informative ... during expansionary rather than recessionary periods" and in COVID claims crossed only in Feb 2021. — [Conference Board](https://www.conference-board.org/topics/us-leading-indicators); [St. Louis Fed](https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2025/jan/are-initial-jobless-claims-useful-gauge-labor-market-conditions)
- WARN layoffs lead state initial claims, unemployment-rate changes and job separations, and improve pseudo real-time unemployment forecasts (Cleveland Fed working paper — not peer-reviewed in the version read). — [Cleveland Fed WP 20-03R](https://www.clevelandfed.org/publications/working-paper/2022/wp-2003r-advance-layoff-notices-and-aggregate-job-loss)
- Temporary help: "Since 1990, peaks and troughs in temporary help services employment generally have led those of total nonfarm employment" (BLS MLR, descriptive, three recessions). — [BLS MLR April 2011](https://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/2011/04/art6full.pdf)
- Average weekly hours in manufacturing: an LEI component (Conference Board), i.e. a long-standing but proprietary-index-based leading claim; no separate test read this run. — [Conference Board](https://www.conference-board.org/topics/us-leading-indicators)
- NBER uses payroll employment, household-survey employment and real personal income less transfers to date recessions — these are coincident by construction. — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/research/business-cycle-dating)
- Market-contact view (May 2026 Fed FSR survey of 20 contacts): "Labor market weakness" was one of the cited risks, and respondents raised that "widespread adoption of AI may contribute to labor market weakness". — [Federal Reserve, Financial Stability Report, May 2026 (PDF)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/financial-stability-report-20260508.pdf)

- **Federal Reserve supervisory stress-test models (2026 documentation, "October 2025, Updated January 2026"):** credit card PD model uses the **state-level unemployment rate in the previous quarter** and its year-over-year change: "The previous quarter's value is used to reflect that there is generally a lag between economic changes and consumer responses, as borrowers may be able to rely on savings or temporary budget cuts before eventually defaulting." Charge-card model uses state unemployment in the same quarter plus its YoY change. Auto LGD: "The national unemployment rate enters the model". For the domestic Other Retail models, "quarterly changes in the unemployment rate" are "the key macroeconomic specification" (alternatives considered: YoY change; gap to NAIRU). Home equity PD: "House prices, unemployment rate, and various ..." — [Federal Reserve Board, Credit Risk Models (2026 Supervisory Stress Test)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/files/credit-risk-models.pdf)
- **FEDS Note, Barnes, Bopst and Driscoll (28 Feb 2025):** linear model of card delinquency 2000Q1–2019Q4 (adj. R² .97) on prime rate, unemployment, real revolving credit, SLOOS card tightening (lagged 4 quarters), nonprime balance share (lagged 4 quarters): "A 100 basis point increase in the unemployment rate is associated with about a 45 basis point increase in the delinquency rate." But: "The spike in the unemployment rate at the beginning of the COVID-19 pandemic leads the model to far over-predict the level of delinquency rates", and over 2003Q1–2024Q3 the unemployment contribution was "little changed" — the post-2022 rise came from credit availability to riskier borrowers and real debt. Alternatives using debt-to-disposable-income "tend to work less well". The authors note "the model is not causally estimated". — [FEDS Notes, Predicting Credit Card Delinquency Rates](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/predicting-credit-card-delinquency-rates-20250228.html)

### Inferences
- Strength ranking for a lending narrative, from the evidence above: (1) unemployment rate, national and state — official models and every CECL disclosure read; (2) change in unemployment (quarterly or year-over-year) — the transformation the Fed's models and the repo's test both prefer over the level; (3) initial/continuing claims — strong recession/labor-market timing record, loss link only in this repo's own correlation; (4) JOLTS layoffs, WARN, temp help, hours — leading for jobs, no loss evidence; (5) income and saving — used by some banks (KeyCorp) but no published loss-leading test.
- Because unemployment is coincident with recessions and losses lag unemployment by roughly two to four quarters (Fed card model's one-quarter lag; repo's best fit at four quarters), the labor lane gives a lending risk team about a year of lead on card losses from the point unemployment turns — but little or no lead on the recession itself. Claims and the Michaillat–Saez combination add a few months.
- Commercial lending: banks name U.S. unemployment as a key MEV for wholesale/C&I/CRE (JPMorgan, KeyCorp), but the commercial evidence found here is model-usage, not a published lead-lag test; the business-conditions researcher's strand should carry the commercial case.
- 2020 shows the labor-to-loss link is conditional on income support; a narrative tool should say when transfers (gap between `DSPIC96` and `W875RX1`) are unusually large.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or official study was found linking initial claims, continuing claims, JOLTS layoffs/quits, temp help, hours, WARN notices or Challenger announcements directly to bank loan delinquencies or charge-offs; only this repo's own correlations (claims vs card losses six quarters later, 0.66 to 2019).
- Rating-agency use (Moody's, S&P, Fitch macro assumptions) was not researched in this strand.
- Evidence that JOLTS quits or openings lead recessions beyond the Michaillat–Saez vacancy indicator was not found in a primary source this run.
- The commercial-loan (C&I, CRE) side of the unemployment link was not tested against a published study.

## Published outlier / trigger rules already in use for these series, and who published them

### Takeaway
Four published, numeric rules apply directly to this strand: the Sahm rule (0.50 pp rise in the 3-month average unemployment rate over its prior-12-month low; Claudia Sahm, Hamilton Project 2019; FRED hosts real-time and current versions), the Michaillat–Saez rule (0.29 pp on the minimum of the Sahm-style unemployment and vacancy indicators, 0.81 pp for "certain"; Oxford Bulletin 2025), the statutory Extended Benefits triggers on the insured unemployment rate (5% and 120% of prior two years; optional 6% IUR or 6.5% TUR and 110%), and the Conference Board's LEI 3Ds rule (diffusion ≤50 and six-month annualized LEI growth below −4.3%, licensed index). Claims have only a rule of thumb (400,000) whose best-fitting level the St. Louis Fed shows drifting over time. All are change-based or relative rules, which suits a "normal level vs outlier" tool better than fixed levels.

### Cited Findings
- Sahm rule: 0.50 pp rise in 3-month average U-3 over the minimum 3-month average of the prior 12 months (FRED SAHMCURRENT / SAHMREALTIME notes, source Claudia Sahm). — [FRED SAHMCURRENT](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SAHMCURRENT)
- Original publication: Claudia Sahm, "Direct Stimulus Payments to Individuals", The Hamilton Project / Brookings, May 2019. The rule was designed as a **trigger for automatic stimulus payments**, not as a forecasting model: payments "when the three-month average national unemployment rate rises by at least 0.50 percentage points relative to its low in the previous 12 months." Rationale for a change rather than a level: "this type of trigger accommodates changes over time in the natural rate of unemployment." Record claimed (real-time BLS data 1969–2019): "Early in each recession since 1970, the unemployment rate rose at least a 0.50 percentage points ... On average, payments would have been triggered within three months of the start of the past six recessions" (four months after the 2008–09 recession began, two months after 2001's). Self-stated limits: it "gives little advance warning of recessions", and "the rise in unemployment prior to a recession does not predict the severity of the recession" (2001 and 2008–09 had similar pre-recession rises). — [Hamilton Project, Sahm (2019) PDF](https://www.hamiltonproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Sahm_web_20190506.pdf); [paper page](https://www.hamiltonproject.org/publication/paper/direct-stimulus-payments-to-individuals/)
- **Real-time Sahm trigger history (computed this run from FRED `SAHMREALTIME` CSV, 14 Oct 2026):** months at or above 0.50 began 1959-12, 1960-08, 1969-10 (one month at exactly 0.50), 1970-02, 1974-03 (one month at 0.50), 1974-07, 1976-11 (one month at 0.50), 1980-04, 1981-11, 1990-11, 2001-06, 2008-04, 2020-04, and **2024-07 to 2024-09 (peak 0.57)**. The FRED CSV leaves October 2025 blank. The latest NBER chronology entry is the February 2020 peak / April 2020 trough, i.e. NBER has not dated a recession after 2020 — so the 2024 signal is, as of Oct 2026, a false positive. Reading the episodes against NBER peaks (my inference, not a published scoring): 1969-10 came two months before the Dec 1969 peak and 1974-03 inside the Nov 1973–Mar 1975 recession, so both are early/true touches; **1976-11 (one month at exactly 0.50) had no recession** and is a false positive; the first FRED observation, 1959-12, is already ≥0.50 and lapsed before the Apr 1960 peak (cause not checked). So in real time the rule has two clear false positives (1976 single month, 2024) out of ~14 episodes, and the 1976 one sits exactly on the threshold. — [FRED SAHMREALTIME CSV](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=SAHMREALTIME); [NBER, US Business Cycle Expansions and Contractions](https://www.nber.org/research/data/us-business-cycle-expansions-and-contractions)

Not verified on a primary page this run. — [Benzinga, Aug 2024](https://benzinga.com/news/24/08/40389835/claudia-sahm-stands-by-sahm-rule-amid-criticism-from-jerome-powell-and-others-on-its-economic-signif); [Britannica Money](https://britannica.com/money/sahm-rule-recession-indicator)
- **Michaillat–Saez rule (academic, peer-reviewed 2025):** the minimum of the Sahm unemployment indicator and an analogous vacancy indicator (3-month average vacancy rate's decline from its 12-month high); "we then set the recession threshold to 0.29 percentage points (pp) ... This is the lowest threshold that does not produce false positives"; values 0.29–0.81 pp signal a probable recession, above 0.81 pp a certain one; "average delay of 1.2 months instead of 2.7 months, and a maximum delay of 3 months instead of 7 months" vs Sahm; "identifies all 15 recessions since 1929 without false positives, whereas the Sahm rule breaks down before 1960" (three false positives 1929–59). Uses JOLTS openings (vacancy rate) plus historical vacancy proxies. — [Michaillat and Saez, "Has the Recession Started?", Oxford Bulletin of Economics and Statistics (2025), author PDF](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~saez/michaillat-saezOBES25.pdf); earlier working paper with 0.3/0.8 pp thresholds: [arXiv 2408.05856](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05856v2)
- **Initial-claims threshold (St. Louis Fed, Jan 2025 blog revisiting Kliesen, McCracken and Zheng 2011):** the business-economist rule of thumb is 400,000; a rolling 10-year threshold regression of next-month payroll growth on claims finds the "optimal" threshold "varies significantly over time, though on average it is close to 400,000": ~306,090 average Jan 1958–Dec 1983, ~434,165 after 1984. In 1974 claims crossed the optimal threshold in June vs March 1975 for the 400k rule; in the 1990s recession claims rose above 400k but not above the optimal threshold; in COVID they crossed only in Feb 2021. "Since 1984, the optimal threshold seems more informative for gauging labor market conditions during expansionary rather than recessionary periods." Note: claims now run ~200k (197,000 for the week ending 3 Oct 2026), so fixed-level thresholds calibrated on older labor-force sizes need scaling — inference. — [St. Louis Fed, On the Economy, "Are Initial Jobless Claims a Useful Gauge of Labor Market Conditions?"](https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2025/jan/are-initial-jobless-claims-useful-gauge-labor-market-conditions)
- **Conference Board LEI "3Ds" rule (licensed index):** signals an impending recession when the six-month diffusion index is at or below 50 and the LEI's six-month annualized growth falls below −4.3%. Two of its ten components are labor series used here: average weekly hours in manufacturing and average weekly initial UI claims. August 2026: "no recession signal was triggered". The LEI itself is proprietary (®; history sold through The Conference Board's data products) — licence terms were not readable in a scripted fetch. — [The Conference Board, US Leading Indicators](https://www.conference-board.org/topics/us-leading-indicators)
- NBER's own monthly inputs for dating peaks/troughs include "real personal income less transfers, nonfarm payroll employment, employment as measured by the household survey, real personal consumption expenditures, manufacturing and trade sales adjusted for price changes, and industrial production" with "no fixed rule". — [NBER, Business Cycle Dating](https://www.nber.org/research/business-cycle-dating)

### Inferences
- The Sahm and Michaillat–Saez rules are themselves "normal level vs outlier" constructions (distance from a trailing 12-month extreme), so the tool can reproduce them exactly and use them as validated reference points; both thresholds were chosen as the lowest values without historical false positives, so they are calibrated on 6–15 recessions and will have an inherent in-sample optimism.
- The real-time Sahm record (computed here) shows false positives in 1976 (one month at exactly 0.50) and 2024 (0.50–0.57 for three months) — both shallow, short crossings — so a persistence or margin requirement (e.g., two consecutive months, or a value well above 0.50) would have removed them; this is an inference, not a published rule.
- The EB trigger is the only threshold written into law for these series and DOL publishes it weekly per state; with zero states "on" (Oct 2026) it is a stress-only signal, useful as a state-level red line rather than an early warning.
- Fixed-level claims thresholds (400k) are stale relative to today's ~200k level; ratio-to-trailing-low rules generalise better (inference).

### Gaps
- No primary DOL page was read that states the EB 5%/120% and 6.5%/110% trigger numbers (confirmed only via the 2009 Federal Register and secondary pages); the governing statute/regulation (20 CFR part 615) was not opened.
- No official, published outlier rule was found for JOLTS layoffs, temp help, average weekly hours, WARN counts, the saving rate, real disposable income or wage growth.
- The current (2026) value of the Michaillat–Saez indicator was not found from the authors and was not computed here (their vacancy rate is vacancies over labor force, which differs from the JOLTS openings rate).
- Sahm's own 2024 assessment and Powell's comments were confirmed only through news coverage, not primary transcripts.
