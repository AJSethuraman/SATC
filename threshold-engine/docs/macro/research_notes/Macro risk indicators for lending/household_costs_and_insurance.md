## Home insurance: what public series exist, what they measure, and the evidence linking premiums to mortgage stress

### Takeaway
There is no free, monthly, public series that measures what US homeowners actually pay for insurance. The BLS CPI "tenants' and household insurance" index prices renters' policies only and rose ~5% from 2019 to 2024 while mortgage-escrow data show homeowners premiums up ~62%; the best free monthly proxy is the BLS PPI for homeowner's insurance premiums (PCU9241269241262 / PCU5241265241262, from June 1998), which tracks insurer pricing rather than household outlay. Causal evidence that premium increases raise mortgage (and credit card) delinquency exists (Dallas Fed WP 2505, 2025), but it rests on licensed ICE McDash escrow data, not on any public series.

### Cited Findings

**A. BLS CPI — Tenants' and household insurance (item SEHD)**
- Series id CUUR0000SEHD: "Tenants' and household insurance in U.S. city average, all urban consumers, not seasonally adjusted"; base period December 1997=100; monthly from December 1997 (first value 100.0, Dec 1997). A seasonally adjusted version CUSR0000SEHD does not exist ("ERROR: Invalid Series ID: CUSR0000SEHD"), and a South-region version CUUR0300SEHD also returns "Invalid Series ID" — i.e. this item is published only as a US city average, NSA. — [BLS data, CUUR0000SEHD](https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/CUUR0000SEHD); [BLS, CUSR0000SEHD invalid](https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/CUSR0000SEHD)
- Latest values: Sep 2025 169.935; Oct 2025 missing (lapse in appropriations); Nov 2025 170.366; Dec 2025 172.129; Jan 2026 171.940 … Sep 2026 175.093. 2024 annual path roughly 157–159. — [BLS data, CUUR0000SEHD](https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/CUUR0000SEHD)
- What it measures (BLS factsheet, last modified 20 May 2026; read from BLS's blsmon1.bls.gov mirror because www.bls.gov returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch): "The tenants' and household insurance index includes contents coverage insurance for tenants of rental housing and households ... In keeping with the scope of the index, only renters' insurance policies are sampled and priced." "Therefore, insurance on physical damage to structures and liability coverage included in homeowners' policies as well as insurance on commercial properties are excluded from the index." Published at the U.S. city average level only. — [BLS factsheet: Tenants' and household insurance](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/factsheets/tenants-household-insurance.htm)
- Weight: relative importance December 2025 = 0.292 of CPI-U (0.296 CPI-W); shelter 35.625. **Methodology change:** "Since January 2025, information and data from the National Association of Insurance Commissioners are used to calculate a factor applied to the total spending by homeowners on homeowner's insurance to derive the portion of insurance premium that accounts for contents coverage" (the weight change, not the price measurement). — [BLS factsheet](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/factsheets/tenants-household-insurance.htm); [BLS relative importance, Dec 2025](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/tables/relative-importance/2025.htm)
- Pricing: carriers sampled from a national insurance trade organisation's universe; specific policies held at constant characteristics; "pricing is spread out over three separate pricing periods covering the entire collection month"; once a year the contents coverage amount is inflated by the regional inflation rate or a company factor, and the resulting premium changes enter the index; "reduced sample rotation schedule". — [BLS factsheet](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/factsheets/tenants-household-insurance.htm)
- Dallas Fed (Jones, Taylor and Tzur-Ilan, 9 Apr 2026): "The CPI tenants and homeowners insurance component is measured using premiums for renters insurance policies, which primarily cover personal belongings"; "The tenant and household insurance component of CPI increased by just 5 percent" 2019–2024, versus 62% for homeowners premiums in ICE McDash and 35% for the PCE "net household insurance" component. — [Dallas Fed, "Measures of inflation misalign with pricier home insurance"](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2026/0409)
- PCE "net household insurance" (BEA) = premiums minus expected losses (long-run average), weighted to personal belongings and liability; it "made up just 0.1 percent of overall PCE consumption in 2024"; PCE gross premiums rose 64% 2019–24 while insurer losses doubled. — [Dallas Fed, 9 Apr 2026](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2026/0409)

**D. Mortgage-escrow-based premium data (licensed) and research linking premiums to loan performance**
- ICE McDash "captures actual homeowner insurance payments for mortgagees, covering roughly two-thirds of the U.S. mortgage market"; average insured mortgagor paid about $2,300 in 2024, ~2.7% of median household income (≈2% pre-pandemic); premiums +62% nationally 2019–2024; +73% TX, +78% FL, +84% ID, +90% UT; US reinsurance rates +107% 2019–2024 and "have begun decreasing"; Dallas Fed expects premium growth "likely to ease through year-end 2026". ICE McDash is a commercial (licensed) dataset. — [Dallas Fed, 9 Apr 2026](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2026/0409)
- Dallas Fed WP 2505 (Ge, Johnson, Tzur-Ilan, 16 Jan 2025), "Climate Risk, Insurance Premiums and the Effects on Mortgage and Credit Outcomes": 6.7 million borrowers; data from CoreLogic, ICE McDash (servicing + Property Insurance Module) and Equifax CRISM; outcome is 30+ days delinquent within 12 months of policy renewal, renewals July 2022–June 2023, ZIP fixed effects plus instrumental variables. "When premiums increase by one standard deviation, the probability of delinquency rises by 0.6 percentage points, representing a 16% increase relative to the mean probability (3.7%)"; larger for high-DTI/high-LTV borrowers; "three times larger for non-jumbo compared to jumbo"; present in GSE, bank-held and private-label loans; higher premiums "significantly raise the probability of credit card delinquency and worsen borrowers' creditworthiness"; prepayment effect mostly relocation. — [Dallas Fed WP 2505 landing page](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/papers/2025/wp2505); [WP 2505 PDF](https://www.dallasfed.org/-/media/documents/research/papers/2025/wp2505.pdf)
- A widely repeated figure — "for every $500 in annual increased homeowners insurance cost, a borrower is 20 percent more likely to become delinquent" — comes from advocacy-group summaries (Public Citizen / Americans for Financial Reform) and was not found in the WP 2505 January 2025 text read here; treat as unverified secondary. — [Public Citizen](https://www.citizen.org/article/rising-property-insurance-premiums-the-uneven-risks-to-household-and-systemic-financial-stability/); [Americans for Financial Reform](https://ourfinancialsecurity.org/resources/propertyinsurancepremiums2025report/)
- Chicago Fed WP 2026-01 (Meisenzahl, Polacek, Ramnath, Yudhishthu, Jan 2026): "increases in property insurance premiums reduce mortgage originations. The effect is strongest for the rate refinancing and cash-out refinancing segments"; denials linked to premium increases are more often attributed to high DTI and insufficient collateral; concentrated among highly levered borrowers; may "attenuate the refinancing channel of monetary policy". — [Chicago Fed WP 2026-01](https://www.chicagofed.org/-/media/publications/working-papers/2026/wp2026-01.pdf)
- Keys and Mulder, NBER WP 32579 (June 2024, revised Nov 2025): "over 74 million premiums from 2014–2024 inferred from mortgage escrow payments"; "a doubling of the pass-through from disaster risk into premiums"; premium increases capitalised into home values, "reducing home price growth by over $40,000 in the most exposed zipcodes". — [NBER w32579](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32579)
- Philadelphia Fed (Zhang and Aglar, dated May 2026 on the PDF), "The Rising Burden of Homeowners Insurance in the Third District States", using ICE McDash: real average premium Dec 2021→Jun 2025 +28.9% PA, +26.1% NJ, +25.4% DE; LMI neighbourhoods saw larger increases in premium per $1,000 coverage; "The larger increase in insurance premiums is positively correlated with a higher likelihood of mortgage delinquency across all three states, although this correlation does not necessarily represent a causal relationship." — [Philadelphia Fed brief (PDF)](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/FRBP/Assets/Community-Development/Briefs/homeowners_insurance_brief.pdf); [summary page](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/community-development/housing-and-neighborhoods/rising-burden-homeowners-insurance)
- Cotality (formerly CoreLogic; commercial, licensed) on escrow: trade-press coverage (4 Mar 2026) says escrow payments since 2019 are up 77% in Colorado and 70% in Florida, and 45% nationally over five years, and links fast-rising escrows to delinquency increases (correlational). The Cotality report itself was not retrieved. — [The MortgagePoint, 4 Mar 2026](https://themortgagepoint.com/2026/03/04/cotality-fast-rising-escrows-leading-to-delinquency-spikes/)
- NY Fed Liberty Street (Jung and Jung, Apr 2026), on 2021 ICE McDash + CoreLogic contracts: median coverage limit = 77% of recovery value; median deductible ≈0.3% of recovery value; "Measures such as premium divided by coverage limit are often used as proxies for the price of insurance. However, because coverage limits rarely bind, such measures may not be informative about the effective price of insurance." A caution for any premium-per-$1,000-coverage indicator. — [Liberty Street Economics, Apr 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/04/what-millions-of-homeowners-insurance-contracts-reveal-about-risk-sharing/)

**E. State-level and other premium sources**
- NAIC homeowners report (annual, state-level, ~3-year lag): search results report the 2022-data edition released 21 May 2025, with the nationwide average dwelling fire + homeowners owner-occupied premium up 10.5% 2021→2022 and HO-3 up 11.26% (HO-4 renters +0.6%). content.naic.org returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch, so the release text, licence/price terms and state tables were NOT verified. — [NAIC release, 2022 data](https://content.naic.org/article/naic-releases-homeowners-insurance-report-2022) (unverified)
- The FIO/NAIC PCMI ZIP-level data (2018–2022) are the only free sub-state premium data found (see C). The Census ACS also collects homeowners' insurance costs annually (Census: the ACS "also provides data on insurance costs, property taxes, mortgage and utilities expenses"). — [Census, 11 Sep 2025](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2025/09/condo-hoa-fees.html)

### Inferences
- For a model that rates "where a series stands today", the PPI homeowner's premium index is the only free, monthly, long (June 1998–) national series that moves with homeowners premiums. Its September step pattern means month-on-month changes are mostly noise outside September; a year-over-year transform is the natural input. This is my reading of the data pages, not a BLS statement about methodology.
- The CPI SEHD index should be presented in the narrative as a renters'-insurance index; using it as "home insurance prices" would understate the 2019–2024 shock by an order of magnitude (5% vs 62%).
- FIO/NAIC ZIP data is suited to a static geographic exposure map (which ZIPs/states are high-premium, high-nonrenewal) rather than to a time-series outlier model; it covers 2018–2022 only.

### Gaps
- www.bls.gov returns HTTP 403 to automated fetch; BLS factsheets and the relative-importance table were read from the BLS mirror host blsmon1.bls.gov (same content, "Last Modified Date: May 20, 2026"), and time-series values from data.bls.gov. Links in these notes point to the canonical www.bls.gov paths.
- NAIC homeowners report: whether it is free or sold, its exact title for the latest edition, and its state tables could not be checked (NAIC site blocks automated fetch). Treat as "access terms unknown" until someone opens it.
- No CFPB publication or CFPB/Federal Reserve Board public time series of escrowed insurance payments was found. All escrow-based premium research found uses licensed ICE McDash or Cotality data. State insurance departments (e.g. Florida OIR's QUASR, referenced in the FIO glossary) publish their own data; these were not inventoried.
- Not verified: the BLS methodology reason for the September step in the PPI homeowner's series.
- The "$500 → 20% more likely delinquent" figure could not be traced to a page of WP 2505; a later revision of the paper may contain it.
- The ACS homeowners-insurance cost table id was not identified in this session.

## Auto costs: motor vehicle insurance, repair, new and used vehicle prices

### Takeaway
Auto-cost pressure is well covered by free, monthly, long BLS series: motor vehicle insurance (SETE, NSA from 1947, SA from 1985), maintenance and repair (SETD), new vehicles (SETA01) and used cars and trucks (SETA02), plus the PPI for private passenger auto insurance (PCU5241265241261, from June 1998). Motor vehicle insurance CPI rose ~13% from January 2024 to December 2025 and then fell ~5% from April to September 2026; repair costs are still climbing. The Manheim wholesale used-vehicle index is free to view and download from Cox Automotive but carries no published redistribution licence — treat it as licensed. Fed research ties auto delinquency to vehicle prices through loan size and monthly payment, not directly to the CPI.

**Evidence linking auto costs to loan performance**
- FEDS Note (Adams, Bord, Sannoh, 26 Sep 2024): the auto delinquency rise is "mainly concentrated in auto loans originated since 2022"; "larger auto loan amount at origination, rather than the increases in interest rates, explain most of the increase in delinquencies"; borrowers "demanded larger loans amid the run-up in car prices from mid-2020 to mid-2023"; average required monthly payment rose from $470 to about $600 (Jan 2020 → Jan 2023). Data: NY Fed CCP/Equifax and Experian AutoCount (both non-public). — [FEDS Note, 26 Sep 2024](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/rising-auto-loan-delinquencies-and-high-monthly-payments-20240926.html)
- FEDS Note (Adams, Barnes, Bopst, Sommer, 24 Nov 2025): card and auto delinquency flattened into 2025:Q3 but "auto loan delinquencies picked up for lower-income households in the third quarter"; "auto prices remain elevated and will likely continue to weigh on auto loan performance"; names "inflation, unemployment, leverage, interest rates, or lending conditions" as the macro drivers. — [FEDS Note, 24 Nov 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/a-note-on-recent-dynamics-of-consumer-delinquency-rates-20251124.html)

### Inferences
- The motor vehicle insurance CPI's annual model-year update (October/November) coincides with the October–November 2025 data hole; a year-over-year change computed across late 2025 needs care.
- Vehicle prices matter to auto credit mainly at origination (loan size → payment) and through collateral value (severity). I found no public paper tying the CPI motor vehicle insurance index itself to delinquency; that link is plausible (it raises the all-in monthly cost of car ownership) but undocumented in what I read.

### Gaps
- No published study found that uses the CPI motor vehicle insurance or repair index as a predictor of auto delinquency.
- Manheim licence terms not found; the Cox rebasing PDF and the BLS 2022 new-vehicle notice were summarised by search, not opened.

## Shelter, utilities, gasoline, food and headline/core CPI

### Takeaway
All of these are free, monthly, long-history BLS CPI series (plus EIA weekly/monthly energy prices), so they suit a normal-level/outlier engine well; the main hazards are the October 2025 hole, the 2022 new-vehicle and 2025 tenants-insurance weight methodology changes, and the BLS New Tenant Rent Index, which is a quarterly prototype that, as of 14 October 2026, had not been updated past 2025q3. In the year to September 2026 the standout household cost shock is gasoline (+34% CPI, SA), with electricity (+4.4%) and piped gas (+5.0%) above headline (+3.7%); rent (+2.8%) and food at home (+1.8%) are below it (my calculations from BLS index levels).

### Cited Findings

**Access routes common to the BLS series**
- BLS Public Data API: Version 2 "requires registration" (free key, renewed yearly); registered users "up to 500 queries daily", "up to 50 series per query", "up to 20 years per query"; unregistered: 25 queries/day, 25 series/query, 10 years/query. ## Property tax and HOA costs

### Takeaway
There is no monthly public price index for property taxes or HOA dues (the CPI's owners'-equivalent-rent approach leaves both out of scope). Public options are coarse: Census's Quarterly Summary of State and Local Government Tax Revenue (local property-tax collections, quarterly since 1962, national and state) and annual American Community Survey tables (median real estate taxes paid, B25103; condo/HOA fees, new in the 2024 ACS). The escrow-shock evidence that ties property taxes plus insurance to mortgage delinquency is from Cotality (licensed) and is correlational.

### Cited Findings
- Census Quarterly Summary of State and Local Government Tax Revenue (QTax): "quarterly estimates of property tax revenue and non-property tax revenue for local governments ... as well as quarterly estimates of tax revenue for state governments"; "Data have been collected quarterly since 1962, however information was not released for the 3rd quarter 1992 through the 2nd quarter 1993" (a gap; aggregates later published); national and state detail; tax classification "is entirely the responsibility of the Census Bureau". These are government collections, not a per-household bill. — [Census, About QTax](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/qtax/about.html)
- ACS table B25103 "Mortgage Status by Median Real Estate Taxes Paid (Dollars)" (B25103_001E total, _002E/_003E by mortgage status) exists in the 2024 ACS 1-year API; annual; geography down to counties/places (1-year: areas of 65,000+). — [Census API, ACS 2024 1-year B25103](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs1/groups/B25103.json)
- ACS table B25070 "Gross Rent as a Percentage of Household Income in the Past 12 Months" (B25070_001E–_011E, top bracket "50.0 percent or more") exists in the 2024 ACS 1-year API — the standard public rent-burden measure. — [Census API, ACS 2024 1-year B25070](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs1/groups/B25070.json)
- HOA fees: "The ACS has long asked about condo fees. But the 2024 ACS 1-year estimates include information on condo and homeowners who paid HOA fees, too." In 2024, 21.6 million of 86.6 million owned households paid condo or HOA fees; national median $135/month ($120 with a mortgage, $184 without); ~3 million paid over $500/month; 64% of payers in New York paid over $500. The ACS "also provides data on insurance costs, property taxes, mortgage and utilities expenses". Released 11 Sep 2025 for reference year 2024 (≈9-month lag). New in 2024, so there is no HOA time series before 2024 in the ACS. — [Census, "Condo or Homeowners Association Fees Topped $500 Monthly for About 3 Million Households", 11 Sep 2025](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2025/09/condo-hoa-fees.html)
- American Housing Survey HOA averages ($243/month in 2023, $191 in 2021, $170 in 2019) circulate in secondary articles; not verified against a Census AHS table. — search summary only; see Gaps
- Cotality (licensed): on ~8 million non-escrowed mortgages, the share delinquent on property taxes rose to 5.1% in 2024 from 4.5% a year earlier, below the 2012–2025 average of 5.5%; property taxes owed up 27% nationally since 2019 (trade-press summary). A separate Cotality 2026 property tax delinquency report puts year-to-date 2026 at 5.2% vs a 2012–2025 average of 5.4% (search summary). Escrow increases since 2019: Colorado +77%, Florida +70%, national +45% over five years; Cotality says states with the largest escrow increases "often exhibited higher delinquency rates, though this isn't always the case". — [National Mortgage News](https://www.nationalmortgagenews.com/news/property-tax-delinquencies-jump-as-home-prices-rise); [The MortgagePoint, 4 Mar 2026](https://themortgagepoint.com/2026/03/04/cotality-fast-rising-escrows-leading-to-delinquency-spikes/); [Cotality 2026 property tax delinquency report](https://www.cotality.com/resources/2026-property-tax-delinquency-report) (not opened)

### Inferences
- For a time-series engine, QTax local property-tax collections (quarterly, state-level) is the only public property-tax series with long history; it measures government revenue, so it lags assessments and mixes base growth with rate changes. ACS B25103 and the ACS HOA fee are better as annual geographic cross-sections.
- Property tax and HOA pressure enters the narrative mainly through escrow (tax + insurance) payment shock on existing mortgagors; the public data cannot measure that shock directly — it requires servicer/escrow data (ICE McDash, Cotality), which are licensed.

### Gaps
- No public monthly/quarterly series of property tax bills per household found; ATTOM and Cotality publish property-tax reports but are commercial (licence terms not checked).
- AHS HOA figures unverified; the AHS is biennial.
- Peer-reviewed or Federal Reserve research isolating property-tax increases as a cause of mortgage delinquency was not found in this pass (the Dallas Fed WP 2505 isolates insurance).
- The QTax API/download route and its release lag were not verified in this session.

## Climate and catastrophe exposure as a cost driver (FEMA, NOAA, NFIP)

### Takeaway
NOAA's Billion-Dollar Weather and Climate Disasters database was retired in May 2025 (no NOAA updates past 2024); Climate Central has continued it with the same method and lead scientist, and its page showed 447 events and over $3.3 trillion (2026 dollars) as of September 2026 — now a non-government source with terms to check. FEMA's OpenFEMA API (no key needed) serves disaster declarations from 1953 and NFIP redacted policies and claims, refreshed through 2026. These are exposure and loss-event series; the documented loan-performance channel runs through physical damage and uninsured losses (Harvey studies) and through insurance premiums (Dallas Fed WP 2505), not through declaration counts themselves.

### Cited Findings

**NOAA NCEI Billion-Dollar Weather and Climate Disasters — DISCONTINUED by NOAA (May 2025)**
- NOAA said NCEI would no longer update the database beyond 2024 and that its data back to 1980 would be archived, citing "evolving priorities, statutory mandates, and staffing changes"; CNN reported the database relied on non-public data shared by private industry, making it hard to replicate. — [CNN, 8 May 2025](https://cnn.com/2025/05/08/climate/noaa-ends-disaster-database); [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/noaa-stops-tracking-climate-disasters-billion-dollar-database/748107/)
- The NCEI page still exists with the citation "NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information (NCEI) U.S. Billion-Dollar Weather and Climate Disasters (2025) ... DOI: 10.25921/stkw-7w73". — [NCEI Billion-Dollar Disasters](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/billions/)

**Climate Central continuation (non-government)**
- "Continuing NOAA's dataset. Climate Central now stewards it with the same methodology and lead scientist, Adam Smith"; events since 1980 with damages ≥ $1 billion, CPI-adjusted to 2026 dollars; "As of September 2026, the U.S. has sustained 447 such events, with a total cost exceeding $3.3 trillion"; state and region filters marked "Coming Soon"; year-to-date cumulative cost series by month (e.g. 2025: $63.00bn in January, $109.96bn by June). — [Climate Central, U.S. Billion-Dollar Weather and Climate Disasters](https://www.climatecentral.org/climate-services/billion-dollar-disasters)
- Press coverage: Climate Central's first report counted 23 billion-dollar disasters in 2025 totalling $115 billion, and Climate Central is expanding to events of $100 million+ from 1980. — [NBC Palm Springs, 8 Jan 2026](https://www.nbcpalmsprings.com/2026/01/08/private-sector-revives-climate-disaster-database-after-federal-tracking-was-halted); [KUNR, 3 Nov 2025](https://www.kunr.org/local-stories/2025-11-03/climate-central-revives-the-billon-dollar-disaster-database-project)

**FEMA OpenFEMA (free, no API key)**
- Disaster Declarations Summaries (v2): "all federally declared disasters ... beginning with the first disaster declaration in 1953", three types (major disaster, emergency, fire management); "county not available before 1964"; 70,567 records; dataset last refreshed 14 Oct 2026. — [OpenFEMA DataSets API metadata](https://www.fema.gov/api/open/v1/DataSets?$filter=name%20eq%20%27DisasterDeclarationsSummaries%27)
- My count from the OpenFEMA v2 API (declarationType 'DR', distinct disaster numbers): 100 major disaster declarations in 2024, 45 in 2025, 57 in 2026 through 7 Oct 2026. Queried without a key. — [OpenFEMA DisasterDeclarationsSummaries v2](https://www.fema.gov/api/open/v2/DisasterDeclarationsSummaries)
- NFIP: "FIMA NFIP Redacted Policies" (v2, 73,601,802 records, dataset refresh 1 Jun 2026) and "FIMA NFIP Redacted Claims" (v2, 2,721,780 records, dataset refresh 1 Jun 2026); NFIP created by the National Flood Insurance Act of 1968, run by FEMA since 1979, Write Your Own programme since 1983. — [OpenFEMA DataSets API metadata](https://www.fema.gov/api/open/v1/DataSets?$filter=name%20eq%20%27FimaNfipPolicies%27%20or%20name%20eq%20%27FimaNfipClaims%27)
- www.fema.gov HTML data pages returned "Access Denied" to automated fetch; the API endpoints worked. — (observation in this session)

**Disasters, insurance coverage and mortgage performance (documented)**
- Kousky, Palim and Pan (Fannie Mae, Hurricane Harvey): loans on moderately-to-severely damaged homes were more likely to reach 90 days delinquent soon after the storm; where flood insurance was not required (and few had it), greater damage raised the odds of modification and of 180+ day delinquency or default within two years; where it was required, damage raised prepayment. — [Fannie Mae, Flood Damage and Mortgage Credit Risk: Hurricane Harvey](https://www.fanniemae.com/research-and-insights/publications/flood-damage-mortgage-credit-risk-hurricane-harvey) (search summary; PDF not opened)
- Mota and Palim (Fannie Mae, 2024) using insurance claims as damage proxy: short-term delinquency and forbearance rose with damage. — [Fannie Mae working paper](https://fanniemae.com/media/document/pdf/mortgage-performance-hurricane-harvey.pdf) (search summary)
- OCC working paper: Harvey raised the 180-day delinquency rate by about 20 basis points per quarter over five quarters. — [OCC working paper, hurricanes and residential mortgage loan performance](https://occ.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/economics/working-papers-banking-perf-reg/pub-econ-working-paper-hurricanes-residential-mort-loan-perf.pdf) (search summary)
- FIO: top-climate-risk ZIP quintile paid 82% more in premiums and faced ~80% higher nonrenewal rates than the lowest quintile (2018–2022). — [FIO report via CFA](https://consumerfed.org/news/blogs/download-analyses-of-us-homeowners-insurance-markets-2018-2022-climate-related-risks-and-other-factors-report-and-data-from-federal-insurance-office-fio/)
- Keys and Mulder: pass-through from disaster risk into premiums doubled; premium increases capitalised into home values (> $40,000 lower price growth in most exposed ZIPs) — a collateral channel. — [NBER w32579](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32579)

### Inferences
- Declaration counts are administrative decisions as well as hazard measures (the 2024→2025 drop from 100 to 45 may reflect policy as much as weather); for a hazard signal, the Climate Central cost series or NFIP claims are cleaner. I did not research declaration policy changes.
- NFIP claims (by state/county and date of loss) can be aggregated into a monthly flood-loss series for a bank footprint; NFIP policy counts and premiums can show take-up and pricing (NFIP's Risk Rating 2.0 repricing from 2021 is a known break — not verified in this session).
- Because the Billion-Dollar series is now private, its licence must be checked before it is embedded in a bank tool; the archived NOAA 1980–2024 file is the public-domain fallback.

### Gaps
- Climate Central's data licence/terms of use were not found on the page read.
- Whether the archived NOAA 1980–2024 billion-dollar data remain downloadable from NCEI was not confirmed (page is script-rendered).
- NFIP Risk Rating 2.0 (pricing methodology change) not verified here.
- OpenFEMA API rate limits/page size not verified on the documentation page (fema.gov HTML blocked); $top=10000 worked.

## Which indicators have documented links to delinquency or default, and which are only plausible

### Takeaway
Only one household-cost indicator in this strand has causal, peer-style evidence of raising delinquency: homeowners insurance premiums (Dallas Fed WP 2505: mortgage and credit-card delinquency), measured on licensed escrow data; the free proxy (PPI homeowner's insurance) has no published delinquency study of its own. Vehicle prices have a documented route through loan size and monthly payment to auto delinquency (FEDS 2024). Disaster damage with no insurance has documented mortgage-default effects (Hurricane Harvey studies). Everything else — CPI motor vehicle insurance, repair, rent, utilities, gasoline, food, property tax, HOA, FEMA declaration counts — is plausible as budget pressure but, in what I found, undocumented or only correlational (trade-group or vendor statements). Headline CPI is used by the Federal Reserve as a stress-scenario variable.

### Cited Findings

**Who uses these in a macro/risk narrative**
- Federal Reserve supervisory stress test scenarios (Feb 2026) include, among the domestic variables, quarterly percent changes (annualised) in "the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI)"; the 2026 baseline has CPI inflation easing from 2.8% at end-2025 to 2.2% by 2028:Q1. — [Federal Reserve, 2026 Stress Test Scenarios](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/2026-final-supervisory-stress-test-scenarios-20260204.pdf)
- Federal Reserve Board staff (FEDS Notes) use vehicle prices/loan size to explain auto delinquency and name "inflation, unemployment, leverage, interest rates, or lending conditions" as drivers of consumer delinquency. — [FEDS Note, 26 Sep 2024](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/rising-auto-loan-delinquencies-and-high-monthly-payments-20240926.html); [FEDS Note, 24 Nov 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/a-note-on-recent-dynamics-of-consumer-delinquency-rates-20251124.html)
- Regional Feds (Dallas, Chicago, Philadelphia, New York) and Treasury FIO use homeowners insurance cost and availability as a household and financial-stability risk. — [Dallas Fed, 9 Apr 2026](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2026/0409); [Chicago Fed WP 2026-01](https://www.chicagofed.org/-/media/publications/working-papers/2026/wp2026-01.pdf); [Philadelphia Fed brief](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/FRBP/Assets/Community-Development/Briefs/homeowners_insurance_brief.pdf); [FIO report via CFA](https://consumerfed.org/news/blogs/download-analyses-of-us-homeowners-insurance-markets-2018-2022-climate-related-risks-and-other-factors-report-and-data-from-federal-insurance-office-fio/)
- Gasoline and card delinquency: the American Bankers Association's chief economist attributed record card delinquency in 2005 partly to gasoline costs and credited falling gas prices with improving delinquency in 2014 — trade-group commentary, not a controlled study. — [American Banker](https://americanbanker.com/news/falling-gas-prices-help-consumers-pay-their-debts-aba-says); [NBC News, 2005](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna9514391)

**Evidence tiers (each tier statement cites the evidence behind it)**
- DOCUMENTED, causal design — homeowners insurance premium increase → higher 30+ day mortgage delinquency (+0.6pp per 1 s.d., 16% of mean), stronger for high DTI/LTV and non-jumbo; → higher credit-card delinquency and worse credit scores. — [Dallas Fed WP 2505](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/papers/2025/wp2505)
- DOCUMENTED — premium increases → fewer refinance and cash-out originations; denials tied to DTI and collateral. — [Chicago Fed WP 2026-01](https://www.chicagofed.org/-/media/publications/working-papers/2026/wp2026-01.pdf)
- DOCUMENTED — premium increases capitalised into lower home-price growth in exposed ZIPs (collateral/LGD channel). — [NBER w32579](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32579)
- DOCUMENTED, mechanism — vehicle price run-up → larger loans → higher payments → higher auto delinquency for 2022+ vintages. — [FEDS Note, 26 Sep 2024](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/rising-auto-loan-delinquencies-and-high-monthly-payments-20240926.html)
- DOCUMENTED — flood damage without required insurance → more modification and 180+ day delinquency/default within two years (Harvey). — [Fannie Mae](https://www.fanniemae.com/research-and-insights/publications/flood-damage-mortgage-credit-risk-hurricane-harvey); [OCC working paper](https://occ.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/economics/working-papers-banking-perf-reg/pub-econ-working-paper-hurricanes-residential-mort-loan-perf.pdf)
- CORRELATIONAL only — premium increases and delinquency in PA/NJ/DE ("does not necessarily represent a causal relationship"); escrow (tax + insurance) increases and state delinquency (Cotality, vendor). — [Philadelphia Fed brief](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/FRBP/Assets/Community-Development/Briefs/homeowners_insurance_brief.pdf); [The MortgagePoint](https://themortgagepoint.com/2026/03/04/cotality-fast-rising-escrows-leading-to-delinquency-spikes/)
- CORRELATIONAL / anecdotal — gasoline prices and card delinquency (ABA). — [American Banker](https://americanbanker.com/news/falling-gas-prices-help-consumers-pay-their-debts-aba-says)

### Inferences
- For a bank's macro narrative, the defensible story is: insurance premiums (PPI proxy, with Dallas Fed/ICE evidence quoted), vehicle prices (CPI used/new + FEDS evidence), and gasoline (EIA weekly, very current) — each with its evidence tier stated. Utilities, rent and food belong in the narrative as "budget pressure" context with tier P, not as proven loss drivers.
- Because the strongest evidence uses licensed escrow data, a public-data tool can show the price signal but cannot reproduce the delinquency elasticity; the narrative should cite the elasticity from the paper, not estimate one.
- Commercial lending relevance: PPI commercial multiple peril (PCU5241265241265), commercial auto (PCU5241265241263) and the Dallas Fed's note that commercial insurance rose ~75% 2019–2024 ([Dallas Fed](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2026/0409)) are the insurance cost lines for CRE and business borrowers; I found no delinquency study on them.

### Gaps
- No public study found linking CPI utilities, food at home, rent CPI, motor vehicle insurance CPI, property tax or HOA fees to loan delinquency. Absence in my search is not proof that none exists.
- Census Household Pulse energy-bill hardship series (secondary sources cite ~19–24% of households unable to pay an energy bill in 2021–2024) — not verified against Census tables; the survey's current status after 2024 was not checked. — search summary only ([NEADA](https://neada.org/pulsesurvey/))
- CFPB: no CFPB research on household budgets and insurance was found in this pass (searches returned only Treasury/vendor material).
- PPI September release status (the September 2026 PPI was not on the BLS page on 14 Oct 2026) — whether this reflects schedule or delay was not checked.
