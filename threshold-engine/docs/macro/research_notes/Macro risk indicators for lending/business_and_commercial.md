# US business and commercial-sector conditions indicators for a commercial-lending macro risk narrative

_Researched 2026-10-14. Scope: business and commercial conditions only (C&I, CRE, small business), the conditions outside a bank's control. Labour/income, household costs, household balance sheets/housing, and regulators' general use of macro data are covered by other researchers._

_How the FRED facts were obtained: each FRED series page and its keyless `https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=<ID>` download were fetched on 2026-10-14. "From" and "latest" are the first and last observations in that download; "updated" is the FRED page stamp. FRED prints one of three copyright classes on each page, read off the page: **"Public Domain: Citation Requested"** (BEA, Census, BLS, EIA, Fed Board series), **"Copyrighted: Citation Required"** (regional Feds, St. Louis Fed calculated spreads, Moody's-derived, CBOE, IMF, NY Fed SOFR), **"Copyrighted: Pre-Approval Required"** (ICE BofA). A series id below exists only if its FRED page resolved; two guessed ids that 404'd are listed under Gaps._

## Activity: industrial production and capacity utilisation, GDP and corporate profits, durable goods, regional Fed surveys, ISM and S&P PMIs

### Takeaway
Every core activity series a commercial narrative needs is free and public-domain on FRED with long histories (IP from 1919, capacity utilisation from 1967, GDP and profits from 1947, core capex orders from 1992, Philly Fed survey from 1968). The two best-known PMIs are not: ISM pulled all its series from FRED in 2016 and its terms forbid redistributing any portion without written permission, and S&P Global PMI is a paid product; the free regional Fed manufacturing surveys are the substitute.

### Inferences
- For a free, redistributable activity block, the natural set is INDPRO/TCU (monthly), NEWORDER (monthly, the investment-intent signal that matters most for C&I demand), the three regional Fed surveys (timeliest — same month), CFNAIMA3 (a ready-made activity composite; the Chicago Fed's reading of its zero line was not re-verified in this research), and GDPC1 plus CPROFIT (quarterly). ISM and S&P PMI can be cited in prose from their free press releases but should not be stored or charted in a shared tool without a licence.
- CPROFIT (economic profits, with IVA/CCAdj) is the right profits measure for a credit narrative because it strips inventory-price and tax-depreciation effects. That is a judgement and not tested here.
- NEWORDER and DGORDER are nominal. Anyone measuring a "normal level" over 1992–2026 should deflate them (e.g. with a PPI for capital equipment) or use growth rates; otherwise the 2021–23 inflation reads as strength.

### Gaps
- The ISM terms and the FRED 2016 removal notice were verified only through search-result text; direct fetches failed (JavaScript page; connection refused).
- No licence terms were found for S&P Global PMI; treat it as licensed.
- The FRED ids for the regional **services** surveys (Philadelphia Nonmanufacturing, NY Services, Dallas TSSOS) and the Richmond and Kansas City manufacturing surveys were not verified. The guessed Empire id GACDINA066MSFRBNY returned 404; the working id is GACDISA066MSFRBNY.
- Release dates for the advance durable-goods report and the BEA GDP and profits estimates were not read from the publishers' calendars; the lags above are inferred from FRED update stamps.

## Financial conditions: corporate bond spreads, Baa–Treasury, NFCI, STLFSI, KCFSI, excess bond premium, VIX, term spread, NY Fed recession probability

### Takeaway
The biggest change: **since April 2026 FRED serves only a rolling 3 years of every ICE BofA spread series** (HY, IG and BBB all now start 2023-10-16), and ICE's licence bars redistribution. A full-cycle normal level for corporate spreads must come from another source. The free substitutes are Moody's Baa–10y (BAA10Y from 1986; BAA from 1919, but Moody's licence text also bars redistribution), the Fed's own GZ spread and EBP CSV (public Fed data, 1973 onward, monthly), and the composite indexes NFCI (1971), STLFSI4 (1993) and KCFSI (1990). The term spread and the NY Fed recession probability are free and long.

### Inferences
- For a tool that must find a normal level and outliers over full cycles, ICE OAS on FRED is now unusable as a history source. Use the GZ spread from the Fed's EBP CSV (public Fed data, 1973+, nonfinancial corporate bonds) as the main corporate-spread series, and keep ICE only as a "current reading" tile that is never stored or redistributed.
- A free long-history Baa spread can be built as BAA − GS10 (monthly, from 1953-04). However, Moody's licence text on BAA and BAA10Y bars "STORED FOR SUBSEQUENT USE … REDISTRIBUTED", so stored Moody's-derived values belong in the same legal-review bucket as ICE. The GZ spread avoids the problem.
- The EBP and its probability revise historically every month, so the tool should version each download (vintage) rather than overwrite it, or the "where it stood when we rated it" record will drift.
- NFCI, ANFCI and STLFSI4 are already standardised (0 = average), so "normal level" is built into their definition. Their sample means are full-sample, so a new observation can shift the scale slightly.

### Gaps
- The exact date in April 2026 when FRED's ICE truncation began, and whether ICE sells full history on reasonable terms, were not established.
- No publisher page was found stating the NY Fed spreadsheet's file name, or that the NY Fed's 3-month bill rate is on a bond-equivalent basis. The PDF defines the spread only as "10 yr bond rate-3 month bill rate".
- The KC Fed's own KCFSI page did not return content to a fetch; facts come from FRED's notes.

## Credit conditions: SLOOS C&I and CRE standards and demand, H.8 loan growth, C&I loan rates

### Takeaway
SLOOS C&I standards and demand (1990/1991 onward) and H.8 C&I loans (1947 monthly, 1973 weekly) are free, public-domain and long. SLOOS **CRE** standards and demand by property type start only in **2013-Q4**, so their "normal level" rests on one partial cycle. The Fed's C&I loan-rate survey (E.2) was **discontinued in 2017**; the free business-loan rate left is the prime rate, plus the KC Fed small-business survey. The Fed's Data Download Program is being retired, so FRED becomes the access route.

### Cited Findings
- **SLOOS** — survey of "up to eighty large domestic banks and twenty-four U.S. branches and agencies of foreign banks", conducted "generally" quarterly, with results timed for the January/February, April/May, August and October/November FOMC meetings. Questions cover "changes in the standards and terms of the banks' lending and the state of business and household demand for loans". — [Fed SLOOS page](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/sloos.htm)
- **C&I standards:** **DRTSCILM** (net % tightening, loans to large and middle-market firms; quarterly from 1990-Q2; July 2026 survey = 0.0) and **DRTSCIS** (small firms; from 1990-Q2; 1.8). **C&I demand:** **DRSDCILM** (net % reporting stronger demand, large/middle-market; from 1991-Q4; 16.1) and **DRSDCIS** (small firms; 3.6). Updated 3 Aug 2026; public domain. — [FRED DRTSCILM](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DRTSCILM), [FRED DRTSCIS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DRTSCIS), [FRED DRSDCILM](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DRSDCILM), [FRED DRSDCIS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DRSDCIS)
- **CRE standards by type, from 2013-Q4 only (52 observations):** **SUBLPDRCSC** construction and land development (July 2026 = −3.7), **SUBLPDRCSN** nonfarm nonresidential (−11.3), **SUBLPDRCSM** multifamily (−5.7). **CRE demand:** **SUBLPDRCDC** (−11.1), **SUBLPDRCDN** (1.9), **SUBLPDRCDM** (−3.8). Negative standards = net easing. Public domain. — [FRED SUBLPDRCSC](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SUBLPDRCSC), [FRED SUBLPDRCSN](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SUBLPDRCSN), [FRED SUBLPDRCSM](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SUBLPDRCSM), [FRED SUBLPDRCDC](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SUBLPDRCDC), [FRED SUBLPDRCDN](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SUBLPDRCDN), [FRED SUBLPDRCDM](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SUBLPDRCDM)

- **H.8** — "an estimated weekly aggregated balance sheet for all commercial banks in the United States", "typically available to the public by 4:15 p.m. each Friday". Percent changes "have been adjusted to remove the effects of nonbank structure activity of $5 billion or more, as well as the effects of accounting rule changes, such as … FAS 166/167 and … CECL". Seasonal factors are updated once a year. Thrifts and credit unions are excluded. — [Fed H.8 About](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h8/about.htm)
- **C&I loans, all commercial banks:** **BUSLOANS** (monthly SA from 1947-01; September 2026 = $2,959.0bn) and **TOTCI** (weekly SA from 1973-01-03; 2026-09-30 = $2,970.9bn). **CRE loans, all commercial banks:** **CREACBM027SBOG** (monthly SA from 2004-06; $3,137.0bn) and **CREACBW027SBOG** (weekly SA from 2004-06-02). Updated 9 Oct 2026; public domain. — [FRED BUSLOANS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BUSLOANS), [FRED TOTCI](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/TOTCI), [FRED CREACBM027SBOG](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CREACBM027SBOG), [FRED CREACBW027SBOG](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CREACBW027SBOG)

### Inferences
- The CRE SLOOS series' 2013-Q4 start means no observation covers the 2007–10 CRE bust. A "normal level" from them will be biased toward benign conditions; flag them as short-history in any rating.

## Which indicators have a documented record of leading defaults or recessions, and how strong; which are only plausible

### Takeaway
Three signals have strong, peer-reviewed or Fed-published leading records for **recessions and output**:
- **The term spread (10y–3m)**: Estrella–Mishkin; the NY Fed model.
- **The excess bond premium**: Gilchrist–Zakrajšek (2012); a monthly Fed recession probability.
- **SLOOS lending standards**: Lown–Morgan (2006); Bassett et al. (2014).

**None of the sources read here directly tests leading of commercial loan *charge-offs*.** One Atlanta Fed paper finds SLOOS answers only "marginally" predict banking-sector performance. The financial-stress indexes, PMIs, regional surveys, BFS, NFIB and bankruptcy counts are, on the evidence found, coincident or plausible rather than proven leaders of commercial losses.

### Cited Findings
- **Term spread — strong for recessions.** Estrella & Mishkin (NY Fed, 1996): the 10-year/3-month spread "significantly outperforms other financial and macroeconomic indicators in predicting recessions two to six quarters ahead". Their probit (data 1960-Q1 onward) implies a four-quarters-ahead recession probability of **10% when the spread averages 0.76 pp, 50% at −0.82 pp and 90% at −2.40 pp**. Example: −2.18 pp in 1981-Q1 implied 86.5%, and 1982-Q1 was a recession. — [Estrella & Mishkin, "The Yield Curve as a Predictor of U.S. Recessions," NY Fed Current Issues 2(7), 1996 (PDF)](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/current_issues/ci2-7.pdf)
- The NY Fed runs this model monthly (parameters estimated January 1959 – December 2009). Its October 2026 reading is 12.76% for September 2027. — [NY Fed Prob_Rec.pdf](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/capital_markets/Prob_Rec.pdf)
- **Excess bond premium — strong for activity.** Gilchrist & Zakrajšek, *AER* 102(4), 2012, pp. 1692–1720: their credit spread index is "compared with the standard default-risk indicators—a considerably more powerful predictor of economic activity"; "the predictive content of credit spreads is due primarily to movements in the excess bond premium"; shocks to the EBP "lead to significant declines in economic activity and equity prices". A rise in the EBP "represents a reduction in the effective risk-bearing capacity of the financial sector and, as a result, a contraction in the supply of credit". — [NBER WP 17021 abstract](https://www.nber.org/papers/w17021)
- The Fed publishes the EBP-based 12-month recession probability monthly. It was 0.091 in September 2026, and the series peaked at 0.9997 in October 2008 (this researcher's read of the CSV). — [FEDS Note (Oct 2016)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/updating-the-recession-risk-and-the-excess-bond-premium-20161006.htm), [EBP CSV](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/ebp_csv.csv)
- **SLOOS standards — documented for loans and output.** Lown & Morgan (*JMCB* 38(6), 2006; working-paper abstract): "shocks to lending standards are significantly correlated with innovations in commercial loans of banks and in real output. Credit standards strongly dominate loan rates in explaining variation in business loans and output. Standards remain significant when we include various proxies for loan demand". The estimated impact of a moderate tightening on inventory investment "is of the same order of magnitude as the decline in inventory investment over the typical recession". — [RePEc: Lown & Morgan, "The Credit Cycle and the Business Cycle: New Findings Using the Loan Officer Opinion Survey"](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hhs/sifrwp/0027.html)
- Bassett, Chosak, Driscoll & Zakrajšek (FEDS 2012-24; later *Journal of Monetary Economics* 2014 — journal citation not verified here) build a bank-level SLOOS credit-supply indicator adjusted for demand. "Tightening shocks to this credit supply indicator lead to a substantial decline in output and the capacity of businesses and households to borrow from banks, as well as to a widening of credit spreads and an easing of monetary policy." — [Fed FEDS 2012-24 abstract](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2012/201224/201224abs.html)
- **Counter-evidence on SLOOS and bank outcomes.** An Atlanta Fed working paper (2006-24, "The predictive power of the Senior Loan Officer Survey: do lending officers know anything special?") finds "sector-specific survey results are relevant for predicting real activity in those sectors but, strangely, that the informative power of the survey results only marginally extend to various measures of performance in the banking sector." — [RePEc: Atlanta Fed WP 2006-24](https://ideas.repec.org/p/fip/fedawp/2006-24.html)
- **NFCI — the publisher's claim, not a tested lead on losses.** The Chicago Fed says the nonfinancial leverage subindex "best exemplifies how leverage can serve as an early warning signal for financial stress and its potential impact on economic growth", through a "financial accelerator" feedback in which "tighter financial conditions are associated with rising risk premiums and declining asset values". — [Chicago Fed: About the NFCI](https://www.chicagofed.org/research/data/nfci/about)
- **Supervisory use as a revealed preference.** The Fed's stress test drives modelled bank losses with these business and commercial variables: real GDP, BBB corporate yield (ICE C4A4), prime rate, Dow Jones Total Stock Market Index, the Z.1 CRE price index (FL075035503.Q), VIX (quarterly max), Treasury yields, and others. The severely adverse paths are guided by stated ranges (e.g. CRE prices −30% to −45%, trough in quarters 8–10). — [Fed 2026 Stress Test Scenarios (PDF)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/2026-final-supervisory-stress-test-scenarios-20260204.pdf)
- **Regulators' narrative practice (current-state, not lead-tested):** the FSR, the FDIC Risk Review and the OCC SRP all frame business and CRE risk using spreads vs historical medians, the EBP, SLOOS, ICRs, leveraged-loan defaults, CRE prices, vacancy, cap rates, CMBS delinquency, NFIB and bankruptcy filings. See the citations in the sections above.

### Inferences
Evidence ranking, as a working classification by this researcher from the sources above:

| Tier | Indicator | Evidence found | Lead target shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strong (published, quantified) | Term spread T10Y3M / NY Fed probability | Estrella–Mishkin 1996; NY Fed model | Recessions 2–6 quarters ahead |
| Strong | EBP / GZ spread (Fed CSV) | Gilchrist–Zakrajšek 2012; Fed monthly probability | Activity, equity prices; recessions 12 months ahead |

| Coincident stress gauges | NFCI/ANFCI, STLFSI4, KCFSI, VIX | Publisher descriptions only | Not verified here |
| Coincident/lagging outcomes | Bankruptcies, CMBS delinquency, CORBLACBS, DRCRELEXFACBS | Outcome measures | n/a (these are what is being predicted) |
| Plausible only | Regional Fed surveys, CFNAI, NEWORDER, BFS, BTOS, NFIB, PPI and import prices, EPU, tariffs | No lead-on-losses evidence found | Not verified |

- The literature is about predicting **recessions and output**. The step from a recession to C&I and CRE charge-offs is a second link. A tool that rates "where it stands today" can lean on the first link with citations; any claim that an indicator leads *losses* by N quarters needs the bank's own backtest against CORBLACBS / CORCREXFACBS (both free from 1985/1991).
- The term spread's record includes the 2022–24 inversion, which this research did not evaluate. The NY Fed model's parameters stop at 2009, so its most recent miss or hit is not reflected in the fit.

### Gaps
- No published study read here quantifies how many quarters SLOOS, EBP or the term spread lead **C&I or CRE charge-off rates**. This is the key missing evidence for a loss-focused narrative.
- Lown & Morgan were verified through the working-paper abstract (RePEc), not the 2006 JMCB text. The JME 2014 citation for Bassett et al. was not verified.
- No primary evidence was gathered on the leading record of the NFCI, STLFSI4 or KCFSI for defaults. The Chicago Fed cites "Monitoring Financial Stability: A Financial Conditions Index Approach" and "Detecting Early Signs of Financial Instability", but these were not read.
- How the 2022–24 term-spread inversion affects the indicator's credibility was not researched. (The May 2026 FSR's recession-shading notes list NBER recessions only through "February 2020–April 2020", consistent with no NBER recession following that inversion as of May 2026 — [Fed FSR May 2026, Borrowing](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-may-financial-stability-report-borrowing.htm).)
