# Household balance sheets, credit conditions, consumer sentiment and housing: US public indicators for a consumer-lending macro risk narrative

_Researcher notes, compiled 14 Oct 2026. Scope: household debt and delinquency (market-wide, not bank Call Report rates), consumer credit volume and price, bank lending standards (SLOOS), consumer sentiment and expectations, and housing. Labour/income, household costs and insurance, business conditions and regulators' use of macro data are covered by other researchers._

_Verification method: every FRED id named here was checked on 2026-10-14 by downloading `https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=<ID>` (no API key needed for that CSV route) and reading the first and last observation; "first obs"/"last obs" are what that download returned. FRED's documented API (`api.stlouisfed.org`) needs a free key; I did not test it. Where a publisher page could not be fetched from this machine and only a search summary was available, the line says so. Federal Reserve Board pages say the Data Download Program is being retired: "During the week of November 9, the 'Build Your Package' feature in the Data Download Program (DDP) will be removed in preparation for the eventual retirement of the DDP"; users are pointed to FRED — [Fed G.19 page](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/default.htm). So any Fed series should be pulled from FRED or the release's XML/CSV files, not DDP packages._

## Household debt service, delinquency and transitions, bankruptcy, and student loans (DSR/FOR, NY Fed HHDC, US Courts, student loans)

### Takeaway
The Fed's Debt Service Ratio still exists but was **rebuilt in 2024 on credit-bureau data and now starts only in 2005** (FRED `TDSP`/`MDSP`/`CDSP` carry the new series); the **Financial Obligations Ratio was discontinued after 2023:Q3**. The NY Fed Household Debt and Credit report is free, machine-readable Excel, market-wide by product, with **flow (transition) rates into 30+ and 90+ delinquency** from 2003 — but its state tables cover only 11 states, and for cards the NY Fed itself now says the 90+ *stock* overstates distress, so the *flow* series is the one to monitor. Bankruptcy is free from US Courts (quarterly, PDF, by district and county) and fast but licensed from Epiq AACER. Student-loan delinquency jumped from under 1% to ~8% of balances 90+ in 2025:Q1 when reporting resumed and sat at 10.6% in 2026:Q2, a series with an administrative hole from 2020:Q2 to 2024:Q4.

### Cited Findings
#### Household Debt Service Ratio (DSR) — Federal Reserve Board. METHODOLOGY BREAK in 2024: the series a user downloads today is a different series from the one most published research used.
- What it measures: required household debt payments (principal and interest; the new version also includes mortgage escrow) as a percent of disposable personal income; split into Mortgage DSR and Consumer DSR, which sum to the total; seasonally adjusted, quarterly — [Fed DSR release, 22 Sep 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/dsr/default.htm)
- Current release: "Release date: September 22, 2026", data through 2026:Q2: total DSR 11.11, mortgage 5.83, consumer 5.28. Pre-pandemic 2019:Q4 was 11.73 / 5.92 / 5.81; the 2007:Q4 peak in the new series was 15.85 — [Fed DSR release](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/dsr/default.htm)
- Break: "Starting with the 2024:Q2 Debt Service Ratio (DSR) publication, the Board will transition to a new, credit bureau data-based methodology ... An archived version of the old DSR series will continue to be hosted at .../releases/housedebt/default.htm, but will no longer be updated." — [Fed archived DSR/FOR page](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/housedebt/default.htm)
- New method uses the FRBNY/Equifax Consumer Credit Panel (5% sample of adults with a credit record), summing reported monthly scheduled payments per tradeline; "Coverage of the full range of tradeline types in the CCP only begins in 2005, so the new method lacks the long history of the old version"; the new series includes escrow (taxes/insurance) in mortgage payments, so its level "is also consistently higher than that from the old DSR"; the old DSR did not account for pandemic student-loan forbearance — [Ringo, FEDS Notes, 4 Sep 2024](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/introducing-a-credit-bureau-based-measure-of-u-s-household-debt-service-20240904.html)
- The old (archived) DSR ran quarterly from 1980:Q1 (10.61 in 1980:Q1; peak 13.29 in 2007:Q4) and assumed card minimum payments of 2.5% of balance per month — [Fed archived DSR/FOR page](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/housedebt/default.htm); [About the (old) release](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/housedebt/about.htm)
- FRED ids (release rid=89): `TDSP` (total), `MDSP` (mortgage), `CDSP` (consumer); on FRED these now carry the NEW series, first obs 2005-01-01, last obs 2026-04-01 (=2026:Q2) — [FRED TDSP](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/TDSP), [FRED release 89](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/release?rid=89). Anyone with a pre-2024 TDSP download starting in 1980 holds the old series; do not splice without a level adjustment (inference from the level gap documented by Ringo, above).
- Access: Fed page, XML zip (`/releases/dsr/data/FRB_dsr_xml.zip`), Data Download Program, FRED. The Fed announces that "During the week of November 9, the 'Build Your Package' feature in the Data Download Program (DDP) will be removed in preparation for the eventual retirement of the DDP"; users are pointed to FRED — [Fed DSR release](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/dsr/default.htm). Free; Fed data are public. Note the new DSR is derived from Equifax CCP microdata, but only the aggregate is published (licensing of the aggregate: no restriction stated on the release page — not verified further).
- Release lag: 2026:Q2 published 22 Sep 2026, i.e. roughly one quarter (~12 weeks) after quarter-end — [Fed DSR release](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/dsr/default.htm)

#### Financial Obligations Ratio (FOR) — DISCONTINUED.
- "The final data update for the Financial Obligations Ratios (FOR) statistical release is 2023:Q3. The Board decided to stop publishing the FOR due to a lack of high-quality data on property tax and homeowners' insurance payments." The Board suggests BEA rental-of-tenant-occupied-housing data for users who want to add rent — [Fed archived DSR/FOR page](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/housedebt/default.htm)
- FOR was broader than DSR: added rent on tenant-occupied property, auto lease payments, homeowners' insurance and property tax — [About the release](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/housedebt/about.htm)
- FRED `FODSP`: first obs 1980-01-01, last obs 2023-07-01 (=2023:Q3); it will not update — [FRED FODSP](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/FODSP)

#### NY Fed Quarterly Report on Household Debt and Credit (HHDC) — FRBNY Center for Microeconomic Data, built on the NY Fed Consumer Credit Panel/Equifax. Free, machine-readable (Excel), market-wide (all lenders, all products), with state detail for 11 states only.
- What it measures: balances, originations, credit limits, delinquency stocks and FLOWS (transitions) by product (mortgage, HELOC, auto, credit card, student loan, other), new foreclosures and bankruptcies (consumers with a new notation on their credit report), by age and for selected states — workbook table of contents of [HHD_C_Report_2026Q2.xlsx](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/interactives/householdcredit/data/xls/HHD_C_Report_2026Q2.xlsx) (downloaded 2026-10-14; every data sheet is labelled "Source: New York Fed Consumer Credit Panel/Equifax")
- Tables in that workbook (sheet titles verbatim): "Percent of Balance 90+ Days Delinquent by Loan Type"; "Flow into Early Delinquency (30+) by Loan Type"; "Flow into Serious Delinquency (90+) by Loan Type"; "Quarterly Transition Rates for Current Mortgage Accounts"; "Quarterly Transition Rates for 30-60 Day Late Mortgage Accounts"; "Number of Consumers with New Foreclosures and Bankruptcies"; "Quarterly Transition into Serious Delinquency (90+) ... by Age" (all, mortgage, auto, card, student); "Percent of Balance 90+ Days Late by State"; "Percent of Mortgage Debt 90+ Days Late by State"; "Quarterly Transition Rates into 30+ Days Late by State"; "Quarterly Transition Rates into 90+ Days Late by State"; "Percent of Consumers with New Foreclosures by State"; "Percent of Consumers with New Bankruptcies by State" — [HHDC Q2 2026 workbook](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/interactives/householdcredit/data/xls/HHD_C_Report_2026Q2.xlsx)
- History: product delinquency and flow tables start 2003:Q1 (e.g. card 90+ share of balance 8.84% in 03:Q1); new-bankruptcy-by-age starts 2000:Q1; a separate "1999-2003 Data" file is offered — same workbook; [HHDC page](https://www.newyorkfed.org/microeconomics/hhdc)
- State coverage in the state tables: AZ, CA, FL, IL, MI, NJ, NV, NY, OH, PA, TX plus a US/ALL line (11 states), quarterly from 2003:Q1 to 2026:Q2 — same workbook. Separate "Statistics by area and year" and "Statistics by county" xlsx files are also posted (annual) — [HHDC page](https://www.newyorkfed.org/microeconomics/hhdc)
- Definitions: state transition rates are "Four Quarter Moving Sum, Rates from Current to 30+ Days Delinquent, All Accounts" and "Rates from Current and up to 60 Days Delinquent to 90+ Days Delinquent" — same workbook. The flow-into-90+ measure is "the balance on loans that became 90+ days past due in the present quarter divided by the balance of loans that were less than 90 days past due in the previous quarter, annualized using a four-quarter moving sum" — [Liberty Street Economics, 11 Aug 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/08/how-distressed-are-consumers-reconciling-diverging-credit-card-delinquency-measures/)
- Latest (Q2 2026, press release 11 Aug 2026): total household debt $18.8tn (-$13bn q/q); 4.7% of debt in some stage of delinquency; card balances $1.26tn; auto $1.71tn; mortgage $13.1tn; HELOC $459bn (17th consecutive quarterly increase); "Student loans remained an exception, with the continued impact of the re-reporting of defaulted student debt causing some distortions in student loan delinquency rates" — [HHDC page, Q2 2026](https://www.newyorkfed.org/microeconomics/hhdc); [press release](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20260811)
- Q2 2026 values from the workbook: 90+ share of balance — mortgage 0.99%, HELOC 0.99%, auto 5.49%, card 12.92%, student 10.6%, all 3.31%; annualized flow into 90+ — auto 3.00%, card 6.97%, mortgage 1.52%, HELOC 1.15%, student 7.83%, all 2.57% — [HHDC Q2 2026 workbook](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/interactives/householdcredit/data/xls/HHD_C_Report_2026Q2.xlsx)
- KNOWN BREAKS / CAVEATS:
  - Stock vs flow divergence in cards: card 90+ stock rose from 7.6% (2022:Q3) to 12.8% (2026:Q1) while the flow rate "has remained relatively stable for almost two years"; NY Fed attributes the stock rise to "a pool of stale, charged-off debts that lenders have been reporting for longer durations" — ~40% of charged-off debts still reported a year later in 2004-2012 vs 80% by 2024. Excluding severely derogatory balances, the stock rate falls in line with the flow rate and the Call Report rate — [Liberty Street Economics, 11 Aug 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/08/how-distressed-are-consumers-reconciling-diverging-credit-card-delinquency-measures/). Implication for an outlier engine: use the FLOW (transition) series, not the 90+ stock, for cards.
  - Mortgage balance dip in Q2 2026 "was due to a temporary gap in the reporting of mortgages on credit reports due to a transfer of servicing" — [HHDC page](https://www.newyorkfed.org/microeconomics/hhdc)
  - Origination credit score switched: "VantageScore 4.0 (2026Q1+); Equifax Riskscore 3.0 (prior)" for mortgage originations; and "2026Q2 report includes a revision to 2026Q1 credit card balances outstanding" — notes in the Q2 2026 workbook
  - Student loans: pandemic pause and on-ramp suppressed reported delinquency 2020-2024 (see the student-loan entry below).
- Access/licensing: free download (PDF report, Excel data, technical notes, data dictionary) from the HHDC page; no key or API. The underlying data are Equifax credit records; the NY Fed posts only aggregates. I did not find explicit redistribution terms for the aggregates on the HHDC page (gap). Not on FRED as a set (I did not find HHDC national delinquency series on FRED; not exhaustively checked).
- Release lag: about 6 weeks after quarter-end (Q2 2026 released 11 Aug 2026) — [press release](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20260811)

#### Student loan delinquency after reporting resumed (2025) — NY Fed HHDC plus ED data.
- Federal payments were paused 43 months (from the start of the pandemic through September 2023); a one-year "on-ramp" kept missed payments off credit reports until it "expired in October 2024 and delinquencies began appearing on credit reports during the first quarter of 2025"; the 90+ share of student debt "surged from below 1 percent to nearly 8 percent" in 2025:Q1 — [Liberty Street Economics, 13 May 2025](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2025/05/student-loan-delinquencies-are-back-and-credit-scores-take-a-tumble/)
- Among borrowers with a payment due, 23.7% were behind in 2025:Q1; state conditional borrower delinquency ranged from 44.6% (Mississippi) to 13.7% (Illinois); more than 2.2 million newly delinquent borrowers saw credit scores fall more than 100 points; 2.4 million newly delinquent had scores above 620 — same post
- Path from the HHDC workbook (student loan 90+ share of balance): 2024:Q4 0.53% → 2025:Q1 7.74% → 2025:Q2 10.16% → 2025:Q3 9.36% → 2025:Q4 9.57% → 2026:Q1 10.34% → 2026:Q2 10.6%. Annualized flow into 90+: 2024:Q4 0.70% → 2025:Q4 16.19% (peak) → 2026:Q2 7.83%. Pre-pandemic 2019:Q4 stock was 11.06% — [HHDC Q2 2026 workbook](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/interactives/householdcredit/data/xls/HHD_C_Report_2026Q2.xlsx)
- Break: the 2020:Q2–2024:Q4 student series is an administrative artifact (pause + on-ramp), and the 2026 numbers are affected by "re-reporting of defaulted student debt" — [HHDC page](https://www.newyorkfed.org/microeconomics/hhdc). Any "normal level" estimate for student delinquency must exclude 2020:Q2–2024:Q4 or treat it as a regime.
- G.19 student loan memo item: "Includes student loans originated under the Federal Family Education Loan Program and the Direct Loan Program; Perkins loans; and private student loans without government guarantees ... Data for this memo item are released for each quarter-end month"; latest value $1,861.8bn (2026:Q2) — [G.19, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/default.htm). On FRED the memo item is now `SLOASM` (quarter-end months, first obs 2006-03, last obs 2026-06); the old id `SLOAS` is marked DISCONTINUED and stops at 2024-10-01 — [FRED SLOASM](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SLOASM); [FRED SLOAS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SLOAS).
- Federal benchmark data on the ED portfolio by loan status are at [studentaid.gov data center](https://studentaid.gov/data-center/student/portfolio) (cited by the G.19 About page).

Tables are PDF (e.g. [F-2, 12 months to 30 Jun 2026](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/bf_f2_0630.2026.pdf)); no API found. Free, US government work.
- Latest: 12 months to 30 Jun 2026 — 608,511 total filings; 581,570 nonbusiness (366,863 Ch.7; 214,153 Ch.13) — [Table F-2, June 2026](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/bf_f2_0630.2026.pdf). - Credit-bureau alternative (free): NY Fed HHDC "Number of Consumers with New Foreclosures and Bankruptcies" (quarterly from 2003:Q1, 136.8 thousand new bankruptcy notations in 2026:Q2 vs 612.26 thousand in 2003:Q1) and "Percent of Consumers with New Bankruptcies by State" (11 states) — [HHDC Q2 2026 workbook](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/interactives/householdcredit/data/xls/HHD_C_Report_2026Q2.xlsx)

- Epiq AACER / ABI: monthly national counts are announced in press releases; the underlying data are a commercial Epiq product (licensing not verified in this session — see Gaps).

### Inferences
- For a "normal level and outliers" engine, the DSR must be treated as **two different series**: the new CCP-based one (2005–) and the archived 1980–2024 one; they differ in level (escrow included in the new one) and the new one has only ~21 years, i.e. one full housing bust (2007–09) and one pandemic distortion (2020–21). Percentile ranks of the new DSR will therefore be dominated by those two episodes.
- The card **flow into 90+** (HHDC "Flow into Serious Delinquency (90+) by Loan Type") is the better stress gauge than the 90+ stock, per the NY Fed's own reconciliation; the stock has a reporting-practice drift (charged-off debts reported for longer) that an outlier engine would misread as stress.
- Student loans should be modelled as a regime-switching series: excluded or flagged for 2020:Q2–2024:Q4 (pause and on-ramp) and 2025–26 (re-reporting of defaulted debt). The 2025:Q4 flow peak (16.2%) is partly mechanical. Its spillover to other products runs through credit scores (2.2m borrowers lost 100+ points), so the right place for it in a narrative is "score migration into subprime", which the May 2026 FSR also notes.
- HHDC's 11-state coverage (AZ, CA, FL, IL, MI, NJ, NV, NY, OH, PA, TX) will not cover most bank footprints; for state/county coverage use FHFA NMDB/CFPB (mortgage), the HHDC area/county annual files, US Courts F-5A (bankruptcy by county) and Equifax county series on FRED.
- Bankruptcy filings rose ~12% y/y on the US Courts 12-month measure (to June 2026) and ~13% for individual Chapter 7 year-to-date on Epiq — a consistent signal of rising household distress from two independent sources, though from a low post-2020 base (the HHDC count of new bankruptcy notations, 136.8k in 2026:Q2, is far below the 612k of 2003:Q1).

## Consumer credit growth, revolving credit, card and personal loan rates (Fed G.19 and related)

### Takeaway
G.19 gives free monthly national consumer credit (total, revolving, nonrevolving) from the 1940s/1968 and quarterly commercial-bank card APRs (from 1994) and 24-month personal-loan rates (from 1972); use **growth rates/flows, not level changes**, because the levels contain documented breaks (2010 FAS 166/167, managed-receivables basis since June 2020, nonfinancial-business sector retired July 2025). The student-loan memo item moved to a new FRED id (`SLOASM`). The Philadelphia Fed's Y-14M large-bank series add card delinquency, charge-off, minimum-payment and utilisation measures (from 2012:Q3) that are not in Call Reports.

### Cited Findings
#### Fed G.19 Consumer Credit — free, monthly, national; FRED release rid=14.
- What: "Covers most credit extended to individuals, excluding loans secured by real estate"; revolving (mostly credit cards, plus prearranged overdraft plans) and nonrevolving (mainly motor vehicle and education loans); levels are "total managed receivables" (on-book plus securitized) since June 2020 — [G.19, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/default.htm); [G.19 About](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/about.htm)
- Latest: "Release Date: October 7, 2026", data for August 2026: total consumer credit +1.9% saar; revolving -4.2% saar; nonrevolving +4.1%; outstanding $5,196.8bn (revolving $1,352.4bn) — [G.19](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/default.htm). "This release is generally issued on the fifth business day of each month" (≈5-week lag) — same page.
- FRED ids (checked 2026-10-14): `TOTALSL` (from 1943-01), `REVOLSL` (from 1968-01), `NONREVSL` (from 1943-01), all last obs 2026-08 — [FRED TOTALSL](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/TOTALSL). Student loan memo item: `SLOASM` (quarter-end months, first obs 2006-03, last 2026-06 = $1,861.8bn) replaces discontinued `SLOAS` (last obs 2024-10) — [FRED SLOASM](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SLOASM); [FRED SLOAS (DISCONTINUED)](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SLOAS)
- Breaks: "The series for consumer credit outstanding and its components may contain breaks that result from discontinuities in source data. Percent changes are adjusted to exclude the effect of such breaks" — use flows/growth rates, not level differences, in a tool — [G.19](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/default.htm). Other documented changes: 2010 FAS 166/167 consolidation of securitized balances; shift to total managed receivables since June 2020; "In July 2025, nonfinancial business was retired as a sector due to greatly diminished balances and discontinued source data" — [G.19 About](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/about.htm). The finance-company new-car-loan terms in previous releases "are discontinued" (remain in DDP) — [G.19](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/default.htm)
- Use in risk narratives: the Fed's DSR uses G.19 balances as input (old method) — [About the old DSR](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/housedebt/about.htm). Credit growth itself is a cycle/volume indicator, not a stress indicator; rapid revolving growth plus rising rates raises payment burden (inference).

#### G.19 "Terms of Credit": credit-card and personal-loan rates at commercial banks — free; QUARTERLY (mid-quarter month) even though FRED stores them on a monthly calendar.
- Definitions: new-car and personal-loan rates "are simple unweighted averages of each bank's most common rate charged during the first calendar week of the middle month of each quarter"; card "all accounts" = stated APR averaged across all card accounts at reporting banks; "accounts assessed interest" = annualized finance charges / average daily balances on accounts that were charged interest — [G.19 footnotes](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/default.htm)
- Latest (Aug 2026): card APR all accounts 21.19%; accounts assessed interest 22.36%; 24-month personal loan 11.90%; 60-month new car 7.54%; 72-month new car 7.17% — [G.19](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/default.htm)
- FRED ids (checked): `TERMCBCCALLNS` (card, all accounts; from 1994-11), `TERMCBCCINTNS` (accounts assessed interest; from 1994-11), `TERMCBPER24NS` (24-month personal loan; from 1972-02), `RIFLPBCIANM60NM` (60-month new car; from 2006-08), `TERMCBAUTO48NS` (48-month new car; from 1972-02, still populated on FRED to 2026-08 though the current G.19 table shows only 60- and 72-month) — all last obs 2026-08.
- Gap: no free, official, market-wide series for fintech/nonbank personal-loan rates was found; the G.19 24-month bank rate is the only long official personal-loan price series identified.

#### Philadelphia Fed "FR Y-14M Large Bank Credit Card and Mortgage Data" — free, quarterly from 2012:Q3, on FRED. (Large-bank supervisory loan-level data aggregated; covers the big card issuers, so it is a market-wide card measure rather than any one bank's Call Report.)
- FRED ids (checked 2026-10-14; first obs 2012-07, last obs 2026-04 = 2026:Q2): `RCCCBBALDPD30P` (share of card balances 30+ DPD; 2.99% in 2026:Q2), `RCCCBBALDPD60P`, `RCCCBBALDPD90P`, `RCCCBACTDPD30P`/`RCCCBACTDPD90P` (account-based), `RCCCBNCOPCT` (net charge-off rate; 5.01%), `RCCCBSHRMIN` ("Share of Accounts Making the Minimum Payment"; 10.15% in 2026:Q2 vs 8.76% in 2012:Q3), `RCCCBSHRFULL` (share paying in full), `RCCCBACTIVEUTILPCT75` (utilization), `RCCCBSCOREPCT25`/`PCT50` (current score percentiles), `RCCCBBALTOT`; mortgage: `RCMFLBBALDPDPCT30P`/`60P`/`90P` (incl. foreclosures; 90+ = 0.50% in 2026:Q2), origination LTV/DTI percentiles — FRED notes: "Source: Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia Release: FR Y-14M Large Bank Credit Card and Mortgage Data ... Frequency: Quarterly, End of Quarter" — [FRED RCCCBBALDPD30P](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/RCCCBBALDPD30P); [FRED RCCCBSHRMIN](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/RCCCBSHRMIN)
- Why it matters for stress: the minimum-payment share is a direct "running out of room" measure absent from Call Reports (inference). Caveat: covers only banks filing FR Y-14M (large BHCs), so it excludes smaller issuers and nonbanks (inference from the release name; scope threshold not verified here).


### Inferences
- Revolving credit fell at a 4.2% annual rate in August 2026 while card APRs (~21–22%) remain near the top of their 1994– range; for a stress narrative, the combination "falling revolving balances + rising minimum-payment share (`RCCCBSHRMIN` 10.15% vs 8.76% in 2012)" is more informative than either alone. Falling balances can mean either lender tightening (see SLOOS card standards tightening in July 2026) or borrower deleveraging.
- Because G.19 card and personal-loan rates are sampled once a quarter (first week of the middle month), they should be treated as quarterly in the engine even though FRED stores them on a monthly calendar with gaps.
- Consumer credit growth is a volume/cycle indicator; the literature cited in the last section (Mian-Sufi-Verner) links *rapid household debt growth* to later weakness, so the engine's outlier test is more meaningful on multi-year growth of debt-to-income/GDP than on monthly G.19 growth.

### Gaps
- No free, official, market-wide series for nonbank/fintech personal-loan rates or volumes was found (TransUnion, Experian and Equifax publish such data commercially; not verified here).
- The exact scope threshold of the Philadelphia Fed Y-14M sample (which banks file FR Y-14M) was not verified in this session.
- The SLOOS-like card terms (credit limits, minimum payment rules) are not in G.19; only rates are.

## Bank lending standards and demand for households (SLOOS)

### Takeaway
SLOOS is free, quarterly, released around FOMC meetings, and on FRED. Card standards (`DRTSCLCC`) run from 1996; auto, other-consumer and the card/auto/other demand series only from 2011:Q2; residential mortgage standards are split across **three regimes** (aggregate 1990–2007, prime/nontraditional/subprime 2007–2014, GSE-eligible/government/QM/non-QM since 2015). The July 2026 survey reported tighter card standards, unchanged auto/other consumer standards, weaker auto and RRE demand, and standards "at the tighter end of the range" since 2005 for all loan categories except C&I.

### Cited Findings
#### Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey on Bank Lending Practices — Federal Reserve Board; free; FRED release rid=191.
- What/frequency: "Survey of up to eighty large domestic banks and twenty-four U.S. branches and agencies of foreign banks"; "generally conducts the survey quarterly, timing it so that results are available for the January/February, April/May, August, and October/November meetings of the [FOMC]"; occasional extra surveys — [SLOOS page](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/sloos.htm). Released ~5 weeks after the survey window; reported values are "net percentage" of banks tightening (or reporting stronger demand).
- Latest: July 2026 SLOOS (covering 2026:Q2): "standards tightened for credit card loans, with demand remaining basically unchanged. Standards remained basically unchanged for auto and other consumer loans, while demand weakened for auto loans"; for RRE, mixed standards and weaker demand; HELOC standards unchanged, demand stronger. Special questions: banks said standards "are currently at the tighter end of the range for all loan categories except C&I loans" relative to the range since 2005 — [July 2026 SLOOS](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/sloos/sloos-202607.htm)
- FRED ids and history (checked 2026-10-14; last obs 2026-07 = July 2026 survey unless noted):
  - Credit cards, standards: `DRTSCLCC` (from 1996-01; 6.7 net % tightening). Card demand: `DEMCC` (from 2011-04; 2.2)
  - Auto standards `STDSAUTO` (from 2011-04; -4.5); auto demand `DEMAUTO` (from 2011-04; -11.4)
  - Other consumer standards `STDSOTHCONS` (from 2011-04; 0.0); demand `DEMOTHCONS` (from 2011-04; -4.2)
  - Willingness to make consumer installment loans `DRIWCIL` (FRED CSV first obs 1982-04; -2.0)
  - Residential mortgage standards, CURRENT categories (from 2015-01): GSE-eligible `SUBLPDHMSENQ` (0.0), government `SUBLPDHMSGNQ`, QM jumbo `SUBLPDHMSJNQ`, QM non-jumbo non-GSE `SUBLPDHMSQNQ`, non-QM jumbo `SUBLPDHMSKNQ` (-6.5), non-QM non-jumbo `SUBLPDHMSMNQ` (0.0), subprime `DRTSSP` (from 2007-04; 9.1); demand analogues `SUBLPDHMDENQ` (GSE-eligible demand, -11.8) etc.; HELOC standards `SUBLPDCLHSNQ` (from 2008-01; -4.0) — ids from [FRED search](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/searchresults/?st=tightening%20standards%20mortgage)
  - BREAKS in mortgage categories: `H0SUBLPDHMSNQ` "Tightening Standards for Mortgage Loans (DISCONTINUED)" runs 1990-07 to 2007-01; `DRTSPM` prime and `DRTSNT` nontraditional (both DISCONTINUED) run 2007-04 to 2014-10; the GSE-eligible/government/QM/non-QM split starts 2015-01. A long mortgage-standards history therefore needs splicing across three regimes (my reading of the FRED ids/date ranges).
  - Consumer split break: card/auto/other-consumer split series begin 2011-04; before that only cards (`DRTSCLCC`) and the aggregate "consumer loans" measures exist (inference from FRED start dates; aggregate pre-2011 consumer series id not verified here).
- Access: Fed tables, DDP (being retired — see the DDP retirement note at the top of these notes), XML zip, FRED CSV/API. Free.

- SLOOS is used directly in Federal Reserve research as a credit-supply indicator: "Using bank-level responses to the Federal Reserve's Loan Officer Opinion Survey, we construct a new credit supply indicator ... Tightening shocks to this credit supply indicator lead to a substantial decline in output and the capacity of businesses and households to borrow from banks" — [Bassett, Chosak, Driscoll and Zakrajšek, "Changes in Bank Lending Standards and the Macroeconomy", FEDS 2012-24 (rev. Oct 2013)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/changes-in-bank-lending-standards-and-the-macroeconomy.htm)

### Inferences
- For a long consumer series, `DRTSCLCC` (cards, 1996–) and `DRIWCIL` (willingness to make consumer installment loans) are the only long household-standards measures; the 2011+ auto/other-consumer series give only ~15 years and one stress episode (2020), so their "normal range" is thin.
- SLOOS is a net-percentage diffusion index bounded at ±100 and moves in large jumps in crises; an outlier engine should test levels against the series' own history but expect fat tails (e.g. +59.3 net tightening for HELOCs at its 2008:Q1 start).
- The special July questions on "level of standards relative to the range since 2005" give a direct, publisher-stated "where are we now" reading once a year; it can be quoted verbatim in a narrative without modelling.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm the FRED id of a pre-2011 aggregate "consumer loans other than credit card" standards series, so the consumer split break (2011:Q2) could not be bridged here.
- I did not verify exact SLOOS release dates for 2026 beyond the July 2026 survey page; the FRED last obs (2026-07) corresponds to that survey.
- No published study was found in this session that tests SLOOS *consumer* standards specifically against consumer loan losses (the verified evidence is for output, loan volumes and business lending).

## Consumer sentiment and expectations (University of Michigan, Conference Board, NY Fed SCE)

### Takeaway
Of the three, only the **NY Fed SCE is openly licensed** (royalty-free reuse with attribution) and it has the most lending-relevant question: the **mean probability of missing a minimum debt payment in the next three months** (monthly from June 2013; 12.2% in Sep 2026 vs a 12.3% full-sample mean and a 15.3% Dec 2025 high). University of Michigan data are free to view and use internally but **may not be redistributed without written consent**, are delayed one month on FRED, and have a **mode break (phone→web) in mid-2024**; sentiment hit a record low in May 2026 even on a method-consistent basis. The Conference Board index is licensed.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- For a bank tool that will be shown to others, SCE is the safest sentiment input on licensing grounds; UMich can be used internally (display/reformat for the organisation's use) but not redistributed; Conference Board needs a subscription.
- UMich readings since 2025 carry a documented partisan composition shift; an outlier test on UMich alone will flag "record pessimism" that the publisher partly attributes to respondent politics. - Conference Board licence terms were not read directly; the redistribution summary comes from a search result.

### Inferences
- For geography, the cheapest complete stack is FHFA HPI (state/metro) + NMDB (state delinquency, by market) + CFPB MPT (county delinquency) + HOAM (metro/county affordability) + Census permits; all free.
- Mortgage risk today looks like a **product-mix** story (FHA/VA, low-down-payment recent buyers) rather than a **leverage** story (negative equity 1.9% vs 26% in 2009; FSR "housing leverage ... remained subdued"). An engine reading only national delinquency would miss it; split NMDB by market ("Government / Non-Conventional").
- New-home months' supply of 8.5 and existing-home supply of 4.8 (Sep 2026) are both inventory measures but on different bases; the new-home number is noisy (±17.2% CI on monthly change) and should be smoothed before outlier testing.
- High mortgage rates with low negative equity mean HELOC growth (17 straight quarterly increases per HHDC) is the main channel for housing wealth to support consumer credit; watch HELOC transitions in HHDC and SLOOS HELOC standards.

## Which indicators have a documented record of leading consumer loan losses or recessions, and how strong?

### Takeaway
The strongest **published** evidence is for (1) the **debt service ratio** as an early-warning indicator of banking crises at 1–2 year horizons (BIS cross-country work); (2) **rapid growth in household debt/leverage** predicting weaker growth and, across US counties, the 2007–09 default and consumption collapse (Mian and Sufi; Mian, Sufi and Verner); (3) **SLOOS lending standards** predicting loan volumes and output (Lown and Morgan; Bassett et al.); and (4) **negative equity combined with income shocks** ("double trigger") driving mortgage default (Bhutta, Dokko and Shan). **Consumer sentiment** has a documented but economically small and period-dependent incremental forecasting power for spending. Delinquency *transitions* (HHDC flows, ICE new defaults) are coincident-to-short-leading for charge-offs by construction rather than by econometric evidence. I found **no** published test that the SCE "probability of missing a debt payment" leads realised delinquency.

### Cited Findings
**Debt service ratio — strong evidence (cross-country, crisis prediction, not US consumer losses specifically).**
- "Applying the criteria to a set of potential EWIs, we find that the credit-to-GDP gap and a new indicator, the debt service ratio (DSR), consistently outperform other measures. The credit-to-GDP gap is the best indicator at longer horizons, whereas the DSR dominates at shorter horizons" — [Drehmann and Juselius, BIS Working Paper 421 (2013), abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bis:biswps:421); [BIS page](https://www.bis.org/publ/work421.htm)
- BIS Quarterly Review (Sept 2012) reports the DSR gives an accurate early-warning signal of systemic banking crises up to one to two years ahead — [BIS QR Sept 2012, Drehmann and Juselius](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1209e.pdf) (claim taken from search-result excerpt; PDF not readable from this machine). Note: the BIS DSR covers the private non-financial sector, not the Fed's household DSR; the evidence transfers to the household DSR only by analogy (inference).

**Household leverage growth — strong evidence for recessions and county-level default.**
- "An increase in the household debt to GDP ratio in the medium run predicts lower subsequent GDP growth, higher unemployment, and negative growth forecasting errors in a panel of 30 countries from 1960 to 2012" — [Mian, Sufi and Verner, NBER WP 21581 (2015, rev. 2016)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21581) (published in QJE 2017 — publication venue not re-verified here)
- "household leverage as of 2006 is a powerful statistical predictor of the severity of the 2007 to 2009 recession across U.S. counties"; leverage growth and credit-card dependence "explain a large fraction of the overall consumer default, house price, unemployment, residential investment, and durable consumption patterns during the recession" — [Mian and Sufi, "Household Leverage and the Recession of 2007 to 2009", NBER WP 15896 (Apr 2010)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15896)

**Lending standards (SLOOS) — moderate-to-strong evidence for loan volumes and output; consumer-loss link not directly tested in the sources found.**
- Lown and Morgan, "The Credit Cycle and the Business Cycle: New Findings Using the Loan Officer Opinion Survey" (JMCB 2006): shocks to SLOOS lending standards are significantly correlated with innovations in commercial loans and real output; standards dominate loan rates in explaining business loans and output — [NY Fed working copy](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/economists/morgan/morgan_credit_cycle.pdf) (findings from abstract/search excerpt; full text not read)
- Tightening shocks to a demand-adjusted SLOOS credit-supply indicator "lead to a substantial decline in output and the capacity of businesses and households to borrow from banks" — [Bassett, Chosak, Driscoll, Zakrajšek, FEDS 2012-24](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/changes-in-bank-lending-standards-and-the-macroeconomy.htm)

**House prices / negative equity — strong evidence for mortgage default, via the double trigger.**
- Among 2006 non-prime 100%-financed purchasers in AZ, CA, FL, NV, "the median borrower does not strategically default until equity falls to -62 percent"; "about 80 percent of defaults in our sample are the result of income shocks combined with negative equity", supporting the "double-trigger" theory — [Bhutta, Dokko and Shan, "The Depth of Negative Equity and Mortgage Default Decisions", FEDS 2010-35](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2010/201035/index.html)
- FHFA positions its HPI as useful "for estimating changes in the rates of mortgage defaults" — [FHFA HPI](https://www.fhfa.gov/data/hpi); the Fed's stress tests drive losses with a 30% house-price decline — [2026 scenarios](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-stress-test-scenarios.htm)

**Consumer sentiment — weak-to-moderate, period-dependent evidence (for spending, not credit losses).**
- Michigan says its expectations measures are included in the Commerce/BEA Leading Indicator Composite Index and the OECD Composite Leading Indicator for the US — [UMich survey description](https://data.sca.isr.umich.edu/survey-description.php) (publisher's own claim)
- Literature review: "Carroll, Fuhrer, and Wilcox (1994) reported that consumer attitudes further improved consumption forecasts, even after other macroeconomic variables were allowed for. In other studies, however, consumer attitudes did not significantly improve consumption forecasts when macroeconomic variables ... were taken into account"; attitudes helped "at some times", e.g. near the 1990–91 recession — [Wilcox, FRBSF Economic Letter 2008-19 (June 2008)](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2008/06/consumer-sentiment-spending/)
- A Kansas City Fed bulletin titled "Forecasting with Feelings: The Modest Link between Consumer Sentiment and Spending" — [KC Fed Economic Bulletin](https://www.kansascityfed.org/research/economic-bulletin/forecasting-with-feelings-the-modest-link-between-consumer-sentiment-and-spending/) (title and summary via search only; page could not be fetched)

**Delinquency flows and stock measures — mechanical lead to charge-offs; measurement matters.**
- NY Fed: the flow into 90+ for cards tracks the Call Report delinquency rate, while the 90+ stock diverged because charged-off debts stay on credit reports longer (40% still reported after a year in 2004–2012 vs 80% by 2024) — [Liberty Street Economics, 11 Aug 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/08/how-distressed-are-consumers-reconciling-diverging-credit-card-delinquency-measures/)
- Practitioners read delinquency and labour data forward: MBA's Marina Walsh (Aug 2025) said early labour-market weakness and rising delinquencies in student, card and auto debt "suggest future increases in mortgage delinquencies" — [MBA NewsLink, 15 Aug 2025](https://newslink.mba.org/?p=259179) (opinion, not a tested result)

**How the Fed frames "where are we now".**
- The FSR describes household indicators by their position in their own history (e.g. auto delinquency "at the 90th percentile of its historical distribution"; mortgage delinquency "at the lower end of its historical distribution") — [FSR May 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-may-financial-stability-report-borrowing.htm)

### Inferences
- Indicative strength ranking for a consumer-loss narrative (my judgement from the sources above, not a published ranking):
  - **Strong, documented:** household DSR (as crisis early-warning, 1–2 years); multi-year household debt/leverage growth (recession severity, county default); house-price declines/negative equity × unemployment (mortgage default); SLOOS standards (credit volumes/output).
  - **Coincident or mechanically short-leading:** HHDC flow-into-delinquency by product; ICE new 90+ defaults and foreclosure starts; NMDB/CFPB 30–89 day mortgage delinquency; Philadelphia Fed Y-14M 30+ DPD and minimum-payment share; bankruptcy filings.
  - **Plausible but weakly documented for losses:** consumer sentiment (UMich, Conference Board); SCE debt-payment probability; affordability (HOAM, NAR); housing starts/permits/months' supply (better documented as business-cycle leads than as loan-loss leads — not verified here); consumer credit growth in a single month; card APR levels.
- Many of these indicators have short or broken histories (new DSR 2005–, SLOOS consumer splits 2011–, SCE 2013–, Realtor.com 2016–, Philly Y-14M 2012–); with ≤2 stress episodes in-sample, statistical "outlier" thresholds will be fragile. Longer anchors: PMMS (1971–), FHFA HPI (1975–), Census starts (1959–), UMich (monthly 1978–), G.19 (1943/1968–), card standards (1996–), NMDB delinquency (2002–), HHDC (2003–).

- No study verified in this session that tests SLOOS *consumer* standards against consumer charge-offs or that quantifies the lead of housing starts/permits for consumer losses.
- BIS QR 2012 claims are from a search excerpt; the PDF returned a browser challenge on this machine.
