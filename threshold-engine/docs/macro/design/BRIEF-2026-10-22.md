## What the workbook is

The Macrobook reads public economic series and says where each stands against
its own history. Its readers are a bank's consumer and commercial credit-risk
teams. It holds no bank data at all, only public series from the BLS, the
Federal Reserve, FHFA, BEA, the NY Fed and FRED.

Each series is read two ways:

- **Level**: where the latest reading sits in its own history, as the share of
  every reading that it is at or above (0% the lowest, 100% the highest). A
  series that grows with prices, such as CPI, has no level reading. Its place
  in history says nothing, so it is read on its change only.
- **Change** over 12 months, against its **normal range**: the median change
  plus or minus one *typical move*. A change inside the range reads
  **Stable**; at or above the top, **Increasing**; at or below the bottom,
  **Decreasing**. *Typical moves* is how far the change sits from its median,
  on a scale every series shares.

**Nothing is rated.** The firm, 22 Oct 2026: *"We are not rating these. At most
these are directional."* Directions are facts about movement, not verdicts.
Where the research says which way a series signals stress, the series tab says
so in words, as a fact about the research.

## Why now

The workbook was built to work first, and it does: every figure ties to its
publisher. It was styled after PocketBook and the threshold workbook. But it
has grown in one day: no more ratings, a Dashboard tab, household debt by
type, the prime rate, a zoom, and quarters that can be left out. And no part
of it has had a design pass of its own. The firm, 22 Oct 2026, in Excel:

- *"Hard to read and I would really like to auto adjust the axis to match and
  also why can't I have a custom view of quarters? Also how are these dashes
  useful…"* (the Dashboard, `excel-before/`)
- *"And this is just blank"* (the levels chart, when both picks are read on
  their change only)
- *"Finally there should be a fairly simple to drive dashboard tab that
  basically lets you layer any 2 charts or something see the individual charts
  and see a chart the shows magnitude in movement"*
- *"i want to be able to simply change the view of quarters not change cutoffs
  or averages or anything but to literally zoom in on a set of quarters"*
- *"I think we should have household as a graph on this that split between as
  it is literally reported"*

The Dashboard was rebuilt that evening. Its axes now run in years and fit
the picks, there is one legend entry a line, From and To boxes set the
quarters, and the levels chart says when a pick has no line. That fixed what
was broken. It did not make it easy to read.

## The ask

**Design the whole workbook for what its readers come to it to do.** Do not
just restyle what is there. For each tab, work out:

- what the reader comes to do;
- whether they can do it without hunting or scrolling;
- a design that lets them.

Where the right answer is a different set of tabs, a different order, or a
section moved, propose it. Fix every design problem you find. The list below
is one look, not a complete list.

Work in this order:

1. **Read before designing.** Open the workbook in Excel, click through every
   kind of tab, and change the Dashboard's four boxes. Read `TOOL.md` for what
   each number is.
2. **For every tab, write down first** its job in one sentence, who opens it and
   when, what must be visible without scrolling, and what is wrong today. Then
   design.
3. **Design the workbook as one thing.** One grid, one type scale, and one way
   each to show a reading, a direction, a normal range, a source, a chart, a
   note and a control. A reader who has learned one tab can read the next.
4. **Check the design against the constraints below** before handing it over.
   Say where an idea did not fit and what you did instead.

**The firm judges the work by fine detail.** That means:

- correct number formats;
- consistent spacing;
- the same font and size for the same job;
- columns that line up;
- rules and borders used one way everywhere;
- nothing cut off and nothing wrapping badly.

It does not mean more words. Add no explanatory text, helper sentences or
decorative headings. If a tab needs a sentence to be understood, fix the
layout first.

Priorities, in order:

1. **Dashboard**: the tab the firm uses to compare. It must be simple to
   drive and readable at a glance.
2. **Overview**: the front page. Every core series by topic, with level,
   change and direction.
3. **The series tab**, one design that 66 tabs share. Its variants:
   - level and change (Unemployment);
   - change only (Prices);
   - quarterly (House prices);
   - daily or weekly (Initial claims, Prime rate);
   - with the Sahm reading (Unemployment).
4. **The tab set**: 76 visible tabs, one per series. Propose how a reader finds
   one without scrolling the tab bar.
5. **Household debt, Usable with care, States, What if**.
6. **The reference tabs** (Glossary, Run, Sources, Not read, State data):
   quiet, consistent, readable when someone opens one.

## Constraints

**What the generator can write** (Python and openpyxl, values written once):

- fills, fonts, borders, number formats, row heights, column widths, merges;
- freeze panes, hidden rows and columns, grouped (folding) rows and columns;
- hyperlinks to another tab, tab colours, tab order;
- list boxes (data validation) as the only controls;
- native line, area, bar and scatter charts.

**What it cannot write**: slicers, form buttons, macros, shapes or text boxes,
sparklines, pivot tables, or conditional formatting that depends on a picked
series' history. A control is a cell with a list.

**Formulas exist only on the Dashboard**, and only look up values on its
hidden data tab. They may use only functions every Excel since 2007 knows by
name: INDEX, MATCH, IF, IFERROR, ISNUMBER, ISNA, AND, OR, NA, MIN, MAX, ROW.
A newer function, such as AGGREGATE, TEXTJOIN, FILTER or XLOOKUP, reads #NAME?
in some Excel versions. Everywhere else every cell is a value, so nothing moves
after the build.

**A chart's axis cannot follow a cell.** Bounds are either fixed when the file
is built, or left to Excel to fit to what is drawn. The Dashboard leaves them
to Excel, so the axes fit the picks and the quarters. That is why a spike
dominates when every quarter is shown.

**Never two value axes on one chart.** That is why the Dashboard puts two
series on shared scales (typical moves; at or above, 0 to 100%) and not on a
secondary axis.

**Excel draws what LibreOffice does not.** These are known from the firm's
screen:

- A chart's points must be one colour a line, stated in the file. Otherwise
  Excel gives each point a legend entry: the rows of dashes in `excel-before/`.
- A date axis in day numbers starts at 1900. The Dashboard uses decimal years.
- Labels set at the foot of the chart overlap a legend placed at the bottom.

**Fonts**: Arial, Calibri and Consolas only.

**Colour**:

- Extend `keybank_style.py`; don't replace it. The threshold workbook uses it
  too.
- Lines must pass 3:1 contrast against white, or the chart carries a legend
  and a table.
- **No colour may read as good or bad.** No red and green, and no heat fill
  on a direction or on a level. Either would make the workbook a scorecard,
  and it rates nothing.
- Today Increasing and Decreasing carry an arrow and a light fill. Say
  whether that is right.

**Words are the firm's.**

- Do not rewrite a sentence or a label.
- If a label does not fit, keep it and list it with a shorter version for the
  firm to choose.
- Plain, literal language: no verdicts and no adjectives on a reading.

**Many tabs come from one design.** One function builds every series tab. A
design for the series tab applies to all 66, across five frequencies (daily,
weekly, monthly, quarterly) and the variants above. The Overview's and Usable
with care's rows come from the catalog, so the sections grow and shrink.

**Public data only.** Add no bank's logo or brand marks beyond the tokens
already in `keybank_style.py`.

## What to deliver

So the design can be applied to the generator directly, deliver what the
threshold workbook's rounds did (`reference/threshold-HANDOFF.md` shows the
shape):

1. **A mock**, one HTML file (`Macrobook Redesign.dc.html`) drawn cell for cell
   at Excel's 100% zoom on a 1920×1080 screen, using `data/*.json` for real
   figures. It should show:
   - the Overview;
   - the Dashboard, with its boxes working in the mock;
   - a series tab in each variant;
   - Household debt;
   - Usable with care;
   - one reference tab;
   - for each, what is above the fold and below.
2. **`README.md`, the build spec, tab by tab**:
   - column widths in Excel units;
   - row heights in points;
   - every cell role's font, size, colour, fill, border and number format;
   - every chart's type, size in centimetres, line widths in points,
     colours, dashes, axis positions and formats, legend position and
     gridlines;
   - freeze panes;
   - the tab list in its new order, with tab colours and what is hidden.
3. **A token sheet**: new tokens named, each with its hex and its job, as
   additions to `keybank_style.py`.
4. **`MESSAGE_TO_ARCHITECT.md`**:
   - what the round decides;
   - what to confirm before building;
   - what in the mock is drawn to shape and not real;
   - any label too long to fit, with a shorter version for the firm.
5. **Cuts**: anything you would remove, and why.

The design comes back to the generator's maintainer, who applies it in
`workbook.py`, rebuilds the example, and checks it in Excel and LibreOffice
before the firm sees it.
