# Good records for a business

A business return starts from books that match the bank statements.

---

## 01 · One account for the business, one for you

The IRS's own first instruction to a new business is to open a separate
checking account and put every business receipt through it.

When one account does both jobs, someone has to read it line by line and
decide which side each entry belongs on. That work happens before the return
can start.

A credit card used only for the business does the same job.

---

## 02 · What matching the bank means

- Each month's closing balance in the books equals the closing balance on the
  statement.
- The same holds at year end for every account: checking, savings, credit
  cards, loans.
- A number in the books with no statement behind it is a guess. Finding the
  record behind it is part of keeping the books, and it has to happen before
  the return can use that number.

---

## 03 · The year-end set

- A profit and loss, and a balance sheet, as of the last day of the year.
- Year-end statements for every account, and for each loan its balance and the
  interest paid.
- The year's payroll summary, and copies of the W-2s that went out.
- Anything bought that lasts more than a year: what it was, the date, the
  cost. Vehicles, equipment, building work.
- A count of what is on the shelves at year end, if you carry stock.
- What the owners put in or took out, and any loan taken or repaid.

That is the usual set, not the whole of it. Your business may need more, and
if you are not sure whether something belongs, ask us before the year closes
rather than after.

---

## 04 · Miles, home office, meals

- **Miles.** Written and kept as you drive: date, distance, and the reason for
  the trip.
- **The car method is a one-time choice.** Standard mileage has to be picked in
  the first year the car is used for the business. A leased car stays on that
  method for the whole lease, renewals included.
- **Home office.** The square footage of the room and of the house, plus the
  year's bills for the house &mdash; utilities and insurance to start with. What
  counts here shifts, so send more than you think we need and ask us if you are
  unsure.
- **Meals.** A receipt does not carry who was there or why. Write it on the
  receipt at the time.

---

## 05 · People you paid

- Ask for a W-9 before the first payment, not in January.
- $2,000 or more to someone who is not an employee usually means a 1099-NEC. It
  is due both to them and to the IRS by January 31. That threshold was $600
  until recently and it rises with inflation from 2027, so ask rather than
  assume it.
- Schedule C asks on the form itself whether you made payments that required a
  1099. It is a question the return has to answer either way.

---

## 06 · If it is a partnership or a corporation

- The return is due March 15 for a calendar year, and the owners' K-1s are due
  on that date too. If March 15 is a weekend or a holiday it moves to the next
  working day.
- So the company's books decide when its owners can file, not just when the
  company can.
- Past a certain size the return carries a balance sheet, which means year-end
  balances have to be right rather than close.
- If you own more than 2% of an S corporation and it pays for your health
  insurance, that amount belongs in your W-2 wages. Fixing it after the W-2 has
  gone out means reissuing forms.

## 07 · Letters from a tax office

Send the whole letter, both sides, with the envelope. Some of them run on a
30-day clock from the date printed at the top.

---

## 08 · What a business keeps for longer

How long to keep things is on the other page. One rule sits on top of it here:
payroll records run four years, longer than most of the rest.
