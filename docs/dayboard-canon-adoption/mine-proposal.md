## 3. Proposed convictions

**None.**

Not because the beliefs are absent — this repository is unusually opinionated —
but because a conviction is a quotation, and I have nothing of his to quote.
Canon is explicit that *"a conviction paraphrased is one they will disown the
moment it is read back at them"*, and the version available here is worse than
paraphrase: it would be **an agent's sentence, filed under the owner's name, in
the file he is later challenged from.** The mechanism does not survive that once.

The four entries I would have drafted, and the exact reason each is withdrawn:

| Would-be conviction | Best available text | Why it is not proposable |
|---|---|---|
| The board must never show anything that is wrong | `CLAUDE.md` hard rule 1 + `DESIGN-BRIEF.md` hard constraint 1 | Agent's wording in both. The brief cites a real incident — the board once showed a fixed fake day to everyone — but the sentence about it is not his. |
| It is the shared surface, not the entry point | `CLAUDE.md` "What Dayboard is for"; `DESIGN-BRIEF.md` "The one idea everything else follows from" | The most conviction-shaped thing in the repo and the one only an owner can hold — and there is not one word of his behind it. |
| The wall device is the least privileged thing in the house | `CLAUDE.md`: *"it sits in a kitchen and is touched by guests, babysitters and eventually children"* | Carries a reason about people, which is canon's tell. Still the agent's sentence. |
| Merging is the deploy, and self-merge is a temporary loan | `CLAUDE.md` "Shipping a change" | Records that *he granted* a standing permission — an owner act I can evidence — but not a word of how he granted it. |

**One thing I am declining to propose that I could technically have proposed.**
The section-2 quote is real and dated. I am not drafting it as a conviction:
it is a calibration remark about *this exercise*, in the same class as canon's
own counter-example *"unblock the estimate first"* — correct when said, empty as
a standing rule. It also has a shape I distrust in my own hands: a conviction
reading "do not be strict here" is one an agent benefits from holding, and that
is a reason to hand it back rather than file it. **If he reads that and says it
is a conviction, it is — that call is his, not mine.**

## 4. What to do instead — four questions, one line each

Nothing below is a proposal. Each is a question whose answer, typed by him, would
be a quotable passage and the start of an actual Dayboard corpus.

**1. The one that is already live, and needs answering first.**
`C5 · The rule is publication, not the word "main"` — *"This conviction was really
meant to stop it from pushing to the website live."* (the firm, 4 Sep 2026) —
fires here on subject and on fact. Dayboard's `main` **is** a publish path:
`deploy.yml` runs on push to `main` and ships to a tablet on a kitchen wall, and
`main` is unprotected. `CLAUDE.md` meanwhile carries a standing permission for
agents to merge their own work, granted — in its own words — *"specifically
because nothing depends on it yet"*, with an instruction to revisit **"the moment
the household starts actually using the board."**

Two things collide and I am resolving neither: C5 says the publish path is the
control; the repo says self-merge is fine *for now*. Both were right when
written. **Is the board in daily use yet?** — because that is the condition the
repo itself set for the permission expiring, and no agent can see the answer from
here. Note also that C5 records `Applies: SATC repositories`, and Dayboard is
not one; whether the firm's record governs a personal repository is itself
unanswered.

**2. Why must the board never show anything that is wrong?** The rule is
enforced in five places and cost the mock layer. One or two sentences in his
words on *why* — stale-with-a-timestamp being acceptable and invented never
being acceptable — is the strongest conviction candidate in the project.

**3. What is the board for, in his words?** "The shared surface, not the entry
point" is the premise every other decision in the repo is derived from, and it is
currently held only in an agent's paraphrase of him.

**4. Should there be a Dayboard corpus at all?** SATC's exists because someone
harvested transcripts before a container was wiped. Dayboard's sessions were
wiped un-harvested, and the same thing will happen to this one. Either something
captures his words as he types them, or this run's result — *no owner words
survive* — is the permanent answer. Dayboard also has **no card in
`projects/REGISTER.md`**; `canon-adopt` (which proposes *tenets* from commits, and
needs no owner quotes) is the tool that fits this repository as it actually is,
where `canon-mine` is not.
