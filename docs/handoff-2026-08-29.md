## What was built

**A live engagement can be quoted again.** `client-documents/requote.py` — an
engagement used to be priced exactly once, inside `intake.finish`, at the moment
it was created, and not one of the twenty-one commands could add a chargeable
line to work that already existed. A client who rang in March to say they had
bought a rental had to be re-interviewed as a second engagement, or have a
figure typed onto an invoice by hand. `requote` changes the **answers** and
prices them again through the same engine that priced them the first time; there
is no box for an amount, because a box for an amount is a second way onto the
money with none of the fee schedule's rules behind it. Which answers are offered
is read out of the schedule — `count_from`, `tier_from`, `select_from` and every
gate key — rather than kept in a list in Python that would go stale silently.
Nothing is written without a stated reason, `revisions.json` is append-only, and
`EstimateDate` is now split from `LetterDate` so the estimate carries the day it
was quoted while the engagement letter keeps the date the client signed under.
Two sheets in a drawer showing different totals under one date is a question
nobody can answer next February.

**The signature register.** `client-documents/signing.py` plus
`registry/signing.yaml`. Six places in this codebase already spoke of a signed
engagement letter as a fact, and nothing anywhere recorded that anybody had
signed anything. Who must sign is **censused off the templates' own `sigrow` and
`siglab` blocks**, so a block that moves or gains a signer is followed without
anybody editing code — the spouse's line is conditional in the template and
conditional here, from the same fact. How a signature reached us is kept as a
record rather than a tick, because "he signed it in front of me" and "the portal
said it completed" are different kinds of knowing and flattening them loses the
difference at the moment somebody asks which it was.

**The e-file authorization is declared, not censused.** Drake prints it and no
template here will ever be one, so the census that reads our own paper cannot
see it — and it is the signature the delivery letter's promise is actually
about. `registry/signing.yaml` declares it as a signature the *engagement*
needs, whoever printed the form, which is what lets it be blocked on rather than
shrugged at. The form names live in the registry because the IRS renames them:
8879-C and 8879-S became one **8879-CORP**, and **8879-PE** carries a December
2025 revision. `may_file` reports what it **cannot** see — the 8879 and whether
the invoice is settled — as unknown rather than as passed.

**A send date, and the morning list.** `sending.build` wrote a pack to a folder
and stopped; it had no recipient and no date. `--sent` records that it went, by
which route, and starts the clock, so "outstanding" stops meaning "nobody has
signed yet", which on the morning you built the pack is not a chase. `cli.py
sign` with no engagement sweeps every one of them, overdue first then longest
waiting, and an engagement whose record cannot be read is **skipped rather than
counted clear** — the rule `reconcile` already follows, because a control that
only examines the work somebody filed properly is a control over the diligent.

**The pack, addressed.** `client-documents/outgoing.py` composes the covering
email and writes an ordinary `.eml` — it opens in the mail client already
addressed, already attached, already written, and a human presses send. It
**refuses to compose** until the firm has accepted or rewritten the covering
wording, which lives behind a `[CONFIRM: ]` in `registry/signing.yaml`; the
draft is assembled from sentences the firm has already published in the
onboarding letter rather than invented. Nothing here sends: a test asserts the
module names neither `smtplib`, `requests`, `urllib` nor `socket`, because the
send is the only irreversible step in the pipeline and a gate that blocks nine
ways followed by an automatic send has moved the risk rather than removed it.
`secure_keyword` is a registry setting and ships **empty** — two things about
Encyro's `[Secure]` keyword are unverified, including whether the add-in strips
it before the client reads the subject line.

**Supporting work, each its own join nothing was making.** `money.parse` is one
parser beside the formatter where `invoicing` held the only copy;
`interview.coerce` is one converter for both front doors;
`interview.composable_fields` fixes a recompose that only merged, and so left
every field the new answers had stopped supplying — a letter going on naming a
prior firm the client had just said they never had; and `consistency` gained the
K-1 check described below.

## The bugs, because they share a shape — and the firm named it

The 26 August handoff named the shape as *the software saying something was fine
when it was not*. This session's crop is a narrower relative of it, and it is
worth naming separately because the fix is different: **a claim in one place,
behaviour in another, and nothing comparing them.** Four instances:

1. **`count_k1s` against the additional-forms scope line.** The count is billed
   on the estimate and the scope line is printed two inches above it in the
   preparer's own words. Seen live on an engagement billing four K-1s under a
   line reading *"Two K-1s as reported"*. `consistency.k1_claim` now parses the
   sentence and compares it.
2. **`count_states` against the `states` list**, and `count_localities` against
   `localities` — the letter naming one number of returns while another is
   billed.
3. **Six places asserting a signed engagement letter with nothing recording
   one**, which is what `signing.py` exists to close.
4. **The invoice promising a Square link the invoice cannot produce.** Still
   open; see below.

The remedy that generalises is not a test. Where a re-quote **opens** one of
these gaps it is blocked; where the gap was already there it is said out loud
and stops nothing — because the first cut blocked on the gap however it got
there, and that trapped a preparer: an engagement that already disagreed with
itself refused every re-quote, including the one that would have fixed it.

**Two more that were only found by running the thing.** The two-K-1 allowance
(below) was billed from the first K-1 while `base.1065.starting_note` promised —
and the live price page published — *"Each partner's K-1 after the first two"*.
A two-partner return was quoted $880 against a page promising $800, on every
entity engagement with two or more owners, for as long as both existed, while
1,001 tests passed, because no test knew the sentence existed. And YAML's folded
block (`>-`) turned every line break in the covering note into a space, so the
composed message arrived as one paragraph — a letter to a client with no
paragraphs in it, found by reading the message rather than the registry.

## Two mistakes I made, recorded so they are not repeated

**Weak evidence, stated strongly.** I flagged Encyro's 8879 KBA as
non-compliant on the strength of a search-index summary of a help article's
*title*, never having been able to open the page — encyro.com is blocked from
this environment. The firm, who has the product in front of them, corrected it:
*"No encyro is cheaper and has kba."* The flag is kept in
`docs/research-e-signature.md` as what was found, explicitly not as what is
believed, with the one written question that settles it permanently. A finding
recovered from a title is a guess, and a guess in a research document is **S1**
in a hat.

**Reasoning backwards from the right conclusion.** I cited `api.encyro.com`
appearing in Encyro's own allowlist guidance as proof that there is no customer
API. A vendor telling customers to allowlist a host is evidence that the host
**exists**. The conclusion survived a proper look — it is the web app's and the
Outlook add-in's own backend, with no developer portal, key or docs — but the
argument was inverted, and an inverted argument that happens to land right is
one that will land wrong the next time it is used.

## The regulatory research was never read from primary sources

This survives every other caveat in this file, and it is repeated at the top of
`docs/research-e-signature.md` for the same reason.

**Nobody has opened `p1345.pdf`.** This environment's egress proxy denies
`irs.gov`, `govinfo.gov`, `uscode.house.gov`, `codes.ohio.gov`, `ecfr.gov` and

**Before a line of it is built against, or a word of it reaches a client,
somebody must open the PDFs and check.** That is exactly what **S1** exists for:
a proof artifact once declared 190 documents fine when every one of them was

## Standing constraints — binding on every session, not just this one

None of these is new. They are here because a fresh session that has to
rediscover one of them will discover it by breaking it.

- **Never push to `main` without explicit approval.** It publishes to the live
  domain **satcllp.com** through Cloudflare Pages. Develop on a branch, open a
  draft PR, and let a human push.
- **`website/` is hands-off for this line of work.** The firm: *"stop making
  website decisions. Let me handle that with the other agent."* Read it to check
  a published price against the schedule; do not edit it.
- **`client-documents/leads.xlsx` is real client data** — every row a real
  person's name, email and phone. Never commit it, never copy it into a fixture,
  never let it into an artifact. The same goes for `client-documents/engagements/`
  and any `out/`. All three are in `client-documents/.gitignore` and none is
  tracked; the tests build their own one-row workbook with a made-up prospect in
  it. `git status` before `git add`, every time.
- **Where a fact is missing or a decision needs a human, leave `[CONFIRM: ...]`
  and keep going.** Never invent legal, regulatory or assurance wording. The
  merge engine treats a surviving `[CONFIRM:` as an unfilled field and refuses
  to render, so a placeholder can never reach a client — which is the whole
  point of writing one rather than a guess.
- **Decisions and anything needing discussion are given back in interview
  mode** — a decision-room artifact, one question at a time, rather than a wall
  of prose with the choices buried in it.
- **The line that governs a change here:** *change anything a test can prove;
  change nothing a client reads or pays.*

## Things a fresh session should not have to rediscover

- **Fan when you change one document.** The firm: *"you should fan documents
  when changing one to ensure consistency across."* Changing the K-1 allowance
  meant checking the fee schedule, the published price page, the sign-off
  register, the pricing threads document and the tests — and the last sentence
  in `fee-schedule.yaml` still describing the old behaviour was found by fanning,
  not by a test. It is now **S29** in `docs/SOFTWARE-TENETS.md`.
- **Front to back or it is not delivered** — **S28**, in the firm's own words:
  *"A tenet to our software and procedural creation should be doing this process
  front to back or I can't trust it'll work without debugging it myself."* A
  deliverable is the core, plus the command **and** the browser route, plus its
  step in the generated `docs/OPERATING-PROCEDURES.md`, plus the artifact opened
  in what the client opens.
- **Use subagents, and the firm asked for this explicitly** — for context
  economy, for genuinely independent work, and because a long single turn that
  dies loses everything in it. This repository's sessions are ephemeral; work
  that is not committed is gone, and a subagent that returns a result you commit
  is a smaller thing to lose.
- **One engine, two front doors.** Every gate lives in the core; tests read
  `cli.py` and `web.py` as source and fail if a decision appears there. Both
  doors and the walkthrough screens — 24 of them now — are part of shipping a
  command, not a follow-up.
- **`docs/OPERATING-PROCEDURES.md` is generated.** Regenerate it; do not edit it.
