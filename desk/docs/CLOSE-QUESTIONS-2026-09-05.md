# The 43 questions a close raised — and the firm's reading of them

**Where this came from.** An agent was closing a real set of books, could not
finish, and was told to stop and write down what it needed to know instead. It
produced 43 questions. The firm read them and answered about twenty in their own
words — as they put it, *"really just kinda rambling thoughts, but they do
represent sort of a theme."*

**This file is the denominator.** Every claim about what a desk can take over is
measured against these 43 and not against a set anyone here invented. The firm's
words are quoted verbatim and are never paraphrased into a rule; where a rule is
drawn from them it is proposed to `canon` and waits for a yes.

**De-identification is theirs.** No client name, account number or figure beyond
what they published here appears anywhere in this repository.

---

## What the firm's responses actually say

Read across the twenty they answered, six themes repeat. Each is quoted, because
the point of quoting is that the next session argues with the firm's words and
not with a summary of them.

### 1 · The question is often framed as a binary and is not one

> *"OK so I don't think this is an inherently bad question but obviously we
> shouldn't be like is it one or four we should be like OK how many accounts
> should it be what other reasons could there be for that like I'm assuming we
> want to know the reasoning so I had to give us the reasoning as well"* — Q1

> *"I think that Book them or refuse them is too narrow of a question to me this
> is more like the first question in the sense that it is how many accounts do we
> need to account for what the client does and how do we find that out and what
> do we need to know anyway"* — Q5

A desk that answers the binary it was handed answers the wrong question. The
reasoning is the deliverable, not the pick.

### 2 · Many questions carry their own answer

> *"see I would just take the default threshold here call it good generally so
> this is just a good question to have and to ratify on the spot if you can and
> like I know that why it matters already has it"* — Q4

The `WHY IT MATTERS` paragraph names the safe harbour and then the question asks
what to do. That is a question that has already been answered by the person
asking it.

### 3 · Where a safe harbour exists, take it

> *"this is another safe harbor moment for me like you don't have to defer to
> safe harbor but if there's no reason not to I feel like you might as well in
> general"* — Q19

Said three separate times, on Q4, Q18 and Q19. It is the closest thing in these
responses to a standing rule, and it is proposed to `canon` rather than assumed.

### 4 · What the client tells you is evidence

> *"the client had already told me that he could guarantee they were deductible
> and honestly I will believe the guy however this is a great question and I
> think that like the j.crew thing it may at least instead of being booked to
> owners drawers be flagged for attention"* — Q7

> *"knowing the client also helps not like literally knowing them but knowing
> that they're a contractor it's like I'm more willing to believe that they're
> going to shop at places that I normally wouldn't"* — Q8

> *"I just assume when the client accepts A Venmo payment that it was revenue and
> maybe experience tells me that knowing the client tells me that or maybe some
> website would even suggest that that someone would trust however we don't want
> to trust blindly"* — Q20

Three registers of evidence that the record currently has no way to hold: what
the client said, what the client IS, and what experience says. The last one is
explicitly not to be trusted blindly.

### 5 · "It does not matter" is a real answer and cannot be given

> *"This is an odd question because like it's a cash balance or it's a CSV from a
> bank so obviously you book it net ... I'm not really sure why this matters and
> it's also useful for an agent to tell someone something doesn't matter is if
> that's possible"* — Q28

> *"if an agent were an expert in accounting it would be able to make this kind
> of deductive reasoning"* — Q28

The engine has four outcomes and none of them is *this question does not change
anything*. On Q28 the profit is right either way and both lines are wrong by the
same amount; that is a fact about the question, not an escalation.

### 6 · Not every question is an authority question

> *"This is an interesting question because it's like well is this an authority
> question or is this like the problem with the software kind of question like
> the logic doing it"* — Q23

> *"yeah this connects to the vehicle expenses there should be something telling
> us to get like loan statements and stuff to make sure we understand the deal"*
> — Q26

Some of the 43 are answered by a document nobody requested, and some are software
defects wearing a question's clothes. Filing either under `authority_absent`
sends it to the wrong queue and it is never resolved.

### And one question put directly to this work

> *"I guess maybe it's critical for experts to consult each other every now and
> then and to synthesize an answer is that what the desk's job is?"* — Q18

> *"it'd be cool to tell an agent like hey this is your job and then like desk is
> just going to be available so when it gets stuck it kind of can ask desk for
> the next best step maybe even iterate on itself over time possibly but that's
> also kind of desk's job in the sense that it saves authoritative decisions"*
> — Q22

Answered in `docs/WHAT-A-DESK-IS-FOR.md` rather than here.

## The 43, verbatim

Each entry is the question as the closing agent wrote it, the reasoning it gave,
the call it made anyway, and the firm's response where they gave one. Nothing is
edited.

### TAXES (15)

**Q1 · Should vehicle costs be one account or four?**
*Why it matters:* A single Auto and Fuel account blends fuel, repairs, tyres, servicing and registration. The actual-expense method needs each of those itemised on the return, and a client may switch between the standard mileage rate and actual expenses year to year — you cannot rebuild the components from a blended total afterwards.
*What was done:* Everything to the one account, because that is the chart the client has. The cost is real and it is paid at return time, not now.
*The firm:* "OK so I don't think this is an inherently bad question but obviously we shouldn't be like is it one or four we should be like OK how many accounts should it be what other reasons could there be for that like I'm assuming we want to know the reasoning so I had to give us the reasoning as well no idea why I spelled tires with AY"

**Q2 · Is the vehicle used for business only?**
*Why it matters:* Mixed use needs a business-use percentage applied, and that needs a mileage log.
*What was done:* Expensed at 100%, flagged for the preparer.
*The firm:* "this is a good question especially if we do their taxes we want it to be right either way"

**Q3 · Whose vehicle is it?**
*Why it matters:* No vehicle appeared in fixed assets and no vehicle loan appeared anywhere in the year, yet the business ran vehicle costs all twelve months. If the truck is owned personally, the correct treatment is an accountable-plan reimbursement or mileage — not direct expensing of fuel on the business card.
*What was done:* Expensed as incurred; raised as a client question.
*The firm:* "This is reasonable and I suppose maybe I didn't really even think about this like this is kind of why earlier I said hmm what happens when there's interest and stuff it needs to be separated technically is it a lease do they own it does is it actually in the business's name is the entity an LLC that owns it is the owner just using it and using it as an LLC which is like probably most likely and that's what Chris does do but like I'm trying to provide more context so you can understand what all advice could be given and like this could provoke questions back from the principle that's doing the work so this influences even like what documents we might want whether we record it as an asset whether it's depreciable et cetera and those are all things that we probably charge for in a sense"

**Q4 · What is the capitalisation threshold?**
*Why it matters:* The de minimis safe harbour allows expensing items under $2,500 with an election on the return. Without a stated firm threshold, every tool purchase is a judgement call — and on this client it was a judgement call made 127 times.
*What was done:* Everything expensed. The election needs to be on the return for that to hold.
*The firm:* "see I would just take the default threshold here call it good generally so this is just a good question to have and to ratify on the spot if you can and like I know that why it matters already has it but you know asking it and not having that in your back pocket already umm I don't know I guess maybe it is kind of a firm decision to say we're just going to follow that for sure but it's a good question and it has the answer in it I'm not sure if this one helps you as much. Also I guess the capitalization threshold should naturally lead to what we do about depreciation especially if that's not something we've currently done and it'd be really nice if sources could exist within our agents that could help us get to that it's better to review something that's correct than have to make something and then correct it for me"

**Q5 · Business meals: book them, or refuse them?**
*Why it matters:* Business meals are 50% deductible and require substantiation — who attended and the business purpose. A bank feed carries neither. Is the firm's position to book to Meals and let the return apply the disallowance, or to refuse the deduction without a note from the client?
*What was done:* Booked to Meals. The disallowance is a return-preparation step, not a bookkeeping one.
*The firm:* "I think that Book them or refuse them is too narrow of a question to me this is more like the first question in the sense that it is how many accounts do we need to account for what the client does and how do we find that out and what do we need to know anyway maybe these are the kinds of questions that we should be aware of or the agent should be like OK these are good follow-ups to a question like this and these are similar follow-ups to the question just before because whether or not it is deductible to a certain point also largely depends on who it was with when it was et cetera if it was travel it's almost definitely deductible as long as we trust the expense"

**Q6 · Is a brewery a meal?**
*Why it matters:* Entertainment has been 100% non-deductible since the 2017 Act, and alcohol at a taproom is not a business meal.
*What was done:* Breweries and liquor stores booked as owner draws, not Meals. This is the single most common misclassification in a card feed, and the rules engine had every one of them as Meals.
*The firm:* "I'm just going to say the same thing as above like with the meals question you know entertainment slash meals they're very subjective in these natures so it makes more sense to maybe have some sort of like client rule but it also makes sense just generally speaking for the agent to ask a question that then provokes questions like OK well how many accounts may we need anyway"

**Q7 · Are groceries ever deductible?**
*Why it matters:* Supermarket spending is personal unless it is job-site provision for a crew — in which case it may be 100% deductible rather than 50%, a third answer again.
*What was done:* Booked as owner draws.
*The firm:* "the client had already told me that he could guarantee they were deductible and honestly I will believe the guy however this is a great question and I think that like the j.crew thing it may at least instead of being booked to owners drawers be flagged for attention or something"

**Q8 · Is this clothing deductible?**
*Why it matters:* Work clothing is deductible only if it is not suitable for everyday wear. Trade workwear generally qualifies; ordinary retail clothing does not, whatever card it was bought on.
*What was done:* Workwear to Uniforms, street clothing to owner draws.
*The firm:* "see I don't understand how distinguished and we talked about the j.crew thing so you know. I think knowing the client also helps not like literally knowing them but knowing that they're a contractor it's like I'm more willing to believe that they're going to shop at places that I normally wouldn't and where those things for work especially because of where they live"

**Q9 · Are card rewards income?**
*Why it matters:* The common treatment is a reduction of purchase price rather than income — rebates on spending are generally not taxable. A chart with a Credit Card Rewards income account invites the opposite answer and overstates taxable income.
*What was done:* Booked to the income account the chart provides, and flagged. The firm needs a stated position.
*The firm:* "it's interesting because the answer to this I'm pretty sure is card points are not income they're not taxed they are rebates and you get to just take those rebates however I would definitely like something to tell us for sure"

**Q10 · Is a payment to a municipality a permit, a tax, or a draw?**
*Why it matters:* A single payment to a town could be a trade permit, personal property tax on business equipment, or the owner's residential property tax. Three different answers, one line in the feed.
*What was done:* Licenses and Permits, as most likely for a trades contractor. The town's bill settles it in one look.
*The firm:* "this is a good question I'm not really sure how did you deduce something like this"

**Q11 · A convenience fee implies a payment that isn't in the books**
*Why it matters:* A card convenience fee on a tax payment appeared with no corresponding tax payment anywhere in the file. Which tax was paid, from which account, and is that account missing?
*What was done:* Fee expensed; the missing payment raised.

**Q12 · Do payments to individuals create a 1099-NEC obligation?**
*Why it matters:* Peer-to-peer payments to individuals for services can. Does the workflow track payee totals, and does anything surface the threshold before January?
*What was done:* Nothing tracks it. Raised as a product gap.
*The firm:* "this is fair I guess digging deeper and stuff makes sense and of course there are answers that could immediately make it like uh who cares which is kind of like a safe harbor thing"

**Q13 · Is airfare business travel?**
*Why it matters:* Airfare is deductible with a business purpose and not without one. Nothing in a feed carries the purpose.
*What was done:* Booked to Travel pending a stated purpose.

**Q14 · Is an inspection fee a job cost, a permit, or a rebill?**
*Why it matters:* If it is a permit condition it is Licenses and Permits; if it is rebilled to the customer it is reimbursed expenses; otherwise it is direct job cost.
*What was done:* Job cost.
*The firm:* "see I actually booked this as professional fees when I did this as an example but I understand why I have this question and it's not even something I can considered it's just I knew that he got an appraisal gun on his house and his place of business is house for storage and stuff"

**Q15 · Which policies does a business insurance premium cover?**
*Why it matters:* Business liability is deductible. Owner health or disability cover is treated differently and may belong on the personal return instead.
*What was done:* All to Insurance; the policy schedule settles it.
*The firm:* "yeah insurance questions are just fair in general and the outcome to this may be even like hey this is what you need to know in order to proceed"

### DEPRECIATION (3)

**Q16 · Where is the line between a tool and a fixed asset?**
*Why it matters:* The capitalisation threshold again, asked from the asset side. A single tool purchase in the hundreds is expensed under the safe harbour and capitalised without it, and Section 179 or bonus depreciation may apply either way.
*The firm:* "this is just a good question and an example of why a depreciation expert would be helpful no matter what and I'm not sure if depreciation has to be like as limited as a agent gets because like we've discussed the IRS has so much information in one place and could be saved or whatever and is itself authoritative when it comes to this tax stuff"

**Q17 · Can you have vehicle expense and no vehicle?**
*Why it matters:* A business running fuel, tyres and servicing all year with nothing in fixed assets and no vehicle loan is telling you the asset is owned somewhere else.

**Q18 · Does a hardware-store purchase ever become an asset?**
*Why it matters:* A year of small trade-supplier purchases will contain some that are not consumables. Only line detail separates them, and a bank feed has none.
*The firm:* "To me of course it still connects with possible depreciation questions and like having an answer to something like the first question could immediately make all other questions basically not matter because we're under a safe harbor that we just accept so it's important for the context of the business I guess to be known and I don't mean like whether they're a contractor or something so much as like well do we have this set maybe that's something that should be built into the software if that's not something we explicitly said already so I guess like the agent could even come back with suggestions in that case anyway I'm coming with up with that on the fly really at the end of the day this is just obviously a question we must ask and there are many ways to derive the answer but the expert should hopefully point them in the right direction also I guess maybe it's critical for experts to consult each other every now and then and to synthesize an answer is that what the desk's job is?"

### INVENTORY (1)

**Q19 · Does the client hold materials at year end?**
*Why it matters:* Trades materials bought for jobs are expensed as used; materials bought and held are inventory. A client with a storage unit and five figures of supplier purchases may be holding stock. If so, a physical count and an inventory adjustment are needed.
*What was done:* Everything expensed as purchased, which is correct only if nothing is on the shelf at 31 December. The client was asked.
*The firm:* "I think materials at year end isn't what we want to do here like so for bookkeeping I don't really want to keep track of an inventory and I would charge them if we had to because that's more complicated and like I I kind of base it off the tax code anyway I guess this is another safe harbor moment for me like you don't have to defer to safe harbor but if there's no reason not to I feel like you might as well in general"

### CASH (3)

**Q20 · Is an unnamed account the owner's or the business's?**
*Why it matters:* Transfers ran to and from two account numbers the books cannot identify. If they are the owner's personal accounts, the movements are contributions and draws; if they are a second business account, they are internal transfers and both sides belong on the books.
*What was done:* Left unclassified. This is one of the few questions where no answer beats a guess, because the guess changes equity.
*The firm:* "I think this is interesting I I knew this would be an issue because I've obviously reviewed these books and it's difficult without interviewing the client to know what these kinds of things are but I would also make general assumptions however these general assumptions aren't necessarily authoritative so I'm curious to know what kind of answer it actually comes back with it also makes me think we should probably not look everywhere for answers but there's going to be sometimes where you know I just assume when the client accepts A Venmo payment that it was revenue and maybe experience tells me that knowing the client tells me that or maybe some website would even suggest that that someone would trust however we don't want to trust blindly"

**Q21 · Is the payment processor's balance a cash account?**
*Why it matters:* Money arrived as instant transfers from a payment-service account. If the business controls that balance it is a cash account in its own right, with its own opening and closing balance — not merely a source of transfers.
*What was done:* Treated as a funding source, not an account. Understates cash if wrong.

**Q22 · Does the client take cash?**
*Why it matters:* Cash deposits and cash withdrawals both appeared. Cash received from customers that never reaches a bank account is invisible to a feed-driven system entirely.
*What was done:* Asked. Nothing in the software can detect this.
*The firm:* "Honestly knowing that all of the accounts on this are business accounts there's no other reason to deposit cash into a business account I'm not really sure how the agent could already know this but still like and I don't want agents to be highly highly specific it'd be cool to tell an agent like hey this is your job and then like desk is just going to be available so when it gets stuck it kind of can ask desk for the next best step maybe even iterate on itself over time possibly but that's also kind of desk's job in the sense that it saves authoritative decisions I don't know food for thought"

### LIABILITIES (4)

**Q23 · Why don't the two legs of a card payment agree?**
*Why it matters:* The bank showed 33 payments to the card; the card showed 27 receipts, and the totals differed by a four-figure sum. Candidates: payments straddling the period boundary, a second card, or payments from an account not on the books.
*What was done:* Both legs booked to the card payable, one as a non-posting contra.
*The firm:* "This is an interesting question because it's like well is this an authority question or is this like the problem with the software kind of question like the logic doing it"

**Q24 · Should each card have its own liability account?**
*Why it matters:* Payments went to three different cards. Booking them all to one payable makes the balance unreconcilable against any single statement.
*What was done:* One account, because that is the chart. It is why the payable will not tie.
*The firm:* "so it's interesting because like there's only one business card which means they identify there is other accounts that were not business it didn't know if they were business cards I guess and I count those as owner draws but like kind of the other questions in here I don't know if that's 'cause I make that assumption and something could tell us that but like I feel like this is a bad guess on its part like not only should you sort of have a good handle on the amount of revolving accounts you actually have when you're a small business I feel so I'd probably put it in a separate account if it was 2 actual separate business cards but they're not so I don't know"

**Q25 · Is a store-card payment a purchase?**
*Why it matters:* A payment to a retailer's store card carries the retailer's name and looks exactly like a purchase from that retailer. The rules engine classified one as job materials on the merchant name alone.
*What was done:* Corrected to the payable. A rule keyed on a merchant name cannot see the difference; something else has to.
*The firm:* "Yeah I don't know about connecting this one to the payable I feel like that's an aggressive thing to do but you know it's not even like a question that would have been answered Like especially because again it doesn't know if it's a business card or not and it was not a business card it's essentially an owner draw if it's money out that is"

**Q26 · Is a loan hiding in the deposits?**
*Why it matters:* Absent a loan statement, a loan advance and a customer payment look identical in a bank feed, and the repayments look like any other withdrawal.
*The firm:* "yeah this connects to the vehicle expenses there should be something telling us to get like loan statements and stuff to make sure we understand the deal"

### REVENUE (3)

**Q27 · What proves a deposit is revenue?**
*Why it matters:* A bank feed strips the payer from a cheque deposit. A year of mobile deposits with no payer name could be revenue, an owner contribution, a loan advance, or a customer deposit for work not yet done.
*What was done:* All booked as service revenue, the only plausible default for a trades contractor — and the single largest assumption in the whole close.
*The firm:* "yeah I kind of agree with this because like why else would you deposit money into an account and the simplest thing to do is to just deposit all your money into your business account and call it revenue and that's what I would advise my clients to do this also comes with just like learning I don't really know if the desk could help with this or not but it'd be cool if it could"

**Q28 · Is processor income recorded gross or net?**
*Why it matters:* A payment processor settles net of its fees, so both revenue and merchant fees are understated by the fee. The activity report grosses it up.
*What was done:* Booked net. Understates both sides of the P&L by the same amount, so the profit is right and neither line is.
*The firm:* "This is an odd question because like it's a cash balance or it's a CSV from a bank so obviously you book it net and if another amount came through that was a fee you had booked that I'm not really sure why this matters and it's also useful for an agent to tell someone something doesn't matter is if that's possible I don't know to be clear I'm not trying to say take what I'm saying here as like things the agent should be spawned with from the desk to tell other agents but like I feel like this is deductive reasoning and if an agent were an expert in accounting it would be able to make this kind of deductive reasoning"

**Q29 · Are rewards revenue at all?**
*Why it matters:* See Q9, "Are card rewards income?"

### INTEREST (1)

**Q30 · Should the rules engine ship an interest rule?**
*Why it matters:* Card purchase interest was left unmatched and would have fallen into an expense bucket or none at all. Interest belongs below the operating line. Every card client will have this.
*What was done:* Booked to Interest Expense by hand; raised as a missing standard rule.

### OWNER (5)

**Q31 · Personal spending on a business card is not "excluded" — it is a draw**
*Why it matters:* The money left the business account either way. Booking it nowhere makes the bank unreconcilable by exactly the amount of it.
*What was done:* Every personal item booked to Owner Draws. The software's own Personal decision does the wrong thing here.

**Q32 · Is unreceipted cash a draw?**
*Why it matters:* Cash withdrawn with no receipt is an owner draw under most firm policies. Is it under this firm's?
*What was done:* Left unclassified pending the policy.

**Q33 · Is streaming television ever a business subscription?**
*Why it matters:* Entertainment streaming on a business card is personal unless there is a waiting room.
*What was done:* Owner draws.

**Q34 · Is personal medical a draw or a workers-comp matter?**
*Why it matters:* Urgent care on the business card is a draw — unless it was a job-site injury, in which case the business insurance is involved.
*What was done:* Draw, and asked.

**Q35 · Is there a home office?**
*Why it matters:* A cleaning service was charged to a business with no premises but a storage unit. If a home office is claimed, that opens an allocation question nothing else in the file touches.
*What was done:* Draw.

### DOCUMENTATION (3)

**Q36 · What settles an unidentifiable cheque?**
*Why it matters:* Cheques clear a bank feed with no payee. Only the cheque image or the client's register can classify them. Should an unidentifiable cheque block a close?
*What was done:* Left unclassified.

**Q37 · What is the standard evidence for deposit income?**
*Why it matters:* The deposit detail from the bank, the invoicing system, or a client-supplied list — the firm should pick one rather than deciding per client.

**Q38 · Should the marketplace order history be a standard document request?**
*Why it matters:* A year of online-marketplace purchases with no line detail could be office supplies, tools, or job materials, and the classification changes both gross profit and the capitalisation question. The order history resolves every one of them and nobody asks for it.

### COMPLETENESS (1)

**Q39 · Payments to a card we hold no statements for**
*Why it matters:* Payments went to a card that was not a registered source account. We have no statement and no transactions for it, so every charge on that card is missing from the books — and nothing in the close notices. Should a period refuse to close while the books can see payments to an account they hold no data for?
*What was done:* Payments booked to the payable; raised as the most serious completeness gap in the file.

### RECONCILIATION (3)

**Q40 · Whose job is pairing a transfer?**
*Why it matters:* Payments between two accounts both on the books appeared as two unmatched rows in the review queue. If the matcher cannot pair them, should the unpaired remainder block the close rather than sit in a queue?

**Q41 · A statement cycle that does not end at year end**
*Why it matters:* The card's cycle closed on the 2nd of the month. No statement in existence shows the balance at 31 December. The opening balance for a calendar-year book cannot be read off a statement at all — it has to be derived from the bracketing pair, and if the pre-year data is not there it cannot be derived either.
*What was done:* The checking opening was proven from its bracket and posted. The card opening was derived, then refused by the engine — correctly. The February statement was requested.

**Q42 · Transaction date or post date?**
*Why it matters:* The book runs on transaction date; the card statement closes on post date. The two disagree at every period boundary by whatever is in transit. On this client that was a specific, itemisable set of late-December charges — the book ahead of the statement, which is the only direction that is ever allowed.

### PAYROLL (1)

**Q43 · Does a payment to a payroll processor tell you the wage cost?**
*Why it matters:* No. A payroll sweep is one cash figure covering gross wages, employer taxes and the processor's fee — three accounts, none of which is the amount that left the bank. Net pay is a cash figure, not an account. Without the payroll register the split cannot be made, and a single expense line for the sweep is wrong in three directions at once.
*What was done:* Not triggered on this client, who has no payroll. Recorded because getting it wrong was this session's own mistake on an earlier pass.
