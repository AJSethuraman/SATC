# Sources — what this desk is allowed to rely on

Each entry records **what the source is**, how binding it is, how it may be
reached, and what may be copied here from it. `Checked` is the date a person last
confirmed the entry against the source; a citation with no date is a claim about
the present that nobody re-examines.

**Nothing here is a default.** A source missing any field is a parse error rather
than a guess, because a field that was never read and a field that was empty look
identical downstream.

## S3 · Treasury Regulation § 1.263(a)-2 — Amounts paid to acquire or produce tangible property

**Tier:** primary · **Access:** public_fetch · **May store:** full_text · **Checked:** 2026-09-07

**Citation prefix:** 26 CFR 1.263(a)-2

**Url:** https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.263(a)-2

**Why:** Found by the searcher against a real refusal. This desk holds S1, which
decides whether money spent on property the taxpayer ALREADY OWNS is an
improvement — a betterment, a restoration, an adaptation. Nothing in it decides a
PURCHASE, so the close's own question, *where is the line between a tool and a
fixed asset*, had nowhere to go. § 1.263(a)-2(d)(1) is the rule that draws it,
and it names as its own exceptions § 1.162-3 and § 1.263(a)-1(f) — the two
escapes this desk and the capitalisation desk already hold.

The firm declared it on the eighth docket — **"Declare it"**, 7 September 2026.
Not a new publisher: this desk already reads eCFR for S1 and S2, which is what
the search report said it was asking, having been fixed to tell the two apart.

A work of the United States Government, so 17 U.S.C. § 105 places it in the
public domain and it is storable in full. Only the two paragraphs the searcher
proposed are stored, as with S2: admitting a section is not importing it.
