# Sources — what this desk is allowed to rely on

Each entry records **what the source is**, how binding it is, how it may be
reached, and what may be copied here from it. `Checked` is the date a person last
confirmed the entry against the source; a citation with no date is a claim about
the present that nobody re-examines.

**Nothing here is a default.** A source missing any field is a parse error rather
than a guess, because a field that was never read and a field that was empty look
identical downstream. Where a source permits storing, `Why` carries the term that
was read to establish it — not a summary of it.

**The two halves of this desk are not equally well founded, and that is the
finding.** The information-return half rests on the Code and on Treasury
regulations — primary authority, which settles the question. The rewards half has
NO primary authority at all: the Code and the regulations define gross income and
stop. Every statement that a rebate on spending is not income is a revenue
ruling, an announcement, a publication or a private letter ruling — and the only
one that says it about a CREDIT CARD may not, by its own terms, be cited as
precedent. So every rewards problem on this desk escalates, by construction, and
that is the desk reporting the state of the authority rather than a defect.

## S1 · Treasury Regulation § 1.61-1 — Gross income

**Tier:** primary · **Access:** public_fetch · **May store:** full_text · **Checked:** 2026-09-05

**Citation prefix:** 26 CFR 1.61-1

**Url:** https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-09-03/title-26.xml?part=1&section=1.61-1

**Why:** A work of the United States Government, so 17 U.S.C. § 105 places it in the public domain and it is storable in full. IT IS THE BASELINE THE REBATE ANSWER IS AN EXCEPTION TO, and it is here for that reason and no other: gross income is "all income from whatever source derived, unless excluded by law", so anything received has to be argued out of income rather than into it. This regulation says nothing about rebates, discounts or card rewards — which is itself the answer to "is this settled in the regulations". It is not.

**Url:** https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-09-03/title-26.xml?part=1&section=1.6041-1

**Why:** A work of the United States Government, so 17 U.S.C. § 105 places it in the public domain and it is storable in full. **ITS DOLLAR FIGURE IS OUT OF DATE AND ITS RULES ARE NOT.** The section is still titled "payments of $600 or more" and paragraph (a)(1)(i) still says $600, in the eCFR text as of 2026-09-03 — three times. ## S6 · Treasury Regulation § 1.6050W-1 — Payment card and third party network transactions

**Tier:** primary · **Access:** public_fetch · **May store:** full_text · **Checked:** 2026-09-05

**Citation prefix:** 26 CFR 1.6050W-1

**Url:** https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-09-03/title-26.xml?part=1&section=1.6050W-1

**Why:** A work of the United States Government, so 17 U.S.C. § 105 places it in the public domain and it is storable in full. It is here for the definition, not for the threshold: whether a payment through an app is a "third party network transaction" at all turns on paragraph (c)(3), which requires an arrangement under which a substantial number of PROVIDERS OF GOODS OR SERVICES have accounts and have agreed to settle for the provision of those goods or services. A transfer between two people that is not settled under such an arrangement is not within this section, and the relief in § 1.6041-1(a)(1)(iv) does not reach it.

## S8 · IRS Announcement 2002-18, 2002-10 I.R.B. 621

**Why:** A work of the United States Government, so 17 U.S.C. § 105 places it in the public domain and it is storable in full. It is the only published IRS statement about promotional benefits earned by spending, and it does NOT hold that they are untaxed. ## S10 · IRS Publication 334 (2025) — Tax Guide for Small Business

**Tier:** secondary · **Access:** public_fetch · **May store:** full_text · **Checked:** 2026-09-05

**Citation prefix:** IRS Pub. 334

**Url:** https://www.irs.gov/publications/p334

**Why:** A work of the United States Government, so 17 U.S.C. § 105 places it in the public domain and it is storable in full. Headed "Publication 334 (2025)", "For use in preparing 2025 Returns". It is here because it is the only source reached that says what a BUSINESS does with a discount it receives rather than what an individual does — and because it cuts both ways. A trade discount reduces the recorded cost and is "not shown separately as an item in gross income"; a cash discount MAY be credited to a discount account, and if it is, "you must include this credit balance in your business income at the end of the tax year". Both are about purchases of merchandise, not about card rewards; they are stored so that the difference is visible rather than assumed away.
