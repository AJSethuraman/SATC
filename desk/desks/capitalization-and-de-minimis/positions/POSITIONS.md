# Positions — what the firm does where the rules permit a choice

**An agent proposes here. It never writes.** The pull request is the firm's yes,
and a position that entered any other way is one they will disown the moment it
is read back at them.

Each entry carries the firm's **own words**, the authority it rests on, and the
date. Where a source cannot be read by a desk at all — a licence forbidding the
content reaching a model — a position here is the desk's entire knowledge of it,
and the citation is how a reader gets to the text themselves.

**All three entries below are PROPOSALS.** None carries a `Ratified` field, so
`Desk.position()` will not serve any of them and `authority_for()` will not see
them. Until the firm merges them, this desk escalates on every question that
turns on the dollar figure — which is the correct behaviour and not a gap.

**Why there are positions here at all, when `fixed-assets` has none.** That desk
answers under federal authority alone and the rules leave it nothing to decide.
This one does not: § 1.263(a)-1(f) is an **election**, so making it is a choice;
the ceiling is a ceiling and the book policy underneath it is a choice; and the
current figure is not in the regulation at all. Three real choices, and the firm
has already spoken on all three.

---

## POS1 · The firm makes the de minimis safe harbour election, every year, on every return

**Citation:** 26 CFR 1.263(a)-1(f)(5) · **Recorded:** 2026-09-05

**Position:** elect the de minimis safe harbor; attach the election statement to the timely filed original return

**Why:** The election is the choice the regulation leaves open. It is annual, it
is made by attaching a statement, and it cannot be made on an amended return
without the Commissioner's consent — so a year where nobody decided is a year
where the answer is no, permanently. Without it, every small purchase is
capitalisable on its own facts.

The firm, on Q4, 5 September 2026: *"see I would just take the default threshold
here call it good generally so this is just a good question to have and to ratify
on the spot if you can and like I know that why it matters already has it but you
know asking it and not having that in your back pocket already umm I don't know I
guess maybe it is kind of a firm decision to say we're just going to follow that
for sure but it's a good question and it has the answer in it"*

And on Q19, the same instinct stated as a general rule: *"this is another safe
harbor moment for me like you don't have to defer to safe harbor but if there's
no reason not to I feel like you might as well in general"*

**What this desk cannot do about it.** The election is a filing act on the
return. This desk can say it is required and say when; it cannot see whether the
statement was attached. Whatever checks that lives in the return-preparation
software, not here — and if nothing checks it today, that is a finding, not a
position.

## POS2 · The firm's capitalisation threshold is the de minimis ceiling — $2,500 per invoice or per item, no financial statement

**Citation:** IRS Tangible Property Final Regulations, "What is the de minimis safe harbor election?" · **Recorded:** 2026-09-05

**Position:** $2,500 per invoice or per item; $5,000 where the client has an applicable financial statement

**Why:** Q4 asked what the firm's capitalisation threshold is, and on the close
that produced it the judgement was made 127 times without one. The firm, 5
September 2026: *"see I would just take the default threshold here call it good
generally"* — and, on Q18: *"having an answer to something like the first
question could immediately make all other questions basically not matter because
we're under a safe harbor that we just accept"*.

**Read the citation, and read what it is not.** This rests on an IRS web page,
which is SECONDARY — the Service explaining itself, not authority a client may
rely on. The regulation's own text at § 1.263(a)-1(f)(1)(ii)(D) still says $500
and defers the current figure to "published guidance in the Federal Register or
in the Internal Revenue Bulletin". That guidance is **Notice 2015-82**, which the
page cites and links and which **was not fetched by this session** — so the desk
holds the IRS's description of the notice and not the notice. Before ratifying,
somebody should read Notice 2015-82 itself and either add it as a primary source
or record here that the description is what the position rests on.

**And $2,500 is a ceiling, not the number.** § 1.263(a)-1(f)(1)(ii)(B) requires
the client to have a book policy at the beginning of the year; the safe harbour
protects amounts under whichever is lower, that policy or the ceiling. Setting
the firm's default at the ceiling means the client's own policy has to say
$2,500 too, which is POS3.

## POS3 · The client's book expensing policy is set before the year opens, not at close

**Citation:** 26 CFR 1.263(a)-1(f)(1)(ii)(B) · **Recorded:** 2026-09-05

**Position:** record the client's expensing policy and its effective date at engagement; a policy first written during the close does not support the safe harbour for that year

**Why:** The regulation conditions the safe harbour on procedures the taxpayer
has *"at the beginning of the taxable year"*. A threshold agreed in March about
last year is not that. This is the one part of Q4 that cannot be fixed at close
and is therefore the part that has to be built into the intake.

The firm, on Q18, 5 September 2026: *"so it's important for the context of the
business I guess to be known and I don't mean like whether they're a contractor
or something so much as like well do we have this set maybe that's something that
should be built into the software if that's not something we explicitly said
already so I guess like the agent could even come back with suggestions in that
case"*

**One correction the firm should see before ratifying.** For a client with no
applicable financial statement the procedure does **not** have to be in writing.
The IRS says so under "If you don't have an AFS, are you required to have a
written accounting procedure at the beginning of your taxable year?": *"you are
not required to have written accounting procedures; however, you must expense
amounts on your books and records for the taxable year in accordance with a
consistent accounting procedure or policy existing at the beginning of the
taxable year. If you have AFS, you must have the accounting procedures in
writing."* Writing it down anyway is cheap and is what proves consistency, so
this position asks for it — but it asks for it as practice, not as law.
