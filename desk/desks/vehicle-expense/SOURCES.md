# Sources — what this desk is allowed to rely on

Each entry records **what the source is**, how binding it is, how it may be
reached, and what may be copied here from it. `Checked` is the date a person last
confirmed the entry against the source; a citation with no date is a claim about
the present that nobody re-examines.

**Nothing here is a default.** A source missing any field is a parse error rather
than a guess, because a field that was never read and a field that was empty look
identical downstream. Where a source permits storing, `Why` carries the term that
was read to establish it — not a summary of it.

**One thing this desk could not reach, named.** The standard mileage rate is not
set by any of these. It is fixed for each year by a Revenue Procedure or Notice,
and no such pronouncement was fetched while this desk was built. The only rate
stored anywhere here is the one Pub. 463 (2025) prints for **2025**, quoted from
the publication with its year attached. **No rate for any other year exists in
this record, and none may be written from memory.**

## S1 · Treasury Regulation § 1.280F-6 — Special rules and definitions (listed property, business use percentage)

**Tier:** primary · **Access:** public_fetch · **May store:** full_text · **Checked:** 2026-09-05

**Citation prefix:** 26 CFR 1.280F-6

**Url:** https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.280F-6

**Why:** A work of the United States Government, so 17 U.S.C. § 105 places it in the public domain and it is storable in full. IT IS HERE BECAUSE IT ANSWERS "WHOSE VEHICLE IS IT". A passenger automobile is listed property under (b)(1)(i); (d)(1) defines the business use percentage; (e)(2) says a vehicle's use is allocated on the basis of mileage and not on anything else; (d)(2)(ii) and (d)(3)(iv) settle what happens when the person driving is the owner, a relative, or an employee — which is the case the firm called "probably most likely". It binds.

## S2 · Treasury Regulation § 1.62-2 — Reimbursements and other expense allowance arrangements

**Tier:** primary · **Access:** public_fetch · **May store:** full_text · **Checked:** 2026-09-05

**Citation prefix:** 26 CFR 1.62-2

**Url:** https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.62-2

**Why:** A work of the United States Government, so 17 U.S.C. § 105 places it in the public domain and it is storable in full. THIS IS THE ACCOUNTABLE PLAN. Where a vehicle belongs to the owner personally and the business pays for its running, the arrangement is either an accountable plan under (c)(2) — the payments are not wages, not on the W-2, not subject to employment taxes — or a nonaccountable plan under (c)(3), and everything paid is wages. Three requirements decide it: business connection (d), substantiation (e), and returning the excess (f). It binds.

## S3 · Treasury Regulation § 1.274-5T — Substantiation requirements (temporary)

**Tier:** primary · **Access:** public_fetch · **May store:** full_text · **Checked:** 2026-09-05

**Citation prefix:** 26 CFR 1.274-5T

**Url:** https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.274-5T

**Why:** A work of the United States Government, so 17 U.S.C. § 105 places it in the public domain and it is storable in full. WHY THE TEMPORARY REGULATION AND NOT § 1.274-5. The permanent section is largely "[Reserved]. For further guidance, see § 1.274-5T" — it says so in its own text at (a)-(b), (c)(1), (c)(2)(i), (d)-(e) — so the operative substantiation rules for listed property are in the temporary one. (b)(6) lists the elements § 274(d) demands of any listed property; (c)(2) is what an adequate record is; (c)(3)(ii)(A) is the sampling rule that lets part of a year stand for the whole. It binds.

## S4 · IRS Publication 463 (2025) — Travel, Gift, and Car Expenses

**Tier:** secondary · **Access:** public_fetch · **May store:** full_text · **Checked:** 2026-09-05

**Citation prefix:** IRS Pub. 463 (2025)

**Url:** https://www.irs.gov/publications/p463

**Why:** A work of the United States Government, so 17 U.S.C. § 105 places it in the public domain and it is storable in full. SECONDARY, AND THE DESK'S ONLY ESCALATION ROUTE. An IRS publication is the Service's own plain-language explanation, not authority a taxpayer may rely on — so an answer resting on it alone is a choice the rules leave open, and the engine refuses it to the firm rather than settling it. It is stored because it is the only place reached that states the mileage-versus-actual election, the rule against switching back, what the actual-expense method itself comprises, and how a lease is treated. The revision was read off the page, which titles itself "Publication 463 (2025), Travel, Gift, and Car Expenses" and "For use in preparing 2025 Returns"; it was not assumed.
