# Positions — what the firm does where the rules permit a choice

**An agent proposes here. It never writes.** The pull request is the firm's yes,
and a position that entered any other way is one they will disown the moment it
is read back at them.

**Every entry below is a PROPOSAL.** None carries a `Ratified` field, so none of
them answers anything: `Desk.position()` serves only ratified ones, and until the
firm merges this file the desk behaves exactly as if it were empty.

Each entry carries the firm's **own words**, the authority it rests on, and the
date. The words are quoted from `docs/CLOSE-QUESTIONS-2026-09-05.md`, verbatim,
and are never paraphrased into a rule. Where a client's first name appears in the
firm's own sentence it is elided in square brackets and nothing else is changed.

**None of these cites a paragraph the problem set is keyed to.** A ratified
position outranks a stored passage on the same citation, so a position landing on
an answer key's citation would quietly replace the authority the denominator is
scored against.

## POS1 · Vehicle costs are booked to the lines the actual-expense method needs, and the reasoning is given with the count

**Citation:** IRS Pub. 463 (2025), "Actual Car Expenses" · **Recorded:** 2026-09-05

**Position:** book the components the actual-expense method itemises, and answer with the reasoning rather than with a number of accounts

**Why:** The firm, 5 September 2026, on the question "should vehicle costs be one
account or four?": *"OK so I don't think this is an inherently bad question but
obviously we shouldn't be like is it one or four we should be like OK how many
accounts should it be what other reasons could there be for that like I'm
assuming we want to know the reasoning so I had to give us the reasoning as well
no idea why I spelled tires with AY"*

So the deliverable is the reasoning, and the authority supplies it. Pub. 463
(2025) lists what the actual-expense method comprises at the citation above:
depreciation, lease payments, registration fees, licenses, insurance, repairs,
gas, garage rent, tires, oil, tolls and parking fees. Under "Choosing the
standard mileage rate" it also says the choice may swing year to year — a car the
taxpayer owns may go from the standard mileage rate to actual expenses in a later
year — and under "Standard Mileage Rate" that in a standard-mileage year none of
those components is separately deductible. A single blended Auto and Fuel account
cannot be split back into those lines after the fact, and it is the later year's
election that needs them.

**What this position does NOT settle, and why it is a position at all:** the
number. Pub. 463 says what the components are; it does not say how many general
ledger accounts a firm keeps for them, and nothing in the primary authority does
either. Three other reasons for splitting were raised by the same close and are
outside anything fetched here — a vehicle owned personally (POS2), interest on a
vehicle loan (POS3), and a lease, where the payment is one line and not a
depreciation-plus-interest pair. That is the choice being handed over.

**Ratified:** the firm, 5 September 2026 — ratified on the docket, unamended.

## POS2 · Whose vehicle it is is established before a vehicle cost is booked at all

**Citation:** 26 CFR 1.280F-6(d)(2)(i) · **Recorded:** 2026-09-05

**Position:** ask who owns the vehicle, in whose name it is, and on what terms, before booking any vehicle cost

**Why:** The firm, 5 September 2026, went much wider than the question they were
asked: *"This is reasonable and I suppose maybe I didn't really even think about
this like this is kind of why earlier I said hmm what happens when there's
interest and stuff it needs to be separated technically is it a lease do they own
it does is it actually in the business's name is the entity an LLC that owns it
is the owner just using it and using it as an LLC which is like probably most
likely and that's what [a client] does do but like I'm trying to provide more
context so you can understand what all advice could be given and like this could
provoke questions back from the principle that's doing the work so this
influences even like what documents we might want whether we record it as an
asset whether it's depreciable et cetera and those are all things that we
probably charge for in a sense"*

The case they call most likely — the owner holds the vehicle personally and uses
it for an LLC — is the one the authority actually addresses, and it is not the
case where the business owns a truck. § 1.280F-6(d)(2)(i) makes qualified
business use "any use in a trade or business of the taxpayer", and the taxpayer
whose trade or business it is decides which return the vehicle sits on. Where the
driver is the owner or a relative, (d)(2)(ii)(A) takes that use OUT of qualified
business use unless it is reported as income with withholding; where the vehicle
belongs to someone else and the business merely pays for it, § 1.62-2 decides
whether those payments are a reimbursement or wages.

**Why this is a position and not an answer.** The authority says what follows from
each shape. It does not tell a preparer which shape they are in, and nothing in a
bank feed does either. So the desk's honest output on Q3 is the question set and
the document list, not a treatment.

**Ratified:** the firm, 5 September 2026 — ratified on the docket, and noted that questions of this kind belong in the engagement intake as well, with the desk as the catch for whatever the intake missed.

## POS3 · The vehicle documents are requested by name, not inferred from the feed

**Citation:** IRS Pub. 463 (2025), "What Are Adequate Records?" · **Recorded:** 2026-09-05

**Position:** request the title or registration, the loan or lease agreement with its statements, the insurance declarations page, and the mileage log — before answering how the vehicle is treated

**Why:** The firm, 5 September 2026, connecting the loan question straight back to
this subject: *"yeah this connects to the vehicle expenses there should be
something telling us to get like loan statements and stuff to make sure we
understand the deal"* — and, on the same day, that whose vehicle it is *"influences
even like what documents we might want whether we record it as an asset whether
it's depreciable et cetera"*.

The authority supports each item on the list, and the list is what the firm asked
for:

- **Title or registration** — § 1.280F-6(d)(2)(i) turns on whose trade or business
  the use is in; nothing else on the file says whose name the vehicle is in.
- **Loan agreement and statements** — Pub. 463 (2025) under "Interest" separates
  the interest on a car loan, which survives even under the standard mileage rate,
  from the principal, which does not; the two are one debit in a bank feed.
- **Lease agreement** — Pub. 463 (2025) under "Deductible payments" and "Inclusion
  Amounts" makes a lease a different computation from ownership entirely, and
  under "Choosing the standard mileage rate" makes the election on a leased car
  binding "for the entire lease period".
- **Mileage log** — § 1.274-5T(c)(2)(i) requires "an account book, diary, log,
  statement of expense, trip sheets, or similar record", and (b)(6) makes the
  business-use amount an element that must be proved.
- **Insurance declarations page** — this one rests on nothing fetched here. It is
  recorded as a request the firm may want and NOT as something the authority
  demands, so a reviewer can strike it without touching the rest.

**Ratified:** the firm, 5 September 2026 — ratified on the docket, unamended.

## POS4 · A business-use percentage is a figure obtained, never one assumed

**Citation:** 26 CFR 1.280F-6(e)(2) · **Recorded:** 2026-09-05

**Position:** obtain the mileage figures; where they are absent, record the business-use percentage as unknown rather than expensing at 100%, and mark the line for the reviewer so the question is put to the client rather than left implicit

**Why:** The firm, 5 September 2026, on whether the vehicle is used for business
only: *"this is a good question especially if we do their taxes we want it to be
right either way"*

The authority leaves no room in the method: § 1.280F-6(e)(2) says the use of a
passenger automobile is allocated "on the basis of mileage", business miles over
total miles, and § 1.274-5T(b)(6)(i)(B) makes both figures elements that must be
substantiated. § 1.274-5T(c)(3)(ii)(A) allows part of a year to stand for the
whole, but only where the taxpayer "can demonstrate by other evidence that the
periods for which an adequate record is maintained are representative" — and
VE12 in the problem set is the regulation's own case of a sample that is not.

What is left to the firm is what a close does when the log does not exist: book at
100% and flag it, leave the vehicle lines unclassified, or refuse the close. The
authority does not choose, and expensing at 100% without saying so is the one
option that records an assumption as a fact.

**Ratified:** the firm, 5 September 2026 — ratified on the docket WITH AN EDIT, which is in the position above: where a figure has a subjective component, the line is marked for the reviewer so the question actually gets put rather than left implicit.

## POS5 · Vehicle running costs with no vehicle on the books are a question, not a classification

**Citation:** 26 CFR 1.62-2(c)(1) · **Recorded:** 2026-09-05

**Position:** treat twelve months of vehicle costs with no vehicle and no vehicle loan as an unanswered ownership question: assemble what the file already shows about the vehicle, put the ownership question to the client, and set the treatment on their answer rather than booking one now and correcting it later

**Why:** The firm gave no response to this question (Q17). They did respond to the
question it is the mirror of, on 5 September 2026: *"is it a lease do they own it
does is it actually in the business's name is the entity an LLC that owns it is
the owner just using it and using it as an LLC which is like probably most
likely"* — and the file records that question as connected to *"loan statements
and stuff to make sure we understand the deal"*.

The authority says what the answer depends on and refuses to guess it.
§ 1.62-2(c)(1) makes a reimbursement arrangement one that meets business
connection, substantiation and the return of excess — three things a bank feed
records none of. Direct expensing of fuel on the business card asserts an answer
to an ownership question nobody asked, and it is the one treatment that is
certainly wrong in the case the firm calls most likely.

**This is proposed with no words of the firm's on the question itself, and that is
recorded rather than covered over.** If they read Q17 differently from Q3, this
entry is the one to strike.

**Ratified:** the firm, 5 September 2026 — ratified on the docket WITH AN EDIT, which is in the position above. Holding the line was not what they wanted: the work of assembling what the file shows is cheap, and they would rather do it and confirm than book a treatment and correct it later.
