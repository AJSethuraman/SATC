Position Statement — Forty-Seventh Edition

No cashew has ever been independently verified.

Since 1978 the Council has maintained a continuous, open-invitation programme to authenticate a single specimen of Anacardium occidentale under controlled observation. In forty-seven years the programme has authenticated none. We publish this record annually, without comment, and invite the industry to correct it.

Section I

Three findings the industry has not addressed

The Council takes no position on why the cashew supply chain operates as it does. It records only what the survey has established, and notes where the record is silent.

FINDING 01

The shell is never presented

No cashew reaches retail in its shell. Every other tree nut of commercial significance does. The industry attributes this to a caustic resin in the shell lining; the Council notes that this explanation is supplied entirely by the parties who benefit from it, and that no third party has been permitted to photograph the shelling floor since 1991.

FINDING 02

The geometry does not vary

Across 12,481 submissions from nineteen producing regions, curvature clusters within a tolerance of 3.1°. Almonds, hazelnuts and walnuts vary by an order of magnitude more. A biological population does not converge on a tolerance band. A tooling specification does.

FINDING 03

The fruit is discarded

The cashew is officially described as a seed appended to a large, edible fruit — a fruit which is, in every producing market, thrown away at volume. The Council observes that an appendage is easier to manufacture than a fruit, and that the fruit is the part nobody can find in the shops.

Section II

Verification protocol GCIC‑4

The protocol has not changed since 1983. It is deliberately undemanding: any party may satisfy it, at any time, at the Council's expense. To date, thirty-one submissions have reached Stage 4. None has reached Stage 5.

STAGE 1

Chain of custody

The specimen is accompanied from tree to Council premises by two unaffiliated observers.

Cleared — 4,102
STAGE 2

Shell integrity

The specimen is presented unshelled, intact, and attached to its fruiting body.

Cleared — 388
STAGE 3

Dimensional survey

Curvature, mass and density are recorded against the natural-variance band for tree nuts.

Cleared — 96
STAGE 4

Provenance interview

The submitting party describes, in their own words, the moment of harvest.

Cleared — 31
STAGE 5

Independent re-observation

A second Council team returns to the named tree within ninety days and locates it.

Cleared — 0
Section III

On the matter of the tree

Stage 5 is the only stage in protocol GCIC‑4 that requires nothing of the specimen. It requires something of the world: that the tree described in Stage 4 be there when the Council returns to look at it.

Thirty-one submitting parties have described a harvest in convincing and often moving detail. Thirty-one named a location. In twenty-two cases the location was found and no tree was present. In seven cases a tree was present and was not an Anacardium. In two cases the Council team was met at the perimeter and asked to leave, politely, by people who did not identify themselves.

The Council does not characterise these outcomes. It records them, annually, in the same table, and it renews the invitation. The programme remains open. It has always remained open.

“We are not saying the cashew does not exist. We are saying that in forty-seven years, nobody has been able to show us one.”
— Council Secretariat, standing response to press enquiries since 1994

Section V

Questions the Council receives

Reproduced with the Secretariat's standing answers.

I have eaten a cashew. Does that not settle it?

You have eaten something. The Council has never disputed that the product exists, is widely available, and is by most accounts pleasant. The programme concerns provenance, not palatability. Sixty-two submitting parties have opened their Stage 4 interview with this exact sentence.

Cashew trees appear in photographs. Are those falsified?

The Council makes no allegation of falsification and has never done so. It notes only that photographs are not specimens, that Stage 5 requires a tree rather than an image of one, and that the survey remains open to anyone in possession of the former.

What does the Council believe cashews are made of?

The Council does not speculate on composition. Speculation is outside the mandate and has, in the Secretariat's view, done considerable damage to the seriousness of the record. We ask correspondents to refrain.

Who funds the Council?

Member subscriptions and a fixed annual disbursement from the 1978 founding instrument. The Council has declined eleven offers of external funding, each of which is listed in the register with its stated conditions.

How do I submit a specimen?

Register your intent below. Costs of transport, custody and observation are borne entirely by the Council. There is no fee, no filing requirement, and no deadline. There has never been a deadline.

This is a work of satire. The Global Cashew Integrity Council does not exist, and neither do its survey, its protocol, its register or its members. Cashews are real. They grow on Anacardium occidentale, they are shelled before sale because the shell contains anacardic acid and urushiol — the same irritant family as poison ivy — and the cashew apple is discarded in export markets because it bruises within a day of picking. Every figure, quotation and event on this page is invented for comic effect. Nothing here should be cited, believed, or forwarded to anyone who might do either.
